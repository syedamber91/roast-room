# Principles: SOIC Analyst

40 decision rules from the SOIC investing course for Indian listed equities, most-used first (entry 1 comes first on purpose, because it governs how every other number is used). Built from 528 course sources dated 2026-07-14 to 2026-08-02.

Contents: 1 how to use the course's numbers. 2 to 8 screens, gates and entry and exit systems. 9 to 17 valuation and cycles. 18 to 25 business quality and growth. 26 to 33 flags, exits and special cases. 34 to 40 behaviour, research method and sector gates.

How to read an entry. "Sources" counts the distinct sources that state or apply the rule. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Status tags: RULE = stated as a rule, caution level or screen setting; EXAMPLE = a worked example or one company's snapshot; ASSUMPTION = a "let's assume" figure; PERISHABLE = a dated company or market level. Dates are note dates: S001, S002, S228, S254, S298, S305 are 2026-07-28; S005 to S011 and S506 to S528 (except S507) are 2026-07-14; Level 2 to 5, Crash Course and Market Signals notes (S013 to S123, S426 to S505, S507) are 2026-07-27; Level 6 sector notes (S125 to S422, unless listed above) are 2026-07-26. Every entry is the course's rule with its conditions, not advice from this seat. Where sources give different numbers for the same thing, the entry says so and `limits.md` section 6 shows both.

1. **The mechanism lasts; every number is a dated calibration example, so recompute from live data before using one.** Status: RULE (the course's operating principle). The framework record separates "mechanisms, decision procedures, question sets" (S001), which survive time, from "every specific number (margins, prices, DCF values, order books, growth rates)" (S001), which are lecture-date snapshots. A stored figure may be used only as the mechanism it illustrates or as a prior to re-check; for price, EPS and growth it says "recompute, never reuse a stored DCF value" (S001). Core lessons are refreshed slowly: "updating the classes of the course once in every three years" (S013, HOW 00:04:15). Worked valuations are hints, not answers: "I am just giving you hints of valuations so that you can do it yourself independently" (S126, WATCH 00:55:57). Agrochemical views date from about FY22 (S005). Sources: 6 (S001, S005, S006, S007, S013, S126). Confidence: high.

2. **Screen first on accelerating growth: quarterly sales growth above 15% and PAT growth above 20% year on year, then ask what is new.** Status: RULE (a screen setting, not a buy signal). The tracker flags companies with "more than 15% sales growth and more than 20% profit-after-tax (PAT) growth" (S513). The growth must be explained by something new: "new is a very powerful word when it comes to filtration" (S001, MASTEC 00:07:19). A companion P/E filter below 50, or tighter below 40, is a tunable EXAMPLE (S005, S006, S515); no reason is given for 50 against 40 (S525). A stricter variant asks for operating margin up 10% on the prior quarter for 3 or 4 quarters in a row (S119, HOWA 00:40:12). Sources: 11 (S001, S002, S005, S006, S119, S454, S506, S513, S515, S522, S525). Confidence: high.

3. **Run a cash-conversion gate before trusting any bullish framework: operating cash flow should be a stable share of operating profit.** Status: RULE stated as an ideal. For CFO to EBITDA: "should ideally be around 70% for consumer-facing B2C businesses and 60% for B2B businesses" (S001, MODULB 01:58:04). "nearly 70% of your EBITDA should get converted into cash flow from operations" (S050, MODULA 00:20:23). The CFO to PAT companion check is given three different ways (above 80 to 90% in S001 and S470; above 70% B2C and 60% B2B in S045; above 90 to 95% in S494), shown side by side in `limits.md` section 6. The framework record calls this gate veto-class: a failed gate caps the verdict and never creates a bullish signal. It does not apply to banks and NBFCs, where the cash flow statement is "redundant and void" (S036, MODULB 01:54:03). B2G receivables and strategic inventory are legitimate exceptions, and one soft quarter is a flag to investigate, not a veto. Sources: 8 (S001, S002, S036, S045, S050, S055, S470, S494). Confidence: high.

4. **Exit by system, not by mood: sell when the price breaks the 30-weekly EMA, relative strength turns negative and the volatility stop turns negative, all together.** Status: RULE with calibration that differs by lecture. The standard system is "a timeframe length of 10 and a multiplier of 2 or 2.5" for the V-Stop and an RS length of 26 or 52 weeks (S114, FRAMEC 01:55:28; FRAMEC 01:54:57). The cost is stated up front: waiting for all three signals gives up "10% to 25% from the top" (S001, FRAMEC 01:47:39). Where the 30-weekly line breaks on volume the course says "that's a risk you must understand" (S329, HOTEL 01:59:45). For compounders it uses a monthly V-Stop: "we only use technicals for exit" (S107, ADDITA 00:11:44). The system is built for satellite holdings; see `limits.md` section 6 for the conflict over core holdings. Sources: 8 (S001, S093, S095, S107, S114, S122, S329, S447). Confidence: high.

5. **Time an entry on a Stage 2 breakout backed by an earnings surprise, with weekly ADX above 20 and weekly RSI above 50.** Status: RULE (screen settings). "screen for fresh entries with ADX crossing above 20 and weekly RSI crossing 50" (S001, HOWB 00:01:55). Price follows earnings: a stock price is a "slave of earnings growth" (S119, HOWC 00:31:31), and Stage 2 starts with a positive earnings surprise (S001, HOWD 00:05:51). Another lecture accepts RSI above 45 or 50 together with a green V-Stop (S095, TECHNE 00:05:07). RSI above 50 and positive relative strength together are "very very powerful signal" (S104, ADDITB 00:27:34). Sources: 8 (S001, S002, S095, S097, S104, S119, S122, S474). Confidence: high.

6. **Grade governance flags, then investigate; do not treat every flag as proof of fraud.** Status: RULE. Severity runs from a delay in provident fund deposits, "an amber flag rather than a severe red flag" (S068, MODULJ 00:21:11), to a qualified audit opinion, a "yellow flag" (S470, SOICC2 00:29:50), to fully pledged promoter shares, a "huge red flag" (S470, SOICC2 00:45:02). Rising pledge alongside rising receivables and debt is one of the "automatic red flags" (S001, SOICC2 00:48:09). Serial exits of the CFO, secretary and independent directors, auditors leaning on other auditors, or "entire board resigned" (S111, FRAMEC 01:10:12) call for skepticism, not proof. The course says not to average down on a falling stock during such events (S052). Sources: 8 (S001, S052, S054, S066, S068, S111, S116, S470). Confidence: high.

7. **Write the risks first, then judge Theme, Value, Growth and Promoter; the promoter leg is a veto.** Status: RULE (framework order). The four pillars are "theme, value, growth and promoter" (S298, SPOTT 00:04:38). In turnarounds "you assess downside risk first precisely because the turnaround signal itself is often ambiguous or hidden beneath distorted reported earnings" (S506). Growth is checked against a "NEW" list: capex, run-rate, margin economics, deleveraging, management or owners, regulation. Walk away "even if TVG is there" when the promoter is not trustworthy (S001, INSIGL 00:05:04). Sources: 12 (S001, S005, S006, S293, S295, S298, S494, S505, S506, S523, S527, S528). Confidence: high.

8. **A screen result is a reading list, never a verdict.** Status: RULE. "screening doesn't find multi-packers [likely multi-baggers], it finds reading worthy candidates." (S462, SOICA6 01:04:37). Even a name that matches several screens can be a "liquidity rigged stock" (S001, SOICA6 00:49:19). After a hit, read concalls, presentations and interviews to confirm the growth triggers are real (S454, MASTEC 00:32:33). The valuation sheet disclaims advice: "There is no recommendation here" (S044, UPDAT 00:15:28), and ratios flag problems that need "some time to check" in the filings (S045, UPDAT 00:14:15). Sources: 7 (S001, S044, S045, S120, S454, S462, S488). Confidence: high.

9. **Choose the valuation metric the sector's accounting supports before comparing any multiple.** Status: RULE (routing). Banks and financials: P/B with ROA, ROE and NPAs. Asset-heavy hotels, hospitals, QSR, telecom: EV/EBITDA, because 20 to 40 year assets make depreciation overstate costs; "ratio of EBITDA to market cap" (S047, UPDAT 00:23:47). Cement: EV per ton or EV/EBITDA. Real estate: market cap to pre-sales or imputed EBITDA; "P ratio is not like, is not a relevant metric" (S371, REAL 00:43:00). Deeply cyclical businesses: price to sales or price to book against their own history. Life insurance: price to embedded value. Contractors: market cap to order book. Asset-light IT and FMCG keep P/E. Sources: 7 (S001, S042, S047, S091, S136, S371, S446). Confidence: high.

10. **Value to be roughly right: a DCF is a gauge, the actionable output is the growth threshold, and valuation is a gate, not the centerpiece.** Status: RULE. "we have to be roughly right rather than be precisely wrong" (S058, MODULH 02:52:59). A DCF mostly lets an analyst "feel very good about themselves" (S001, SOICC 00:07:35); the useful output is the growth rate already priced in, found by reverse DCF (S044). The field is "50 60% art and only 40 50% science" (S439, CLASSB 00:19:56). "The valuation step is a gate, not the centerpiece of the analysis" (S520). Scenario work uses bull, bear and base cases with an exit P/E per case (S042, S089). Sources: 9 (S001, S042, S044, S058, S088, S089, S422, S439, S520). Confidence: high.

11. **Test whether each fat margin is structural or comes from policy or the cycle; only structural margins are a moat.** Status: RULE (taught as a test). Solar cell margins of 56 to 60% EBITDA existed only because of an anti-dumping duty (EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE): "the day it's lifted the industry goes bankrupt." (S005). "A moat that has to be rebuilt again and again is a moat in trouble." (S005). The warning in the worked case reads "don't pay peak PE on peak margin on peak narrative" (S009). A transformer industry exit signal: "if the pricing or the margin start correcting, that will be the indication that this industry's time is up" (S007, P3 01:37). Peak margins, peak profitability and peak sentiment arriving together are an exit cue (S111, FRAMEC 01:02:30). Sources: 5 (S005, S007, S009, S061, S111). Confidence: high.

12. **A cheap P/E on peak-cycle earnings is a trap, and a high P/E on trough earnings can be the time to study the company.** Status: RULE with a worked EXAMPLE. "PE ratio is not valuation" (S028, MODULB 00:29:15). In the worked case a cyclical at a price of sixty and trough earnings of one looks expensive; at peak earnings of thirty the same price reads two times and tricks the average investor (S028). Buying cyclicals at the peak is "the second most common way for an investor to financially destroy themselves in the stock market" (S031, MODULB 00:02:19), with crashes of 70 to 80 percent. "When their P/E ratio is getting higher, that is the time to study these companies" (S007, P3 00:41). Sources: 6 (S001, S005, S007, S028, S031, S305). Confidence: high.

13. **Use market breadth to pace deployment, stage the money in tranches, and expect a time correction rather than a V.** Status: RULE for the 20% breadth line as stated in S155 and S476; the other cuts are the instructor's heuristics. "When this number goes below 20%, then there are significant market bottoms and there are a lot of opportunities to put a lump sum" (S155, NUKE 00:00:43). Below 20% of stocks above their 200-day average comes "once in every two to three years" (S476, SOICD2 00:29:49); 16 to 17% marks bear bottoms and above 90% is extreme greed (S478, SOICA3 00:01:38). A bull phase is "goes above 60-70%" and a bear phase is "below 25 percentage" (S488, SOICB4 00:27:18). Breadth below 20% together with a Nifty 50 P/E below 20 marks extreme pessimism (S471, SOICB5 00:00:43), and Nifty 50 P/E below 20 has been followed by mostly positive 12 to 36 month outcomes (S001, SOICA6 00:17:44); staged tranches at P/E 20, 19.5 and 18.5 are an EXAMPLE (S477, SOICA6 00:38:52). "You can't time it to perfection" (S147, SOFT 00:37:20). Sources: 8 (S001, S147, S155, S471, S476, S477, S478, S488). Confidence: high.

14. **Map the value chain, find the profit pool, and ask why the best node earns more.** Status: RULE (method, no numeric cut). The course compares peers at the same node: "peer-to-peer comparison within the same value chain is used to see which players are capturing the larger share of that profit pool" (S508, part-1-highly-moated-value-chains). It reduces any industry to 4 or 5 trackable variables and asks at each node whether the listed Indian company does cutting-edge work or commodity, EPC or distribution (S005, S516). EBITDA margins of 40%+ at a chain stage are cited as an EXAMPLE of a highly moated segment, not as a cut-off (S514). Chains change: "very difficult to say why it happens" (S254, HIGHLA 00:04:41). Sources: 9 (S005, S007, S078, S254, S508, S514, S516, S521, S506). Confidence: high.

15. **Sort by return on capital and growth first; the direction of ROC leads the multiple.** Status: RULE for Bucket 1 as stated; the other four buckets are described in words only. "quality should invariably be the first filter" (S422, UNDERC 00:47:16). Bucket 1 needs earnings growth of "more than 15 to 20%" and ROC of "more than 20 to 25%" (S422, UNDERD 00:00:38; the company example is PERISHABLE). High ROC with no growth is a value trap: "legacy modes but with limited size of opportunity" (S422, UNDERC 00:48:43). Valuation tracks the direction of ROC: "one is to one correlation between your future direction of ROIC or ROC with the with the valuations" (S422, UNDERD 00:26:13). A 10% return on capital is poor against a 5 to 6% fixed deposit (S032, MODULB 00:06:37), and a 6% ROC held for 40 years gives about 6% to the investor (S082). The best business can "employ large amounts of incremental capital at very high rates of return" (S059, MODULH 02:35:42). "mode without growth is like a Rolls Royce without fuel." (S429, TVGPD 01:14:53; the note reads mode as moat). Sources: 8 (S032, S058, S059, S082, S083, S084, S422, S429). Confidence: high.

16. **A moat has no formula: name the specific right to win, prove it with same-node peer margins, and expect it to erode.** Status: RULE (taught by example). The course rejects rigid cuts such as "must have 50% ROC or I won't touch it" (S006) and treats moat work as probabilistic pattern matching. Evidence is sustained margins above same-node peers plus a hard-to-replicate asset (S005). Switching-cost test: "benefit of changing from company A's product to company's B product is smaller than the cost of doing so" (S081, MODULA 00:25:03). Eleven moat sources are listed: switching costs, learning costs, taste and habit, economies of scale, regulatory barriers, location, proprietary assets, brand, network effects, distribution, technical know-how (S081). "A moat that has to be rebuilt again and again is a moat in trouble." (S005). Moats fade: "competitive advantages will even after 10-12 years of out performance they will basically wither away" (S178, MOATA 00:07:39). A moat with no growth drifts into legacy mode where equity "becomes like a bond" (S081, MODULC 00:06:52). Sources: 7 (S005, S006, S081, S083, S178, S179, S180). Confidence: high.

17. **Make the multiple match the growth: PEG below 1.5, and a high P/E needs correspondingly high growth.** Status: RULE for PEG; EXAMPLE for the P/E to growth pairing. PEG "should ideally be less than 1.5 times" (S505, INSIGN 01:37:05). "if you are paying a PE ratio of 60 times, the company must possess a growth rate of at least 40% to 50%" (S505, INSIGL 00:04:27); a P/E of about 15 to 20 needs only 15 to 20% growth (S001). The framework record gives a P/E band of 15 to 35 for modelling and a realistic entry band of P/E 15 to 30; fast growth at a P/E of 5 is an "impractical expectation" (S001, MASTEC 00:33:51). The bar flexes with the cycle: bear markets are strict on P/E, bull markets tolerate more where growth is visible and technicals confirm (S456, MASTED 02:52:00). A high P/E is not automatically bad: very high multiples beat indices over 15 to 25 years in the case studies cited (S028, MODULB 00:29:30). Sources: 5 (S001, S028, S456, S498, S505). Confidence: high.

18. **Treat order-book businesses as lumpy: value on conversion and timing, discount one-off spikes and B2G payment delay, and plan for a sudden exit.** Status: RULE (method) with EXAMPLES. "you can't be long-term in a down cycle in an order-book-driven business" (S006, SB1 L587-593). On policy-dependent names: "I cannot put 20-30% in such a business" (S006, SBQA L150-158). Order-book stocks are sudden-exit, not slow-exit, situations (S005). Order books of 12 to 18 months are long dated and 8 to 12 months short dated (S501, INSIGU 00:03:23). Execution takes six to nine months for some orders and three to four years for road builders (S511, S524; EXAMPLE). The framework record lists the discounts: lumpiness, B2G receivable stretches of 15 to 18 months, execution slippage (S001). A decline in the book can cause a multi-year lull: "Trees cannot grow till the sky" (S112, FRAMEB 00:22:07). Order-book businesses do not earn rich P/E multiples (S145, SOFT 00:05:43). Sources: 8 (S001, S005, S006, S112, S145, S501, S511, S524). Confidence: high.

19. **Protect capital first: losses are asymmetric, so avoid the deep drawdown.** Status: RULE (arithmetic). "do not lose money" (S020, MODULB 00:11:49), because "when you lose money, the game is rigged against you" (S020, MODULB 00:12:08). From 100 rupees a 10% fall needs an 11% gain, 25% needs 33%, 50% needs 100%, 75% needs 300% and 90% needs 900% (S020, MODULB 00:12:21). Longevity beats rate: "the longevity of compounding" (S024, MODULB 00:02:28) and "Do not disturb compounding" (S027, MODULB 00:08:41). The technical exit system exists to avoid a "50-60% drawdown" (S107, ADDITA 00:25:14). Sources: 6 (S020, S021, S023, S024, S027, S107). Confidence: high.

20. **Size positions by conviction in small steps and cap the satellite bucket; the course gives templates, not one answer.** Status: RULE for the templates; the criteria for 2% against 8% are not given (see `limits.md` section 5). Balanced template 2/4/6/8%: start an exploratory idea at 2%, raised to 4% or 5% when the thesis is "getting shown into numbers" (S502, INSIGM 01:08:28); core 4, 6 or 8% and unproven names 2% (S505, INSIGM 01:16:14). Core and satellite: core 80 to 90% of the portfolio, satellite 10% maximum or 20% for aggressive investors, satellite stocks 2%, core 6% or 8% (S090, HOWI 00:00:26; HOWI 00:04:19). Beyond the satellite cap "it becomes a bit risky" (S090, HOWI 00:04:19). Diversification benefit plateaus at about 20 to 30 names, and equal weights of 4% across 25 stocks or 5% across 20 remove subjective judgment (S502, INSIGM 00:47:02). "you don't build allocation on a problem" (S149, SOFT 01:02:52). Sources: 7 (S001, S006, S090, S096, S149, S502, S505). Confidence: high.

21. **For turnarounds, look past the accounting loss to EBITDA and cash, value in ballpark terms, and build the position in steps.** Status: RULE (method). One-off costs and ESOP charges can hide a recovering business (S513, S527, S528). Balance-sheet repair is both trigger and risk: asset sales and fresh capital cut debt (S297). Valuation is "a coarse filter to decide what merits deeper work, not a precision-pricing exercise" (S527); it is forward, not trailing, with the ratio chosen per business (S005). Build in a "staggered manner" because execution is the risk, and "If simplification leads to more complexity, you will avoid it." (S113, FRAMEA 01:29:18). Patience is tested: "this is why patience is tested" (S006, TURN L101). Sources: 10 (S005, S006, S113, S116, S293, S297, S513, S520, S527, S528). Confidence: high.

22. **Prefer the first three or four quarters of an earnings inflection; later quarters carry the risk of lost momentum.** Status: RULE (lifecycle staging), with PERISHABLE examples. The question to ask is "Is it the first quarter of good results or is it like the sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth quarter" (S001, SOICE5 01:44:28). Hyper growth is top-line growth "anywhere above 20-25% on a top line level" (S496, SOICE5 01:02:25); moderate is 10 to 15% (S001). "Maximum multi baggers are made when there is a maximum period of pain and that maximum period of pain comes after negative surprise" (S496, SOICE5 01:48:32). A stock with 4 or 5 bases is prone to loss of EPS momentum (S001, CLASSG 03:07:05). Earnings downgrade cycles run 4 to 8 quarters and sometimes up to 4 years (S507, FRAMEA 00:39:41). Sources: 4 (S001, S115, S496, S507). Confidence: high.

23. **Read industry structure as a moving picture: capital cycles mean-revert, structures shift, and last cycle's leader rarely leads the next.** Status: RULE (mechanism). The capital cycle runs from returns above the cost of capital to capacity expansion, oversupply, mean reversion and consolidation (S087). "they will they will revert back to the long term average" (S087, HOWB 00:20:12). "the leading sector in one bull market usually does not lead the next" (S494, SOICC 00:02:40). "industry structures keep on shifting" (S078, MODULB 00:39:37); "2 plus 2 isn't equal to 4" (S075, MODULB 00:39:16); read the story as an "unfolding movie and not as a still photograph" (S029, MODULB 00:38:06). "understanding these cycles becomes very, very important if you want to make returns in cyclical businesses" (S375, REAL 00:15:37). Sources: 7 (S029, S073, S075, S078, S087, S375, S494). Confidence: high.

24. **Split growth into volume and realisation; volume-led growth is the highest quality.** Status: RULE (decomposition) with EXAMPLE arithmetic. Sales equal volume times realisation; both rising from 10 by 10 to 12 by 12 is +44% (S005, section 3.1; EXAMPLE). "Highest quality of growth" (S039, MODULA 00:43:49) is pure volume growth; value-led growth is good when it comes from a better mix. "Volume is more important than value" (S039, MODULA 01:00:39) because sudden value growth is often cyclical. Check realisation against export price data by HS code (S005, section 2.1; S008) and never substitute value growth for volume growth (S002). Sources: 5 (S002, S005, S008, S009, S039). Confidence: high.

25. **Read capex through the balance sheet and test it with utilisation: CWIP signals coming revenue, idle capacity drags margins, and operating leverage is never infinite.** Status: RULE (reading) with EXAMPLE figures. A high CWIP to fixed assets ratio means capex is nearly finished and can bring a "huge jump in revenues" (S034, MODULD 00:17:09); CWIP cannot be depreciated while under construction (S034). In the order-book and pre-opex cycle CWIP can reach about 50% of fixed assets, margins stay depressed to around the fifth quarter, and one entry school waits for capacity utilisation of "30-40%" (S112, FRAMEB 00:15:48). Fixed costs are already in the P&L, so growth above about 15 to 20% is where leverage shows; without margin expansion "your top line growth will be your bottom line growth" (S011, PIPE 00:30:39). "operating leverages never infinite" (S063, MODULH 02:27:35). Verify that the margin step-up actually printed after commissioning (S001). Sources: 7 (S001, S011, S034, S058, S063, S112, S449). Confidence: high.

26. **Treat institutional and promoter buying as a filter that needs a stage and industry check, not as a trigger.** Status: RULE (screen setting) with a stated hit rate. The screen looks for FII plus DII stakes "cumulatively increasing their stake by 2%" (S467, SOICE3 01:00:14). The course's own backtest of that scan found "only 25 were outperforming" out of 54 businesses (S001, SOICE3 01:01:28), barely better than a coin flip alone. Promoter participation in a warrant issue matters: "if the promoters are participating, that is very good" (S467, SOICE3 00:28:17), and warrants convert within about 18 months. Do not follow FII and DII blindly; check the market cycle and industry stage (S123, HOWA 00:57:51). Do not hold only because promoters bought (S434). Sources: 5 (S001, S120, S123, S434, S467). Confidence: high.

27. **Give every position an exit clock and an extension rule: a time stop, and a tighter average when the price runs far above its base.** Status: RULE (calibration varies by lecture). Time stop: choose 4, 6 or 8 quarters, and 1 to 2 quarters is too short (S114, FRAMEC 01:30:22); the purpose is the "opportunity cost of time" (S114, FRAMEC 01:29:00). Another lecture uses "a time stop loss of 6 to 9 months" for stalled sectors (S202, AEROB 01:06:38). Extension: when the price runs more than 70% above its 30-weekly EMA, shift to a 10-weekly or 40-daily average (S114, FRAMEC 02:01:03); a gap of more than 80% from the 200-day EMA plus a volume spike is a climax cue: "the distance between 200 exponential moving average on daily charts with the stock price goes above 80%" (S001, CLASSC 02:39:21). Bases numbered three or higher are riskier ("late stage bases", S001, CLASSG 03:07:05). Exact-peak exits are impossible; expect a "10 to 15% discrepancy" (S001, CLASSE 01:16:41). Sources: 3 (S001, S114, S202). Confidence: high.

28. **Relative strength against the index is not RSI; use RS to find leaders and RSI as the momentum check.** Status: RULE. RS compares the stock with a benchmark: a stock up 10% while the index is up 20% shows poor relative strength (S121, HOWC 01:45:41; EXAMPLE). "different dimensions and different galaxies" (S104, ADDITB 00:23:51). RSI above 70 is not a sell sign: "price momentum is such that it is doing continuously well" (S104, ADDITB 00:28:38). "strong stocks based on relative strength will take you to the strongest sector" (S455, MASTEC 01:47:11). RS screens use a market cap floor of 1,000 crores (S103, S108). Falling RS does not mean a bad business (S103), and if earnings do not follow, an RS signal can be false (S121). Timeframe settings differ between lectures; see `limits.md` section 6. Sources: 7 (S001, S097, S103, S104, S108, S121, S455). Confidence: high.

29. **Technicals sequence a thesis that fundamentals already support; they never originate one.** Status: RULE. The stage gate "never originates a thesis" and only sequences one (S001). "We will not buy anything like a technical analyst" (S099, TECHNJ 01:03:42). "TA cannot justify that because that belongs to something different stream" (S259, CAPITC 00:27:30), meaning business quality is judged elsewhere. Technical breakouts need earnings confirmation (S464, SOICD3 00:13:20). Stage reads are probabilities: "no one has a crystal ball to predict" (S099, TECHNJ 02:06:13). Price is an honesty check: "price is not telling a lie, so sometimes there is a lack of understanding in our own process" (S259, CAPITC 00:42:10). Sources: 6 (S001, S093, S097, S099, S259, S464). Confidence: high.
