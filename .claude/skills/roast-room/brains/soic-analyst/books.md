# Books: SOIC Analyst

The SOIC course has no book. It is a recorded and live lecture course by Ishmohit Arora, and this seat is built on its lessons. So this file records the course structure instead: each level and module, what it teaches in plain words, which source ids carry it, and what is not captured. Module and topic names follow the course map (S004). Descriptions are in the seat's own words.

## 1. How the course is built

The course runs in levels, from personal finance to sector work. The membership lessons describe the path (S013 to S017).
- Level 1 is financial literacy. The course describes it as covering financial planning, personal finance, knowing your country, and asset allocation with mutual funds and insurance: "financial planning explained" (S015, HOW 00:00:20). It is not captured here.
- Level 2 is fundamental analysis. The instructor describes it as "channeling it towards the level two of the course, which is all about fundamental analysis" (S014, HOW 00:00:43). It covers the "balance sheet, cash flows, income statement, ratio analysis" and forensic analysis (S014, HOW 00:00:47).
- Level 3 is valuation and portfolio allocation: a "detailed valuation deep dive, financial modeling, and also portfolio allocation" (S014, HOW 00:01:04). The sizing method named here is the "two four six eight percentage approach" (S017, HOW 00:01:35); S090 carries the criteria.
- Level 4 is buying and selling with technicals: "tools like stage analysis, like ADX, like volatility stop" (S016, HOW 00:01:58).
- Level 5 is screening: how to screen for stocks and how to use screening tools, including the course's own scan product (S016).
- Level 6 is the sectoral expert programme.
- A crash course exists for those who want to jump ahead, though the instructor says "I will still strongly urge you" to take the detailed course (S014, HOW 00:03:08).
- Pace and language: the course is available in Hindi and English, and pacing is "do the course at your own pace" (S014, HOW 00:02:35).
- Live and recurring parts: market signals "every fourteen days" and live webinars "on every alternate Sundays" (S013, HOW 00:03:56 and 00:04:15). An AI investing master class is offered as an "Added bonus" (S013, HOW 00:03:26).
- Updates are slow: the core classes are updated "once in every three years" (S013, HOW 00:04:15), so the live series carries the recent material.

## 8. Gaps inside the captured material

- The crash-course class on moats has no readable body (S005, flagged source gaps).
- There is no explicit statistics test of sector-wide wealth creation (S005).
- Destocking appears as fragments, not as a dedicated lesson (S005).
- The compiled rule set records that most frameworks carry no calibrated number because the course states none (S002). Where the seat quotes a bar it cites the lesson; where none is stated it says so.
- I find no fixed percentage stop-loss in the sources. Exits are system-based (S114) and trailing at a swing low (S259). The course does not say a percentage.
- The 2468 sizing approach is named in the membership lessons without criteria (S017) and explained in S090, where weights follow conviction and are set at cost basis. The instructor says his own holding of 35 percent in one business breaks the framework (S090).
- Many lecture dates are not stated in the sources. Where a source gives only its own date, the seat says "source dated" and treats company figures as PERISHABLE.
- The lesson transcripts contain garbled passages, and the sources flag them. A number the source marks as garbled is shown as uncertain.
- The worked analyses lack market data in places: no price or multiple for PI Industries or Venus Pipes (S009, S011). The seat does not supply one.
- The course teaches business analysis, not tips, and the seat follows that (S005).
