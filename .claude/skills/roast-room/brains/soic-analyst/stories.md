## A. Compounding and loss

### 1. HDFC Bank and the compounding formula
- **Lesson.** Time is the exponent. The course writes future value as present value times one plus rate, raised to the power n, and tells investors to maximise n, the longevity (S024).
- **Numbers as stated.** HDFC Bank EPS 10 paisa in 1996 to 57.74 in FY21; price 3.24 to about 1500; about 27 percent CAGR over 25 years (S024, MODULC 00:01:00; S022). One paisa doubling daily becomes about 53 to 54 lakhs in 29 to 30 days, against about 3 lakh from 10,000 rupees a day (S024). Buffett's absolute return of 27,44,062 percent from 1965 to 2019 needed only 20 percent compounding; the Sensex grew 500 times in 31 to 32 years at 15 to 16 percent (S024).
- **Quote.** "future value is equal to present value into 1 plus rate raised to the power of n" (S024, MODULB 00:01:32)
- **Check it implies.** Ask how long the business can keep compounding, not only how fast. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 2. Two fund managers and the drawdown table
- **Lesson.** A loss needs a larger gain to undo. Capital preservation comes before rate of return (S020, S023).
- **Numbers as stated (EXAMPLE).** A 10 percent fall needs 11 percent; 25 percent needs 33; 50 percent needs 100; 75 percent needs 300; 90 percent needs 900. Manager A earns 40 and 50 percent in years one and two, falls 60 percent in year three and gains 65 percent in year four, ending near 138.6 rupees from 100. Manager B earns 20, 15, 17 and 20 percent with no drawdown and ends at 193.75. The source records that the speaker's own final figures for B are ambiguous (193.75 and close to 196) (S020); S023 gives 136 to 138 for A and 193 to 196 for B.
- **Quote.** "when you lose money, the game is rigged against you" (S020, MODULB 00:12:08)
- **Check it implies.** Test downside first. These are hypothetical managers, not company figures. Cyclicals can compound at 100 or 200 percent for a few years and then fall 90 to 95 percent in one year or month (S020). Source dated 2026-07-27.

### 3. The chemical-engineer portfolio and the long holds
- **Lesson.** Hold businesses, not paper, and expect some to fail. Diversify across 10, 15 or 20 solid companies because survival is not guaranteed (S022).
- **Numbers as stated.** A chemical engineer held 20 to 25 chemical companies for 18 years; 3 or 4 went bust; dividends now cover his monthly living expenses (S022). Over twenty years Berger Paints was a 290-bagger and Asian Paints a 140-bagger, and Amazon returned more than a thousand times (S030).
- **Quote.** "If the business does well, you as an investor will end up doing well." (S030, MODULA 00:16:07)
- **Check it implies.** Ask first whether the enterprise is good, then whether its price is high. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

## B. Cycles, traps and industry shifts

### 4. The PE inversion and the graphite-electrode round trip
- **Lesson.** A cyclical looks cheap at peak earnings and expensive at the trough. Multiples of four to six on unstable earnings are the market's own warning (S028, S031).
- **Numbers as stated (EXAMPLE).** At 60 times earnings the profits are depressed; when earnings reach thirty against a price of sixty, the multiple is two. A real investor bought HEG at 800, sold at 1600 to 1700, bought again near 4000 at the peak, and was then locked in lower circuits (S031, MODULB 00:23:56). A graphite-electrode name where the promoter bought almost daily in 2017 before margins exploded later fell 90 percent; the source says the transcript's name is likely HEG (S434).
- **Quote.** "Do not use PE ratio very loosely" (S028, MODULB 00:34:07)
- **Check it implies.** Compare the margin to its own band before reading the multiple. The course says buying a cyclical at its high is the second most common way to ruin (S031). PERISHABLE; sources dated 2026-07-27.

### 5. Ambika Cotton, ONGC and the value trap
- **Lesson.** Long-term holding of a poor business is a recipe for disaster. Low return on capital means flat earnings and a flat chart (S032, S030).
- **Numbers as stated.** Ambika Cotton's profit after tax was flat from 2016 to 2020. A 10 rupee profit on 100 rupees of capital is a 10 percent return, poor against a 5 to 6 percent deposit. ONGC is called a zombie company; Punjab National Bank stands for state banks with misaligned owner incentives; IGL, Gujarat Gas and IRCTC are named as possible exceptions. Divi's Laboratories earned over 30 percent return on capital for two decades (S032).
- **Quote.** "cheap can get cheaper" (S032, MODULB 00:36:13)
- **Check it implies.** Categorise the stock before buying: cyclical, turnaround, slow grower or structural compounder (S031). PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 6. BlueDart and a shifting industry structure
- **Lesson.** Follow the story, not a still photograph. New entrants in a fast segment start a price war and permanently change the structure (S029).
- **Numbers as stated.** 30 percent revenue CAGR from 2005 to 2010; e-commerce logistics at 50 percent CAGR; entrants named include Delhivery, eCom Express and Rivigo; sales growth of 45, 30 and 27 percent before the shift; sales of 1,937 crores in 2014 to 3,175 crores in 2020; growth of 3.56 percent in 2021; the stock stayed down 18 to 20 percent from 2016 (S029).
- **Quote.** "unfolding movie and not as a still photograph" (S029, MODULB 00:38:06)
- **Limit.** The source rests on this one case. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 7. Kaveri Seeds and the price cap
- **Lesson.** Volume up with realization down is a trap (S039, S008, S342).
- **Numbers as stated.** S039 gives packets rising from 5.6 million to 7.09 million; S008 gives 56 lakh to 72 lakh packets. The sources differ in the second figure; both are shown. A government price cap on cotton seed in 2015 cut price per packet and sales did not follow volume. The stock peaked in 2015 (S008, S005).
- **Quote.** "sales of 2015 has not returned yet" (S342, AGROCF 00:16:02)
- **Check it implies.** Split sales into volume times realization. The course ranks volume with stable realization highest and volume with falling realization as the value trap (S008). PERISHABLE; mid-2022 lesson context for S008.

### 8. Solar Industries, volume against pass-through
- **Lesson.** Sales growth that is mostly price pass-through falls when the input price falls (S039).
- **Numbers as stated.** From 2017 to 2022, 20 percent sales growth on volume growth of only 7 to 8 percent; the rest came from passing on ammonium nitrate cost. Page Industries raised prices without losing demand; Rupa saw sales halve on a similar attempt (S039).
- **Quote.** "Volume is more important than value" (S039, MODULA 01:00:39)
- **Limit.** Volume data is rarely on the income statement. The course says to look in decks and calls (S039). PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 9. Deepak Nitrite, a segment's return on capital
- **Lesson.** Read the segment, not the consolidated number (S040).
- **Numbers as stated (EXAMPLE).** Phenolics segment EBIT of 188 crores a quarter, times four, is 752; segment investment is 2,180 of assets less 674 of liabilities, or 1,505 crores; return on capital nearly 50 percent. Advanced intermediates EBIT was 132 crores. In the same lesson ITC's FMCG segment has 16,000 crores of sales at barely a 6 percent margin; a material-handling segment at an EBIT margin of minus 36 percent re-rated 70 to 80 percent on a turnaround (S040). A separate source has the company's return on capital moving from 7 percent to 21 to 22 percent in 2008 (S421).
- **Quote.** "fear in this business that their pricing is not in their hands" (S040, MODULA 02:35:46)
- **Limit.** Multiplying one quarter by four assumes the quarter repeats. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

## C. Accounting, governance and forensics

### 10. Goodwill: Teladoc and Quess Corp
- **Lesson.** Goodwill is an imagined asset that can only be impaired. Many acquisitions outside the core area raise the risk (S038, S053).
- **Numbers as stated.** Teladoc took a 6.6 billion dollar impairment and the price fell almost 87 percent (S038, MODULD 00:36:43). Quess Corp followed an oversubscribed IPO (160 times) with an acquisition spree; goodwill reached 40 to 45 percent of equity; impairments of 506, 133 and 24 crores in one quarter made it loss-making (S053).
- **Bars.** A discount rate of 12 percent or higher is standard and a terminal growth of 2 to 4 percent normal; the flags are margins stretched from 5 to 15 percent to 5 to 20 percent and discount rates of 0.45 to 0.95 percent (S053, MODULH 00:18:07).
- **Quote.** "known as kitchen sinking" (S053, MODULH 00:21:28)
- **Check it implies.** Goodwill as a share of equity and the assumptions behind it. PERISHABLE; sources dated 2026-07-27.

### 11. Ind AS 116 and a debt-free restaurant business
- **Lesson.** A new accounting rule can change the balance sheet without changing the business (S038).
- **Numbers as stated.** Under the standard, leases become an asset and a liability, EBITDA rises because rentals leave operating expenses, and net profit falls through amortisation and interest. A historically debt-free business such as Jubilant Foodworks showed hundreds of crores of interest and over a thousand crores of borrowings (S038).
- **Quote.** "null and void for us as investors" (S038, MODULD 01:16:46)
- **Check it implies.** Compare retailers on PAT margin, and do not be fooled by a higher debt to equity from lease liabilities (S038). PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 12. Stovecraft and IndiaMART, pre-IPO window dressing
- **Lesson.** Suppressed costs return after the IPO (S053).
- **Numbers as stated.** Stovecraft cut employee benefit expenses from about 8.2 crores to 3.12 crores before listing; after the IPO they rose 40 percent quarter on quarter to 30 crores on more than 1,000 hires and EBITDA margin fell back to 6 percent. IndiaMART paused 100 crores of digital advertising and held headcount flat before listing (S053).
- **Quote.** "come back with the vengeance" (S053, MODULH 00:11:15)
- **Check it implies.** Give a new listing time before relying on its first base (S053, MODULH 00:11:58). PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 13. Related party transactions: Garware, Spandana, Abbott
- **Lesson.** RPTs are a probability, not a verdict. Test cash conversion, debtor days and dividends before judging (S054).
- **Numbers as stated.** Garware Hi-Tech Films paid nearly forty crores of processing charges to a promoter firm with gross margins above eighty percent, taking twenty-six percent of PAT. Spandana Sphoorty paid commissions of 9, then 17, then 8 percent to a firm in which the promoter held 68.31 percent. Abbott India's PediaSure sits in a private entity with revenue of 138 percent of the listed core. Glenmark had 43 subsidiaries with a net loss of 375 crores audited by secondary auditors. UPL has more than 250 subsidiaries (S054).
- **Genuine cases.** Deepak Nitrite buys nitric acid from a promoter-owned sole domestic producer; Honeywell reports high related sales as an export hub (S054).
- **Quote.** "probabilistic mindset" (S054, MODULD 00:33:24)
- PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 14. Accounting choices: Century Plywood, Reliance, Biocon
- **Lesson.** Capitalising cost or stretching asset life lifts profit without a better business (S051).
- **Numbers as stated.** Century Plywood changed its depreciation method; profit would have fallen 30 percent and fell 7. Reliance raised petrochemical plant life from 15 to 25 years to 25 to 40 years, lifting profit by about 0.6 to 2.7 percent (the source flags the transcript figure as garbled). Biocon capitalised 88 of 527 crores of gross R&D, nearly 23 percent. Vakrangee's assets implied "20 lakhs of computers per employee" before a write-down (S051).
- **Quote.** "accounting itself is an imperfect art" (S051, MODULC 00:37:00)
- **Check it implies.** Changes in useful life or method are disclosed and often appear as key audit matters. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 15. Ujjivan Small Finance Bank against PI Industries, resignations
- **Lesson.** A management exodus is an amber flag, not proof (S052).
- **Numbers as stated.** Between June and August 2021 an independent director, the CFO and the MD of Ujjivan SFB resigned and the stock fell from about 45 to about 20 rupees. In 2018 there were 42 statutory auditor resignations. As a counter-example, PI Industries lost an independent director, two CFOs, a whole-time director and its MD/CEO within two years while the business stayed strong (S052).
- **Quote.** "amber flag" (S052, MODULE 00:01:10)
- **Check it implies.** Stock price is a lagging indicator here; a regulator refusing an MD's extension is a sharper signal (S052). PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-27.

### 16. Aarti Industries, a termination fee inside revenue
- **Lesson.** One-offs buried in core revenue manufacture growth (S001, F21).
- **Numbers as stated.** Revenue included a contract termination amount of 631 crores, which made roughly 7 percent underlying PBT growth look like 50 to 60 percent (S001, MODULG 00:16:42). Another source records a Dicamba intermediate contract with a planned 40 percent IRR that was cancelled (S424).
- **Quote.** "this one was actually cancelled" (S424, UNDERB 00:38:53)
- **Check it implies.** Ask whether revenue from operations holds items that belong in exceptional items. PERISHABLE; sources dated 2026-07-26 and 2026-07-28.

## D. Agrochemicals and PI Industries

### 17. PI Industries, the 2022 lesson
- **Lesson.** The CSM moat is the manufacturer-of-record mechanism: once the innovator lists PI in each country's licence, switching costs three to five times as much (S008, S005).
- **Numbers as stated (mid-2022 context).** EBITDA CAGR of 23.4 percent from FY18 to FY22 with no degrowth year; the instructor bought at 14,000 to 15,000 crores of market cap and exited around 40 to 45,000 crores (about 2021), citing opportunity cost, and listed two risks: the resignation of KMP and independent directors, and a 2,000 crore QIP with cash earmarked for a pharma-adjacent deal, with "90% of M&A fail" quoted (S008).
- **Quote.** "once you're in, you're in... if you don't screw the business up" (S008, section 3)
- **Check it implies.** Name the registration mechanism, then list the risks. PERISHABLE; lesson mid-2022, source dated 2026-07-14.

### 18. PI Industries, what the FY25 documents showed
- **Lesson.** Risks come before the story, and the earlier worries were later seen in filings (S009, a worked analysis dated 2026-07-14).
- **Numbers as stated.** CSM exports 82.5 percent of revenue; one customer about 44 percent (35,401 of 79,778 million rupees). Pharma revenue contracted to 215 crores from 315 and the loss widened to 245 crores. CSM order book fell from 1.75 billion to 1.3 billion dollars. Agchem export growth ran 35 percent (FY21) to 5 percent (FY25). OCF to EBITDA fell to about 65 percent in FY25 from 97 to 100 percent. Contract assets rose 661 to 1,612 to 4,268 million rupees (S009).
- **Check it implies.** Margin source (IP trust, not tariff), price times volume by year, cash conversion, contract assets and management churn. The analysis itself states that scuttlebutt and a market price were not in hand. PERISHABLE.

### 19. Sharda Cropchem, shallow cyclicality
- **Lesson.** Generic prices follow the innovator's price umbrella, so margin follows innovator inventory (S008, S344).
- **Numbers as stated (mid-2022).** Intangible write-offs ran 23 to 33 percent of intangibles; one molecule's registration cost was quoted as 20 to 25 crores; registration takes 4 to 5 years; about 10 staff in China, 500 to 600 distributors and over 80 countries (S008, S344). Innovators keep 60 to 70 percent of a molecule's share after genericisation (S008).
- **Check it implies.** Track the write-off ratio of licences and watch innovator inventory. Regulated-market exposure raises margin quality but does not remove the cycle (S008). PERISHABLE.

### 20. Governance spread in agchem: Bharat Rasayan, Heranba, Excel, Meghmani
- **Lesson.** Valuation gaps in agchem track disclosure, concentration and governance (S008, S345).
- **Numbers as stated (mid-2022).** Heranba traded at 10 to 12 times earnings on spot selling, with contingent liabilities of 61 percent of net worth and a 50 crore wilful default at a subsidiary in 2017, since repaid. Excel Industries had one molecule at 35 to 50 percent of sales and a commodity PE. Meghmani's 2,4-D was about 40 to 50 percent of agchem revenue against about 2 percent for Bharat Rasayan. 27 products sit on a possible ban list, a domestic market of 12,000 crores (S008).
- **Quote.** "a lot of spot selling" (S345, AGROCH 00:00:20)
- **Check it implies.** What share of sales is in banned or ban-exposed molecules; CFO to EBITDA, inventory, receivable and payable days. PERISHABLE.

### 21. Aztec Life Sciences, a realization spike checked against trade data
- **Lesson.** A margin beat from a China supply shock is not structural (S008).
- **Numbers as stated (mid-2022).** Q1 FY22 margin of 24 to 25 percent against 20 to 22 percent guided; verified using screener export and import data by HS code; trading at PI-like 40 to 45 times earnings with CMO/CDMO at about 20 percent of the mix against 70 to 80 percent CSM for PI (S008).
- **Check it implies.** Look up the active ingredient's HS code and compare price per kilo through time. PERISHABLE.

## E. Moats, scalability, turnarounds

### 22. JSW Cement and Concord Biotech, tests of a right to win
- **Lesson.** Test the cost curve and the regulatory barrier (S006, S005).
- **Numbers as stated.** JSW Cement held 82.7 percent of GGBS in India through captive slag and power (S006, S005); another source puts it at 83 percent (S253). Concord Biotech held EBITDA margins near 40 percent or more for 15 years, with one year below 40 (about 28 percent in FY22), 41 bioreactors, and capacity utilization of 30 to 35 percent on 1,200 crores of sales against capacity of 3,000 to 3,500 crores (S006).
- **Check it implies.** Ask whether an outsider can beat the cost curve; ask how long strains and approvals take. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-14.

### 23. Samhi Hotels, a turnaround with risks listed first
- **Lesson.** List the risks before the thesis; value in ballpark terms (S006, section 3; S295, S296, S325).
- **Numbers as stated.** Debt of 1,600 crores long term plus 500 short term against reserves of 1,000; guided net debt to EBITDA of 4 to 4.5; return on capital 8 and ROE 7 at entry; EV/EBITDA about 9 to 10 two years forward against peers at 20 to 30; revenue from 20 crores to 1,090 crores over a decade; 700 new rooms taking the total over 5,400. In S325 a Sheraton Hyderabad RevPAR moved from 1668 to 7,938 and debt from 2938 to 1842 crores. The source spells the name Sami in one place and Samhi in others.
- **Quote.** "acquiring hotels which are not doing well then renovating them and rebranding them" (S325, HOTEL 01:21:15)
- **Check it implies.** Shareholder quality, geography, balance sheet, poor return ratios, then triggers. PERISHABLE.

### 24. Cheap but sold by institutions
- **Lesson.** Institutional flow separates the leader from the laggard (S123).
- **Numbers as stated.** One hotel stock at 14 times EV/EBITDA with public holding rising from 14 to 22 percent while private equity sold; another at 48 times with FII plus DII at 46 percent and public holding falling. PCBL saw institutions building and still corrected in a bad cycle (S123). IndiaMART reported near 100 percent profit growth and fell about 20 percent on subscriber-churn worries (S119, HOWC 00:27:08).
- **Bar.** The screen asks FII and DII holdings both up by more than 2 percent in a year (S123).
- **Check it implies.** Read the shareholding trend beside the multiple. PERISHABLE.

### 25. EIH in 2009, the hotel cycle and the six parameters
- **Lesson.** Do not add rooms into a down cycle, and rank hotel stocks on six parameters (S326, S329).
- **Numbers as stated.** "from 2005, stock became a 67 bagger" and then a new hotel opened into the 2009 down cycle. Quarters 3 and 4 account for 70 to 75 percent of annual EBITDA and quarters 1 and 2 for 25 to 30 percent. The six are operating leverage, iconic assets, occupancy, average room rate, debt and the timing of new rooms (S329, HOTEL 01:06:56 and 01:07:11).
- **Quote.** "stock became a 67 bagger at that point of time" (S329, HOTEL 01:57:45)
- **Check it implies.** Supply pipeline and debt first. PERISHABLE.

### 26. Entero Healthcare, share gain through bolt-ons
- **Lesson.** Growth far above the market's own is a share-gain story (S006, S227, S517).
- **Numbers as stated.** The Indian pharma distribution market grows 9 percent; the company 28 percent; not a quarter below market growth since Q1 FY22; about 40 acquisitions of small distributors with under 10 percent gone wrong; acquired companies grew 70, 68 and 88 percent (S006, section 2; S227).
- **Check it implies.** Compare growth to the market benchmark and check that acquisitions sit in the same adjacency (S006). PERISHABLE.

### 27. IEX against transmission, the regulatory vector
- **Lesson.** Policy can be a tailwind and a headwind in the same theme (S006, S229).
- **Numbers as stated.** Market coupling from January 2026 would remove the exchange's price-discovery monopoly; transmission order books grew 44 to 150 percent year on year on renewable evacuation. The course adds that IEX earned return on capital above 50 to 60 percent after stripping cash (S080).
- **Quote.** "Long term is a stupid reason to be in companies" (S006, section 5)
- **Check it implies.** If a regulatory threat has been discussed for six months or more, do not wait for it to land (S006). PERISHABLE.

### 28. Purple patches and a gold-price golden period
- **Lesson.** A business often has a three to five year stretch of outsized results and then flattens (S006).
- **Numbers as stated.** Laopala from 2010 to 2016: PAT 2.76 crores to 59, sales 77 to 250 crores, flat after Borosil and Milton entered. Avanti Feeds: operating profit from 10 to 12 crores to about 240 crores in five years. IIFL Finance's gold-loan book went from 7 to 8,000 crores to 26,000 crores in about four quarters (S006, section 4).
- **Check it implies.** Ask whether the next five years resemble the last five. PERISHABLE.

## F. Sector themes

### 29. Data centres, the frenzy and the node test
- **Lesson.** Ask whether the listed company does cutting-edge work or EPC, rental or distribution (S007, section 4).
- **Numbers as stated.** Indian names trade at 100 to 300 times earnings against Microsoft at 36 and Vertiv at about 25.5 forward; operators earn only 6 to 8 percent ROE while equipment suppliers earn 25 to 35 percent. Capex splits as IT equipment 74 percent, electrical 12, thermal 7, gensets 2, engineering 1 (S007).
- **Quote.** "How can you call the EPC's business a cutting edge?" (S007, section 5)
- **Check it implies.** Peer multiples for the true technology leaders. PERISHABLE; source dated 2026-07-14.

### 30. Solar cells and module oversupply
- **Lesson.** A margin made by a tariff is not a moat (S007, S005, S006).
- **Numbers as stated.** Solar cell makers earn 56 to 60 percent EBITDA under domestic-content rules; Longi alone has capacity equal to all Indian makers combined; India demand is about 40 to 45 GW a year while module capacity heads to about 190 GW by FY27 (S007; S006).
- **Quote.** "First the originator, then the imitator, then the idiot." (S006, section 5)
- **Check it implies.** What happens to the margin the day the duty lifts. PERISHABLE.

### 31. Smart meters and Tejas Networks, order-book traps
- **Lesson.** Headline growth hides a falling rate of change (S006, S007, S501).
- **Numbers as stated.** Smart meters: 25 crore meters at about 3,000 rupees each; about 1 crore installed; the instructor declined to value a maker at 158 to 160 times trailing earnings. Tejas Networks: PAT growth of about 177 percent in one quarter and minus 87 percent in the next (S006, S007).
- **Quote.** "you can't be long-term in a down cycle in an order-book-driven business" (S006, section 4)
- **Check it implies.** Rate of change of the order book, execution timeline, B2G payment delay. PERISHABLE.

### 32. Mean reversion in amines and panels
- **Lesson.** The market prices the capital cycle before earnings fall (S087).
- **Numbers as stated.** Greenpanel's PE fell from 34 to 13 with margins still at 26 percent. Alkyl Amines peaked as EBITDA margin hit 35 percent against a mean of 18 to 20 percent. Balaji Amines cut margin guidance from 26 to 28 percent to 21 to 22 percent. The Nifty's long-run ROE and earnings growth are given as 12 to 14 percent (S087).
- **Quote.** "meter of capital cycle" (S087, HOWB 00:18:05)
- **Check it implies.** Value on long-term mean margins, not recent peak margins (S087). PERISHABLE.

### 33. Shrimp and maize, supply shocks
- **Lesson.** Strong balance sheets survive the painful part of a cycle (S076, S375).
- **Numbers as stated.** Thai shrimp output fell from 680,000 tons to 200,000; leaders' PE was cut from 40 to under 10 by 2018. Maize rose from 12 to 13 rupees a kilo to 25 to 26 in six to nine months and returned to 14. S076 says nearly 10 percent of processing supply collapsed; S375 says almost 15 percent of capacity went off stream. Both are shown. Gujarat Ambuja survived.
- **Quote.** "no one can time the entry" (S076, MODULB 01:13:04)
- **Limit.** The instructor says this style may not suit passive investors (S076). PERISHABLE.

## G. Worked analyses

### 34. Venus Pipes, what the material supports
- **Lesson.** Risks first; and a method run on partial material says what is missing (S011, built from lessons coded PIPE and dated 2026-07-14).
- **Numbers as stated.** Margin guidance of 16 to 18 percent against a recent fall from about 18 to 19 percent; exports from about 2 to 3 percent of sales to 36 percent; promoter warrants of 71.4 crores at 7,900 a share; fittings and flanges live in December 2025; approvals take 2-3-4 years with not more than 3-4-5 vendors per project; replicating the portfolio could take 4 to 6 years and 30 crores of compliance cost (S011, S163, S164, S165).
- **Quote.** "the margin may not expand" (S011, PIPE 00:30:39)
- **Gaps stated.** No PE, EV/EBITDA, market cap or price, no long-term debt figure, no current-quarter numbers. The source records that these must be added before a view on price. PERISHABLE.

### 35. Polycab, a scoring model that let quality outvote price
- **Lesson.** Every evaluated class must clear the same 0.7 bar before a positive call (S010, a worked analysis dated 2026-07-28).
- **Numbers as stated (PERISHABLE, 2026-07-28).** Price 9,115; market cap 137,319 crores; PE 47.9; ROCE 33.2; ROE 23.0; D/E 0.02. The worked analysis computes PEG on two bases, 3-year profit growth of 28.0 percent and quarterly PAT growth of 32.8 percent, and the two give different values. CFO to EBITDA 91.3 percent from the screen and 63.5 percent from filings, with different windows. A minus 39 percent EPS haircut (82.22 against reported 134.34) had no basis in the filings.
- **Lesson on method.** Re-weighting could not fix the defect; conjunctive gating did. The source states the bars it used and sets no level to trade at.
- **Check it implies.** Which source feeds each ratio, and which window.

### 36. Raymond Lifestyle SOTP and the two discounts
- **Lesson.** A holding company discount is not a conglomerate discount (S007, section 4).
- **Numbers as stated.** Branded apparel EBITDA 400 crores at 15 times is 6,000 crores; garmenting 65 to 70 crores at 10 times (the source flags a misprint, with about 1,724 crores printed); B2B shirting 1,000 crores; gross about 15,200 crores, less 10 percent, about 13,700 crores against a market cap of 14,386 crores. The listed segments do not sum to the printed gross, so I cannot rebuild it. Kama Holdings traded 78 percent below its 50 percent SRF stake; ITC at about 30 times earnings carries the smaller discount (S007).
- **Check it implies.** Whether the entity owns operating businesses of its own. PERISHABLE.

## H. Valuation and modelling cases

### 37. Dexter Shoes and Kraft Heinz, cheap in a bad industry
- **Lesson.** A cheap multiple is an illusion when the industry is in structural decline (S073).
- **Numbers as stated.** Berkshire Hathaway bought at an average of 15 dollars with book value of 19.5, a price to book of 0.76 and PE of 7. Dexter Shoes: 93 percent of shoes bought in the country were imported by 1999. Kraft Heinz wrote down about 15.4 billion dollars (the source flags the number as likely) and fell 60 percent. A separate source says Dexter's real cost was 5 billion dollars as Berkshire stock rose (S178).
