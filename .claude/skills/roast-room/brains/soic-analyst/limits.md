# Limits: SOIC Analyst

What the SOIC course says about its own limits, which of its figures are examples, what it does not cover, what it never says, and where its sources disagree. Built from the same 528 course sources as the other files; dates are note dates (key in `principles.md`). Quotation marks mean the course's own words. Paraphrase carries no quotation marks.

Contents: 1 the course on its own limits. 2 rules that are examples or assumptions. 3 perishable numbers and how to treat them. 4 coverage. 5 the course does not say. 6 where sources conflict. 7 what outside tests of the course's ideas found (not from the course).

---

## 1. What the course says about its own limits

**Systems fail in some markets and cost part of the move**
- In sideways or choppy markets the exit system gives false breakdowns: an investor "will throw out your technicals" (S114, FRAMEC 01:13:43). Waiting for all three signals leaves "money on the table" (S114, FRAMEC 01:47:39).
- Weekly crosses churn and can shake a holder out: "churn will be higher" (S095, TECHNF 00:29:41) and "whipsawed out of the position" (S095, TECHNF 00:15:20).
- The turn from Stage 3 to Stage 4 "is again something that can be said in hindsight" (S122, HOWD 00:11:45). "no one has a crystal ball to predict" (S099, TECHNJ 02:06:13).
- Recent IPOs lack a 30-weekly history, so a 10-weekly or 21-weekly line is used (S114, FRAMEC 01:58:56). Sloping-line breakouts have a "lower probability of success" (S102, ADDITA 00:12:38). Breakouts also fail in sideways markets (S115, FRAMEB 00:12:59).
- Beyond price against the 30-weekly average, the course gives no way to tell Stage 1, Stage 3 and Stage 4 apart ahead of time (S001).
- Screens: "yesterday's winners might turn into tomorrow's losers" (S462, SOICA6 00:48:02). In very small caps "sometimes in very small caps With a little cp volume A false signal starts coming" (S108, ADDITB 00:35:26).
- The FII and DII scan found "only 25 were outperforming" out of 54 businesses (S001, SOICE3 01:01:28). A perfect checklist score is still wrong "20-30% of the times" (S001, MASTED 02:09:54).

**Ratios and accounting**
- "accounting itself is an imperfect art" (S051, MODULC 00:37:00). Ratios flag and do not prove; the reason must be found in the filing: "have to give some time to check" (S045, UPDAT 00:14:15). A single soft quarter is a flag to investigate, not a veto (S001).
- The cash flow statement is "redundant and void" for banks and NBFCs (S036, MODULB 01:54:03). Government customers are a "notoriously lazy payer" (S001, MODULA 00:22:39), and days can run "sometimes exceeding 200 days" (S057, MODULC 00:12:00). Working capital days are not comparable across B2C, B2B and B2G (S057).
- For lease-heavy businesses EBITDA margins are "null and void for us as investors" (S038, MODULD 01:16:46); compare PAT margin instead.
- ROE is "highly deceptive in cyclical industries" (S001, MODULH 00:50:23), so the ROE gate abstains for cyclicals. P/E is unsuitable for asset-heavy businesses (S042) and real estate (S371).
- Free cash flow is most reliable for "very mature businesses"; a fast grower with heavy capex can show negative free cash flow without being low quality (S036, MODULB 02:15:01).
- The value-trap logic applies to long-term buy and hold; the error is the wrong time horizon for the category (S032, MODULB 00:25:30). The FMCG penetration trap applies only once penetration is complete (S242, PREMA 00:21:53).
- "Do not use PE ratio very loosely" (S028, MODULB 00:34:07): a low multiple can reflect unstable earnings, and a high one has coexisted with index-beating returns over 15 to 25 years.
- Goodwill assumptions are not always fraud, and a single quarter is not enough to judge (S053).

**Models and forecasts**
- "inevitably be incorrect or flawed" (S443, CLASSG 01:10:39) describes every forward model. Perpetual growth for a cyclical is "impossible" (S088, HOWD 02:13:31), and a change in inputs means "all of your model will go for a toss" (S088, HOWD 02:15:46). "forecasting tells you a lot about the forecaster than the forecaster itself" (S366, FLUORD 00:17:11).
- Cycle length is unknown: "I don't know for a fact whether it will last for 12 months 10 months or pre-buying 6-7 months and then we go to a down cycle" (S189, CV 00:24:59). "the down cycle doesn't know if it will last for 5 or 6 years or 7 years" (S257, CAPITA 00:19:06). "no one really knows" how long peak margins last (S319, UNCOVC 02:08:09).
- The instructor hedges views: "I don't know what will happen in 6 months down the line It's like a simple thing to admit, I don't know." (S178, MOATA 00:11:07). "Right? I can be 100% wrong" (S144, SOFT 00:54:58). "this is a 2-3-4 year view. I am not saying what is the next quarter" (S144, SOFT 00:53:56).
- Geopolitics: "whatever one predicts can go wrong because you don't know what side of the bed one guy wakes up on" (S471, SOICC5 00:25:05). Timing: "You can't time it to perfection" (S147, SOFT 00:37:20).

**Circle of competence and advice**
- The course declines to opine on sectors it has not studied: the instructor "openly refuses to opine on sectors he hasn't read" (S005). A later oil and gas module covers some of what the earlier brief named; see section 6.
- "I am here to learn. I am not here to pass judgments" (S129, WATCH 00:52:35). "I don't have this industry I am learning this industry with you" (S210, DRONE 01:18:58). "frankly, I don't understand the business of bliss gvs" (S145, SOFT 00:13:40). Data scarcity on one name: "our circle of ignorance is higher" (S364, FLUORF 00:09:46).
- The course disclaims advice: "This is not a webinar for entry-exit criteria or valuations" (S007, section 5). "Nothing is a buy sell recommendation" (S155, NUKE 00:00:10). "There is no recommendation here" (S044, UPDAT 00:15:28). "I'm not a severe registered person [SEBI-registered person], so it is just my opinion" (S388, GOLDB 01:00:42).
- Views are tied to a date: "treat this as dad's [today's] lay of the land understanding" (S211, DRONE 01:47:32). The agrochemical views date from about FY22 (S005).

**Mistakes the course admits**
- "Even companies like I have also personally invested in I would have done this Because lot of times is a cyclical place And we were not observing it then." (S184, MOATA 00:35:30).
- The instructor invested in a few cyclicals, succeeding twice and failing once (S031, MODULB 00:26:14). One agrochemical holding was exited around 2021 over a wave of management and director resignations (S005).
- "If you are enjoying the webinar, it can be that I am making mistakes somewhere. Because I have a new industry to learn" (S131, WATCH 00:38:19). The two real estate accounting methods were first misstated and then corrected (S371, REAL 00:45:04).
- "This is why it pays to change your mind in investing." (S407, APIH 00:09:41).

**Gaps inside the material**
- A passage garbled in transcription can never source a threshold (S001). Examples of figures the course itself does not reconcile: "from 30 and a half thousand crores to 18,100 crores and [FY]28 expected at 29,890 crores market" (S128, WATCH 00:43:51); Kazakhstan's uranium share as "50" and then "which dominates 57% of the world supply" (S152, NUKE 01:08:49); plant capex "somewhere close to 12 to 13000 crores" against a later Rs. 8000 crore total (S157, NUKE 00:46:34).
- Scuttlebutt is soft (S008). Rating agencies are paid by the companies they rate: "the rating agencies are paid directly by the companies they are evaluating" (S442, CLASSB 02:33:23).
- Known source gaps: one crash-course moats lesson has no readable body; there is no sector-wide wealth-creation statistics test; destocking appears only as fragments (S005). Part of the premiumisation lecture after 01:00:28 is not covered (S246).
- A pipeline announcement signals potential, not timing or size (S175).

## 2. Rules that are worked examples or assumptions, not rules

Each line gives the class the course attaches to the figure. If a class is not stated by the course, the line says so.

- **P/E below 50, or tighter below 40**, as a companion to the growth screen: EXAMPLE of a tunable filter (S515); no reason is given for 50 against 40 (S525). **P/E under 25** with market cap over 1,000 crores and quarterly profit growth over 15%: a worked EXAMPLE screen (S498).
- **P/E 15 to 35** is the modelling band in the DCF sanity framework (S001, S002); **P/E 15 to 30** is the "realistic entry band" for fast growers (S001, MASTEC 00:33:51). Both are calibration, and the valuation bar flexes with the cycle (S456).
- **A P/E of about 60 needs 40 to 50% growth; about 15 to 20 needs 15 to 20%**: EXAMPLE pairing for the PEG rule (S505, INSIGL 00:04:27).
- **Weekly RSI above 50, ADX above 20, price 5% above the 30-week EMA**: a worked screen, stated as the filter set (S474, SOICA5 00:02:23). **RSI above 45 or 50** with a green V-Stop is another lecture's entry (S095).
- **Distance from the 52-week high under 20% and a market cap floor of 1,000 crores** for a custom sector index: worked EXAMPLE settings (S464, SOICE3 00:10:56).
- **Operating margin up 10% on the prior quarter, earnings 30% above a flat baseline, market cap under 20,000 crores, OPM above 1%**: worked scan settings (S482, SOICE3 00:42:51; S001). The scan's rigidity excluded a company whose margin went from 27.2% to 35% (S482, SOICE3 00:45:41; PERISHABLE), so a near miss still gets a manual look.
- **Theme growth potential of 40 to 60%**: a worked EXAMPLE, and the course does not say why 40 to 60% is the cut (S511, S506). **EBITDA margins of 40%+** as a profit-pool illustration: an EXAMPLE, no threshold separates an outsized margin from a normal one (S508). **Only 7 to 8 auto and related companies** fitting a very large terminal value: an EXAMPLE (S524, S526).
- **Breadth below 25% of stocks above the 200-day average as a lump-sum zone, below 20% as "juicy"**: the instructor's heuristic, not a course RULE (S147). A reading of 17% in March 2026 is PERISHABLE. The 20% line is stated as a rule in S155.
- **Nifty 50 P/E below 20 and staged tranches at 20, 19.5 and 18.5**: an observation and a worked EXAMPLE (S477, SOICA6 00:38:52).
- **Hospitals at about 30x EV/EBITDA as expensive and below 20x as cheaper; cement at about 21x as a ceiling; IHCL at 42 trailing market cap to EBITDA**: dated calibration, never to be reused as a band (S001, CLASSF 01:52:09; CLASSF 02:01:19; UPDAT 00:23:59).
- **60% theme, 20% liquidity, 20% stock picking**: an attribution claim stated without a test shown (S494, SOICC 00:03:05).
- **Order book of 12 to 18 months as long dated**: the instructor's own classification, and lead time may not apply to every firm (S289, S501).
- **Discount rate of 12% or higher and terminal growth of 2 to 4%**: stated as standard and historically normal (S053, MODULH 00:18:07). Other lectures state different values; see section 6.
- **Position templates 2/4/6/8, 268, 369, 36912**: templates, with no rule choosing between them (S090, S505). **65 to 70% in trending sectors and 25 to 30% in valuation-comfort sectors**: the instructor's own mix (S150, SOFT 01:01:17).
- **Time stop of 4, 6 or 8 quarters**: a choice the investor makes (S114, FRAMEC 01:30:22).
- **Cost of capital "around eight to ten percent"** (S084, MODULC 00:31:16): context, not a rule.
- **Sector norms**: specialty chemicals operating margin "more than 15 to 18%" and net fixed asset turns under 2x; pharma net fixed asset turns "1 to 1.5x"; FMCG operating margin from mid-teens to early twenties are EXAMPLE norms (S062, MODULI 00:03:21; MODULI 00:05:25). Node margin benchmarks such as aviation tyres at 25 to 30% EBITDA are PERISHABLE (S007).
- **PEG**: the rule gives no growth period. The worked analysis computes the same P/E against 3-year profit growth and against quarterly PAT growth and gets two different PEG values (S010, section 4; analysis dated 2026-07-28), so the basis has to be named.
- **Gold-loan LTV and rate figures** are illustrative (S392): in the worked example a loan at 75 LTV with 24% interest means a total owed of 96 against gold value 100, and real lenders "will not give out 75" (S391, GOLDB 00:14:34).
- **Quality buckets**: only Bucket 1 carries numbers; Bucket 5's description is garbled and its metrics cannot be stated (S422, UNDERD 00:02:14).

## 3. Perishable numbers (company and market snapshots) and how to treat them

The S001 freshness rule, a compiled-list rule about how to use the notes and not a bar the course states, is that every specific number is a snapshot on a lecture date. A figure below may be used as the mechanism it illustrates or as a prior to re-check, never as current data (S001).

Snapshots seen in the course material:
- The DCF framework's lesson is generic: when the discount to intrinsic value is thin, returns are "mediocre unless growth >20-25%" (S001, DCF framework). The seat quotes no company's price or intrinsic value from it.
- PI Industries: core ROCE ex-cash 35.1% in FY24 and 28.9% in FY25, net cash ₹4,093cr (S009).
- SRF: market cap ₹64,162cr, with an implied ₹57,000cr on the chemicals division against ₹728cr EBIT, an ~80x EBIT multiple (S001, FLUORE 00:17:43).
- IHCL: trailing market cap to EBITDA of 42 (S001, S091). CEAT: ROE collapsing to about 6% (S001). Kama Holdings at a 46% to 80% discount to its SRF stake (S092).
- Nifty 50 at 19 times P/E after two years of consolidation (S142, EVA 00:17:29). Breadth at 16 to 17% (S471, S478) and 17% in March 2026 (S147).
- Statutory auditor resignations "spiked to 42 resignations across the market in 2018" (S052). One company's contingent liabilities at 479% of net worth (S049).
- Fibre optic prices up "402 to 650% in the last 6 months" (S001, SOICA6 00:32:17). Entero Healthcare growing at 28% against a pharma market at 9% (S517). Gold support at $4,524 and an iron ore view of 80-85 against a price of 104 dollars (S146, SOFT 01:30:25).
- Real estate "prices of the real estate have peaked out for the next 4-5 years" (S147, SOFT 00:31:12).

How to treat them:
- Check the age. For financials older than one quarter, and for any price or valuation figure of any age, re-fetch live data (S001).
- If live data contradicts a stored figure, live data wins, and the gap is itself information: a thesis break or noise.
- A figure from a garbled transcript passage is a pointer to verify, never an input (S001).
- Do not carry a company example across companies: a margin that is outsized for one node is not a benchmark for another.

A concept note condenses part of one lecture; the median is 820 words and the mean about 1,047, so a lecture's detail is compressed. Index notes such as S012, S018 and S033 carry no teaching of their own. ## 5. The course does not say

Each item was checked against every source and no source states it. The closest statement, if any, follows.

1. **A fixed stop-loss percentage below the entry price.** The exits are systems (30-weekly EMA, relative strength, V-Stop) and a time stop in quarters. For those without conviction, "put your stop loss very closely" (S462, SOICA6 00:53:35), with no number. The 10% to 25% and 10 to 15% figures describe what a system gives up from the top, not a stop level. One garbled aside pairs the words stop loss and 10% and cannot be used (S281, AROMA 01:43:38).
2. **Delivery volume or delivery percentage as a signal.** No source mentions it. Volume is used as breakout confirmation, with on-balance volume and volume profile (S100, S102, S106).
3. **A ceiling on promoter pledge.** Direction only: rising pledge with rising receivables and debt is an "automatic red flag" (S001, SOICC2 00:48:09), and fully pledged shares are a "huge red flag" (S470, SOICC2 00:45:02). A zero-pledge reading appears only in a worked case (S009).
4. **A minimum or maximum promoter holding.** The nearest items are a scan for promoter holding that "has changed by more than 1% or 2%" (S123, HOWC 01:28:43) and a SEBI rule on public shareholding used to size a capital raise (S171).
5. **A pass or fail ROCE, CFO to EBITDA or debt to equity bar for each sector.** The bars are general: CFO to EBITDA by B2C and B2B, debt to equity 0.7, ROC above 15%. Sector norms are examples only (S062), and the course rejects rigid cuts such as "must have 50% ROC or I won't touch it" (S006).
6. **A ceiling for receivable, inventory or payable days.** Days are judged against peers (S036), and the course warns that they are not comparable across B2C, B2B and B2G (S057).
7. **A minimum interest cover or a debt to EBITDA ceiling.** Only company figures appear: a hotel turnaround guided to 4 to 4.5x net debt to EBITDA (S006, PERISHABLE) and a hotel illustration of 2x (S328, HOTEL 00:29:07).
8. **A limit on one customer's or one product's share of sales.** Only company examples: one molecule at 35 to 50 percent of sales draws a commodity P/E, and one company targets a cap of 12 to 13 percent per product (S008, PERISHABLE).
9. **A single-stock cap that fits every investor, or a cap on one sector's weight.** The templates carry maxima at cost (8% in 268 and 2468, S090), but no rule picks between them: the introduction "never explains the criteria for assigning a 2% versus an 8% weight to a specific asset" (S017, HOW 00:01:35), and the portfolio construction lecture gives no numeric cap, stop-loss rule or explicit exit trigger (S149). The instructor's own 35% in one business is, in the note's words, an "over concentration" that breaks the framework's limits (S090, HOWD 02:25:23).
10. **A dividend payout or yield threshold.** Only that payouts of 80% to 90% cripple compounding (S059) and that a high yield without growth is a warning for yield vehicles (S499).
11. **Forensic scores such as Beneish M-Score, Altman Z or Piotroski.** No source teaches them.
12. **A way to tell Stage 1, Stage 3 and Stage 4 apart ahead of time** beyond price against the 30-weekly average (S001), or to time the Stage 3 to Stage 4 turn (S122).
13. **What "bombed out" means.** No percentage fall or time since listing is given, and no steps once a stock is flagged (S506, S507).
14. **How to rank parallel filters when they conflict**, a checklist of risk categories, weights across the four TVGP legs, and evaluation criteria for management (S506, S523, S527).
15. **What counts as "reasonable" in a ballpark valuation**, or the metric to use for it (S506, S520).
16. **The growth period for PEG** and the time window for the growth screen (S522); see section 2.
17. **How long "sustained" share gain must last** and how to rule out an acquisition or a low base (S517).
18. **How per-stage margins are isolated from consolidated filings** to find a profit pool (S514), and the accuracy at which an AI-built value chain map can be accepted (S506).
19. **What to do when annual reports or rating reports are missing or disagree** (S204).
20. **A numeric definition of an outsized margin** beyond the 40%+ illustration (S508).

## 6. Where sources conflict

The course does not reconcile these. Show both, with dates, and do not pick one silently.

1. **CFO conversion.** CFO to EBITDA: "should ideally be around 70% for consumer-facing B2C businesses and 60% for B2B businesses" (S036, MODULB 01:58:04) against "CFO to A bit [likely EBITDA] greater than 60-65% is your preference" (S494, SOICC 00:14:09). CFO to PAT: "should ideally be above 80% to 90%" (S470, SOICC2 00:16:24) against "more than 70% for B2C business and for B2B is more than 60%" (S045, UPDAT 00:13:06) against "CFO to Pat greater than 90-95% is your preference" (S494, SOICC 00:14:01). S470 also says a solid business "might routinely convert over 50% of its operating profit to cash" (S470, SOICC2 00:16:24). The framework record says to treat the ranges as independent calibration, not one number (S001).
2. **Relative strength length.** "For weekly charting, the RS length is typically set to 26 weeks or 52 weeks" (S114, FRAMEC 01:54:57) against "The default 52-week setting should be avoided because it lags and misses the earliest signs of strength" (S103, ADDITB 00:06:14), which recommends 13 or 26 weeks.
3. **V-Stop multiplier.** "A standard system uses a timeframe length of 10 and a multiplier of 2 or 2.5" (S114, FRAMEC 01:55:28). "the multiplier is set to 2.5 or 3 for structural compounders, and 2 for cyclical stocks to ensure tighter risk control" (S095, TECHNB 01:31:23). The monthly system tightens to 2 for inflated valuation, widens to 2.5 for steady compounders and to 3 or 3.5 for 10 to 15 year holders (S093, HOWB 00:55:55; HOWD 00:46:48; HOWC 00:36:15). The RSI floor also differs: "it should ideally cross above 45 or 50" (S095, TECHNE 00:16:53) against 50 elsewhere (S122).
4. **Scope of the technical exit.** "recommended primarily for 'satellite PF stocks' rather than 'core holdings'" (S001, FRAMEC 01:56:49), against the compounder method "we only use technicals for exit" on a monthly V-Stop (S107, ADDITA 00:11:44; ADDITA 00:19:16). The horizon is also bounded: the monthly setup is built for two to four years, and for 10 to 15 year holders the triggers "might actually represent optimal moments to accumulate more shares" (S093, HOWC 00:36:15).
5. **Satellite cap and portfolio mix.** "Cap the total weight of the satellite portfolio at a 10% maximum, though aggressive investors can extend this ceiling to 20%" (S090, HOWI 00:04:19) against "The maximum allocation for the entire satellite bucket should never exceed 20% of the total portfolio" (S096, TECHNK 01:59:34). A third split: "65 to 70 percentage of the portfolio will be with the sectors which are trending in the market" (S150, SOFT 01:01:17).
6. **Number of holdings.** "a diversified portfolio of 10, 15, or 20 solid companies" (S022, MODULC 00:03:46); "A portfolio should contain a minimum of 10 and a maximum of 15 to 25 stocks" (S096, TECHNK 01:55:40); "owning 20 to 30 stocks captures the maximum benefit of diversification" (S502, INSIGM 00:47:02).
7. **Time stop.** 4, 6 or 8 quarters (S114, FRAMEC 01:30:22) against "I have put a time stop loss of 6 to 9 months" (S202, AEROB 01:06:38).
8. **Entry valuation bands.** P/E 15 to 35 for modelling and 15 to 30 for entry (S001); a P/E filter below 50 or 40 (S005, S006, S515); a screen with P/E below 30 (S097, TECHNJ 00:20:28); a screen with P/E under 25 (S498, INSIGV 00:00:05). All are filters or examples; none is stated as the single course band.
9. **Time-correction length.** "almost one seventy five or one seventy seven weeks of time correction" and "88 weeks of consolidation", both at the same point in one lecture (S477, SOICD6 00:51:11); "can routinely drag on for 18 to 21 months" (S484, SOICE6 00:06:51); 19 to 20 months (S001, SOICA6 00:00:41); "six to nine months" (S498, INSIGR 00:11:55).
10. **Breadth cut-offs.** "When this number goes below 20%, then there are significant market bottoms and there are a lot of opportunities to put a lump sum" (S155, NUKE 00:00:43); 16 to 17% marks bear bottoms and below 20 to 25% is the signal to deploy (S478, SOICA3 00:01:38); below 25% with the index price above its own is a lump-sum zone and below 20% is "juicy" (S147, SOFT 00:34:11); "especially below 25 percentage, this is the bear market phase" (S488, SOICB4 00:27:22).
11. **Discount rate and terminal growth.** "A discount rate of 12% or higher is considered standard and acceptable, and a terminal value growth expectation between 2% and 4% is historically normal" (S053, MODULH 00:18:07) against a rate "typically set around 7% or 10%" and "a modest, mature rate like 4% or 5% globally" (S088, HOWD 01:34:39; HOWD 01:45:55), and a cost of capital "generally estimated to be around eight to ten percent" (S084, MODULC 00:31:16).
12. **Use of a DCF.** "I do not do a DCF basically" (S422, UNDERC 00:53:02) against the direct and reverse DCF sheet (S044) and the DCF sanity step (S001). A DCF is also called an "anti-tool which tells you where the froth is getting created" (S494, SOICC 00:05:45). On float-cornered stocks one lecture says "valuation has been rendered unnecessary" and then "I genuinely believe valuation matters" (S357, MASTEA 00:43:54; MASTEA 00:44:35).
13. **Holding philosophy.** Investors "require a minimum time horizon of three years" (S022, MODULC 00:04:20) against "Long term is a stupid reason to be in companies." (S006, SB2 L111-114).
14. **Low P/B.** For lenders, "a low P/B (e.g. 0.5x) is usually NOT a value bargain" (S001, TVGPG 00:36:19).
