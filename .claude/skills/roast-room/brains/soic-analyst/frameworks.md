# Frameworks: SOIC Analyst

Every distinct framework the SOIC investing course teaches for Indian listed equities, merged across the course sources, most-used first inside each group, plus the course's own Decision Frameworks F1 to F40 (source S001) under their own F-numbers. The title of S001 reads "F1 to F41" but its text carries F1 to F40; the course does not say what an F41 would be.

How to read an entry:
- "Sources" counts distinct sources that state or apply the idea, with ids. A source id with a lesson code, for example (S001, MODULB 01:58:04), points to the lesson and timestamp.
- Quotes are quoted from the notes on the course. Everything without quotation marks is paraphrase.
- Each threshold carries a tag. RULE: the course states it as a rule or a screen setting. EXAMPLE: a worked example or one company's snapshot. ASSUMPTION: the course says "let's assume" or builds a model input. PERISHABLE: company-specific or dated; never reuse it as a current figure. Source dates are note dates: S001 to S003 2026-07-28; S005 to S011 2026-07-14; S012 to S123 2026-07-27; S124 to S225 2026-07-26; S226 to S233 2026-07-28; S234 to S246 2026-07-26; S247 to S254 2026-07-28; S255 to S291 2026-07-26; S292 to S306 2026-07-28; S307 to S424 2026-07-26; S425 to S505 2026-07-27; S506 and S508 to S528 2026-07-14; S507 2026-07-27.
- Where two sources state different numbers for the same thing, both are shown with ids. The course does not reconcile them.
- "How to score" is guidance built only from the thresholds the course states. It is not a view on any company and not advice to act.
- Where the course is silent on something an analyst would expect, the entry says "the course does not say".

# Screening and quality

## 1. F38 TVGP: Theme, Valuation, Growth, Promoter, risks first
- **What it is:** The course's four-pillar company check, taught as a repeatable method for analysing stocks and above all for turnarounds. S298 names it "Theme, Value, Growth, Promoter"; S001 writes Theme, Valuation, Growth, Promoter; one transcript reads "Team Valuation, Growth and Promoter" (S505).
- **Parts:** Risk first (S005, S523, S527): list the downside before the thesis. Named risks: shareholder quality (PE or FII holders are called shaky shareholders in S006), local cyclicality, exact debt against EBITDA, poor return ratios as expected before an inflection. Then Theme (macro tailwind, growth driver, customers, value-chain position, competition), Valuation (ballpark, see entry 28), Growth (a NEW sub-checklist: new capex, new earnings run-rate, new margin economics, deleveraging, new management or institutional owners, new regulation), Promoter (skin in the game, institutionally run firms, board tenure, ESOPs, S295).
- **When to use:** After a name has passed a screen and needs a method that does not depend on a complex model; most of all for turnarounds and self-help cases.
- **Data it needs:** Latest concall and results, shareholding pattern, debt against EBITDA, CFO against PAT and EBITDA, the promoter's record.
- **How to score (guidance):**
  - Weighting, RULE as the framework states it: theme 60%, liquidity 20%, stock picking 20% of a stock's move (S001, SOICC 00:03:05).
  - PEG below 1.5x, RULE (S001, INSIGN 01:37:05). A PE of about 60x needs 40 to 50% growth and a PE of about 15 to 20x needs 15 to 20% growth, a rough correspondence the course gives (S001, INSIGL 00:04:27).
  - Promoter pillar gated on cash conversion, RULE: CFO/PAT above roughly 90 to 95% and CFO/EBITDA above roughly 60 to 65% (S001, SOICC 00:14:01). Entry 10 shows the lower bars other sources state; the course does not reconcile them.
  - A DCF is treated as a froth gauge, not a precise output (S001, SOICC 00:07:35).
  - The promoter pillar is a veto: the course says to walk away from a crooked promoter even when theme, value and growth are present (S001, INSIGL 00:05:04).
  - Growth triggers listed in S006: inflection, ROE or ROC expansion, fastest PAT growth, a valuation gap, margin expansion.
- **Quote:** "60% of a stock's move is attributable to the theme, 20% is attributable to the liquidity and last 20% is attributable to your stock picking" (S001, SOICC 00:03:05)
- **Sources:** 17 (S001, S005, S006, S009, S293, S295, S297, S298, S494, S497, S505, S506, S513, S520, S523, S527, S528)

## 2. F25 CANSLIM accelerating-growth screen and the Results Tracker
- **What it is:** A funnel to narrow the whole listed universe to a short list of accelerating businesses, adapted by the course from William O'Neill's CANSLIM. The course calls its quarterly version the Results Tracker.
- **Parts:** (1) Quarterly sales growth and PAT growth above the bars below; (2) a structurally new catalyst (geography, client, product, capex); (3) sector leader with rising institutional ownership; (4) volume confirmation; (5) run the settings in screening software. Letters: C current quarterly earnings and sales, A annual earnings, N new, S supply and demand with volume, L leader or laggard, I institutional sponsorship, M market direction (S454 says M can be ignored when screening companies).
- **When to use:** First-pass idea generation. The course says it is not a buy signal by itself.
- **Data it needs:** Quarterly and annual sales and PAT growth, shareholding trend of FIIs and DIIs, price and volume.
- **How to score (guidance):**
  - Quarterly sales growth above 15% YoY and PAT growth above 20% YoY, RULE (screen setting, S001, MASTEC 00:09:35; S119; S228; S506; S522; S525). Refresh after each quarter and sort by market cap.
  - Realistic entry PE band 15 to 30x, RULE; a fast grower at PE 5 is called an impractical expectation (S001, MASTEC 00:33:51).
  - Results Tracker overlay: P/E below 50, or below 40 (S006, S522, S525). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: in the quarter described, about 150 companies of the 1,000cr plus universe qualified and only 30 to 40 showed sustained operating-profit growth (S006, SB2 L446-497).
  - Sequential margin screen, RULE as scan settings: operating margin up 10% quarter on quarter for three or four consecutive quarters (S119, HOWA 00:40:12); market cap under 20,000cr, a flat-PAT baseline, a breakout of 30% over that baseline, OPM above 1% to exclude lenders (S001, SOICE3 00:14:46; S482). A near miss (margin 27.2% to 35%) was screened out by the strict rule and deserves a manual look.
  - Caveat from the course: a company can post 100% profit growth and still fall if the market is discounting a forward problem (S001, MASTEC 00:27:08).
- **Quote:** "OPM latest quarter is greater than of OPM one quarter back by 10%" (S001, SOICE3 00:42:51)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S005, S006, S115, S119, S228, S454, S482, S506, S513, S522, S525)

## 3. F26 Four-part comprehensive investing checklist, with Scan Match
- **What it is:** A checklist of checklists run in order so an early failure saves later work: fundamentals, management, technicals, valuation (S001, S456, S450).
- **Parts:**
  - Fundamentals: ROC or ROE above 15% or trending there, expanding and stable margins, visible operating leverage, D/E above 1x treated with caution, profit converting to cash, a stated growth catalyst.
  - Management: independent background checks, insider selling into bullish guidance is a hard red flag, cash hoarding without dividends and heavy related-party deals are amber flags.
  - Technicals: primary trend up, relative strength not consistently negative, RSI crossing above 50, 10-week and 30-week EMA golden crossover.
  - Valuation: PE and price-to-cash-flow against peers and history, PEG below 1.5.
  - Scan Match addition: count how many separate screens flag a stock, using signals such as relative strength, quarterly growth, bulk and block deals and fundamentals (S462, S006). A profitability bar for the underlying screen is sustained ROCE above 20% and operating margin near 28 to 29% (S001, SOICA6 01:10:45), RULE as a screen setting.
- **When to use:** As the final sanity gate after a narrower screen such as entry 2.
- **Data it needs:** ROC or ROE, D/E, margin trend, cash conversion, shareholding and insider disclosures, chart data, PE, price-to-cash-flow.
- **How to score (guidance):** Pass or fail each part in order. The course says even a perfect score is wrong "20-30% of the times" (S001, MASTED 02:09:54). Routine tax disputes are tolerable; only severe fraud or criminal cases are an instant reject (S001, MASTED 02:07:53). The PE bar flexes with the cycle: strict in bear markets, looser in bull markets for a visible multi-year runway with technical confirmation (S001, MASTED 02:51:39). F23 entry (ADX above 20 plus weekly RSI above 50) and this entry's golden crossover are two separately sourced entry variants; they are not shown to agree.
- **Quote:** "screening doesn't find multi-packers [likely multi-baggers], it finds reading worthy candidates" (S001, SOICA6 01:04:37)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S006, S450, S456, S462)

## 4. Growth drivers, the NEW catalyst search and the four phases of a theme
- **What it is:** The course's way to explain why growth is happening and whether it can last. Fundamental analysis studies the rate of change, so the search is for something new (S120, S494).
- **Parts:**
  - Seven growth drivers (S006): self-help or capital-allocation turnaround; market-share gain through a bolt-on acquisition; new-market-category creation; under-penetration with a decade-long tailwind; capacity-expansion-led scale; acquisition-led capability infill (works only in the same industry or an adjacency); regulatory tailwind or headwind.
  - Seven-step variant-perception framework (S444): look beyond industry demand, find white spaces, track new products and geographies, use regulatory vacuums, watch demergers, align with tailwinds and deleveraging, then combine 8 or 9 variables.
  - Announcement keywords to screen (S120): allotments of warrants, preferential issue, acquisition, update on acquisition, related party transaction, acquisition of shares; then read three to four quarters of calls and match to a macro tailwind.
  - Four levers of earnings growth (S429): top-line expansion, operating leverage and cost efficiency, capacity additions, corporate actions and balance-sheet changes. Capex ladder by return on capital: debottlenecking highest, brownfield lower, greenfield lowest.
  - Top-down levels (S440): a macro incentive, then direct beneficiaries, the supply chain, niche businesses.
  - Four phases of a theme (S233): emerging, established, re-established, mature.
  - Market-share gain (S517) and scalability (S524, S519): growth faster than the market benchmark is read as a competitive signal; scalability asks whether the business can grow without proportional cost; a smaller base gives a larger relative growth, and the base effect works against large companies (S232).
- **When to use:** Right after a screen hit, to separate a real driver from a one-off.
- **Data it needs:** Concall and presentation text, order and capex disclosures, a market benchmark growth figure, filings for warrants and acquisitions.
- **How to score (guidance):** A driver needs a date and a source. S444 states a sector rule of thumb, ASSUMPTION grade: about 60% of a stock's return is dictated by its sector. S232 gives a base-effect EXAMPLE: a firm going from 50 crores to 500 crores has a much higher rate of change than one going from 5,000 to 25,000. The course does not say how many drivers are enough.
- **Quote:** "when the good, the bad and the ugly trade together, the valuations given that are of the ugly" (S444, CLASSA 01:03:46)
- **Sources:** 12 (S006, S120, S232, S233, S429, S440, S444, S494, S513, S517, S519, S524)

## 5. Moat sources and right-to-win tests
- **What it is:** The course's way to name what protects a margin. The idea: strong margins attract competition, so a business needs a structural defence (S178). The moat is described as a Lakshman Rekha around ROC (S081).
- **Parts:**
  - Eleven moat sources (S081): switching costs, learning costs, taste and habit, economies of scale, regulatory barriers, location, proprietary assets, brand, network effects, distribution, technical know-how.
  - A second list of fourteen (S179): switching cost, scale, property assets, learning curve, license, brand power, early mover, customer lock-in, network effect, becoming a verb, location, lowest cost, integration, technical know-how.
  - Demand-side moat (customer preference, usually B2C) versus supply-side moat (inputs, process, cost, usually B2B) (S176).
  - Right-to-win tests, reconstructed in S006 because the course never states one checklist: cost curve, replicability, sourcing network, regulatory moat, buyer concentration.
  - Three-point moat checklist (S180): market dominance, a niche and unique company, higher margin than peers.
- **When to use:** Before paying for a margin, and again when a margin is under attack.
- **Data it needs:** Same-node peer margins over several years, the specific barrier (approvals, licence, sourcing network, switching mechanism), customer and supplier concentration.
- **How to score (guidance):**
  - Rule of one hand, the instructor's personal rule: more than five competitors on the fingers of one hand means a sector not to invest in (S179, MOATA 00:21:20).
  - A moat that must be continuously rebuilt is no moat (S179, MOATA 00:22:56). Moat without growth also becomes a value trap (S180, MOATB 01:02:49).
  - Evidence is a sustained margin gap against same-node peers (S005). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE (S180): a margin band of 21 to 26% against 12 to 13% for a generalist peer.
  - Temporary moats exist: a supply shortage widens a short-term moat (S186); a gaming or solar moat must be rebuilt every few years (S186).
- **Quote:** "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all" (S179, MOATA 00:22:56)
- **Sources:** 16 (S005, S006, S081, S082, S176, S177, S178, S179, S180, S181, S182, S183, S184, S185, S186, S254)

## 6. Legacy moat versus reinvestment moat
- **What it is:** Two life paths for a high-return business. Legacy path (S083): high returns, excess capital, capital multiplies faster than opportunities, saturation, payout, the equity behaves like a bond. Reinvestment path (S084): returns above the cost of capital, a surplus, a high reinvestment rate, allocation, high incremental returns.
- **Parts:** Reinvestment rate is one minus the dividend payout (S084, MODULD 00:01:36). The capital-light compounder minimises assets, invests in intangibles, and passes cash to owners (S080). A monopoly cash-cow platform can reinvest 10 to 15% of its cash flow in early-stage startups, a legacy moat with a twist (S413).
- **When to use:** To judge whether a high ROC will keep compounding or turn into a cash-yield story.
- **Data it needs:** ROC and incremental ROC, payout, size of the reinvestment opportunity.
- **How to score (guidance):** Cost of capital is "generally estimated to be around eight to ten percent" (S084, MODULC 00:31:16), EXAMPLE grade. S061 uses about 10% in India. Rare reinvestment opportunities are the limit: "Capital multiplies faster than redeployment opportunities." (S084, MODULC 00:05:48). The course does not give a payout level that marks a legacy moat.
- **Quote:** "Capital multiplies faster than redeployment opportunities." (S084, MODULC 00:05:48)
- **Sources:** 7 (S059, S080, S082, S083, S084, S085, S413)

## 7. SOIC five-bucket valuation framework
- **What it is:** A sort of companies by ROC (or ROIC) and earnings growth, not by multiple. Quality is the first filter (S422).
- **Parts:** Bucket 1: earnings growth more than 15 to 20% and ROC more than 20 to 25%, in reinvestment mode with a large size of opportunity. Bucket 2: high ROC, little growth, legacy mode, limited opportunity. Bucket 3: good ROC and higher growth; the course says most Indian opportunities today sit here. Bucket 4: good ROC but cannot grow earnings at a high rate. Bucket 5: gruesome business with margin fluctuation and cyclicality. S401 applies the buckets to API makers and watches the forward rate of change of ROC as a company moves from a low-ROC bucket toward bucket 3 and then 1.
- **When to use:** To rank a sector's companies before valuation, and to see whether a re-rating has quality behind it.
- **Data it needs:** ROC level and trend, earnings growth, margin stability, size of the opportunity.
- **How to score (guidance):** The bucket 1 numbers are RULE as the lecture states them. Company names placed in each bucket are EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE. Bucket 5 is garbled in the source, so its exact metrics are not given. Buying at the wrong margin level risks permanent loss of capital (S422, UNDERD 00:17:54).
- **Quote:** "quality should invariably be the first filter" (S422, UNDERC 00:47:16)
- **Sources:** 2 (S401, S422)

## 8. F33 Smart-money corporate-action screen
- **What it is:** A screen for filings that may show insider or institutional conviction (S001, S120, S123, S467).
- **Parts:** (1) Keyword screen of filings for warrant, allotments of warrants, preferential issue, acquisition; (2) for a warrant, check that the promoter, not only an outside investor, takes part, and note the typical 18-month conversion window; (3) screen for FII plus DII stake up 2 percentage points or more in a year; (4) read how promoters acquired shares (open market, preferential, warrants); (5) overlay the industry stage, stage 2 or stage 4.
- **When to use:** As one input among several; never as a sole trigger.
- **Data it needs:** Exchange filings, shareholding pattern trend, the industry chart.
- **How to score (guidance):** Screen setting, RULE: FII and DII both up by more than 2% over a year; promoter holding changed by more than 1% or 2% (S123, HOWC 01:28:07). The course's own backtest of the 2% scan listed 54 businesses of which 25 outperformed, so more than half did not (S123, HOWA 01:00:54). A warrant during an industry tailwind (stage 2) is called a signal of upcoming growth (S120, HOWA 00:28:38). Do not follow institutions blindly; check the market cycle and industry stage (S123, HOWA 00:57:51). Promoter warrant EXAMPLE (S434): 15% paid upfront and 85% within 18 months, at Rs. 200 per share for a 2% stake.
- **Quote:** "no business is good or bad it's all about time" (S001, SOICE3 00:58:27)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S120, S123, S434, S467)

## 9. F18 Sitting-duck incumbent displacement
- **What it is:** An efficient or aggressive operator takes share from legacy incumbents who are weak, leveraged, disorganised or hit by forced regulatory disruption (S001, S368).
- **Parts:** Find the vulnerable incumbent; find the supply gap or forced exit (China's fluorspar crackdown is the course's worked case); find a greener or better substitute; find the growth engine in drugs and agrochemicals.
- **When to use:** When competitors in an industry are indebted, unorganised or constrained, and a stronger player can consolidate.
- **Data it needs:** Segment revenue growth against sector or nominal GDP growth, share data for the weakened incumbent.
- **How to score (guidance):** The course asks whether the target is growing revenue at 20 to 30%, well above nominal GDP growth (S001, FLUORA 00:10:32), RULE grade as a test question. The course's analogies: Titan against neighbourhood jewellers, private banks against PSU banks, APL Apollo against indebted pipe makers (S001). PERISHABLE: China's fluorspar share of global supply was put at 60%, with a 15 to 20% global supply gap (S001, FLUORA 00:12:32; note date 2026-07-28).
- **Quote:** "more efficient players come in and they start grabbing away market share." (S368, FLUORA 00:04:29)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S362, S368)

# Forensic and accounting

## 10. F21 Cash-conversion and forensic red-flag gate (veto class)
- **What it is:** Reported profit is an accrual opinion; cash conversion is the check. In the compiled framework list a failed gate stands against the idea whatever valuation or growth says, and passing it never produces a buy signal (S001).
- **Parts:** (1) CFO against EBITDA and against PAT over 3 to 5 years; (2) receivable days against peers and any receivables delayed beyond 180 days; (3) traded-goods share of revenue; (4) one-offs buried in core revenue; (5) governance: promoter pledge, qualified audit opinion, related-party deals, CFO or KMP resignations, a private promoter entity in the same business (justifies a holding-company-style discount); (6) cumulative PAT against cumulative CFO over 10 years (S045); (7) negative cash flow can be temporary year-end inventory loading (S470).
- **When to use:** On any non-financial company before any bullish framework's output is trusted. Not for banks and NBFCs, where cash flow is "redundant and void" (S036, MODULB 01:54:03); entry 51 and entry 24 gate lenders.
- **Data it needs:** CFO, EBITDA, PAT multi-year, receivable days, other income to subtract (S050), pledge percent, audit opinion, related-party detail.
- **How to score (guidance):**
  - CFO/EBITDA about 70% for consumer-facing B2C and 60% for B2B, RULE of thumb (S001, S036, S045, S050). The compiled rule set (S002) sets a numeric bar at a 3-year average of 60% or more (COMPILED RULE).
  - CFO/PAT: S001 and S470 say ideally above 80 to 90% (SOICC2 00:16:24); S001 F38 and S494 say above 90 to 95% (SOICC 00:14:01); S045 states 70% for B2C and 60% for B2B against PAT (UPDAT 00:13:06). The course does not reconcile these.
  - Subtract other income from EBITDA before the ratio (S050).
  - Legitimate exceptions the framework respects: B2B strategic inventory; B2G customers that pay late, which are slow-paid and get lower valuations (S001, S050); a promoter on TV is a softer flag only. One soft quarter is a flag to investigate, not a veto.
  - EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: Aarti Industries revenue included contract-termination income of 631 crores, which made about 7% underlying PBT growth look like 50 to 60% (S001, MODULG 00:16:42; note date 2026-07-28).
  - Ratios signal a possible problem; they do not prove fraud (S045).
- **Quote:** "should ideally be around 70% for consumer-facing B2C businesses and 60% for B2B businesses" (S001, MODULB 01:58:04)
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S005, S009, S010, S036, S045, S050, S055, S470, S494)

## 11. Revenue and expense manipulation
- **What it is:** The mechanisms that make profit look better than cash (S055, S051).
- **Parts:**
  - Five revenue moves (S055): stuff distributor channels (EXAMPLE: ship 15 bikes where 10 sell); lenient credit to consumers, visible as receivables overdue beyond 180 days; dilute sales with low-margin traded goods (10 to 15% manufacturing margin against about 1% on trading); bury one-off windfalls in core revenue; book speculative compensation early.
  - Cost chain (S051): incur costs (interest, pre-operating costs, R&D, trial-run salaries and power); capitalise them into CWIP or intangibles; lengthen the useful life; switch the depreciation method, for example written-down value to straight-line.
- **When to use:** When margins or growth look too clean, or CWIP and intangibles grow faster than the business.
- **Data it needs:** Receivable ageing, traded versus manufactured split, notes on capitalised costs, depreciation policy changes.
- **How to score (guidance):** Capitalising R&D is treated as aggressive accounting; conservative companies expense R&D and pre-operating costs (S051, MODULC 00:39:13). The course gives no numeric limit on capitalisation. S001 F21 adds the conversion test: a fast decline in operating-profit-to-cash conversion is a major red flag.
- **Quote:** "poor quality of revenues" (S055, MODULB 00:17:03)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S051, S055, S470)

## 12. Related parties, governance and resignations
- **What it is:** A scan for extraction, instability and weak controls.
- **Parts:**
  - Governance checklist (S068): board and KMP quality and stability; compensation and disclosure timeliness; auditor report, key audit matters, CARO annexures; related-party deals and contingent liabilities; insider trading and promoter buybacks.
  - Extraction pattern (S054): complex structure (over fifty subsidiaries), promoter-owned private entities, deals that should be at arm's length, inflated fees, variable commissions, launching the best products in unlisted entities. Tests that a related-party deal is genuine: healthy OCF to EBITDA, debtor days showing money is collected, dividends paid (S054).
  - Resignation cascade (S052): audit red flags first (main auditor relying on other auditors for loss-making subsidiaries; audit fee doubling with flat revenue), then exits of CFO, replacement CFO, company secretary, independent directors, MD over 14 to 15 months, then regulator action, then the price falls.
- **When to use:** Before any bullish framework's output is trusted, and after any management exit.
- **Data it needs:** Annual report notes, board changes, auditor reports, shareholding and pledge data.
- **How to score (guidance):** RULE-grade bars stated in S066 and S068: related-party transactions above 10% deserve suspicion (S068 states it as 10% of sales); routing 35 to 40% of PAT through related parties is called uninvestable; miscellaneous expenses of 20 to 30% of other expenses are a red flag; maximum management remuneration is normally 11% of PAT. A mass exodus is an amber flag that demands scepticism, not proof (S052, MODULE 00:01:10); ignoring it is called a cardinal sin. EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: statutory auditor resignations spiked to 42 across the market in 2018 (S052). Counter-view: the instructor downplays related-party paranoia where the commercial logic is sound (S008, section 5).
- **Quote:** "Anything more than 10% should be viewed with suspicion." (S066, MODULJ 00:11:24)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S008, S052, S054, S066, S068, S345, S470)

## 13. Capital misallocation and off-balance-sheet risk
- **What it is:** A four-step sequence (S049): hoard cash (sometimes 60 to 70% of total assets); promoters lend it to their own private subsidiaries; unproductive capex such as maintenance, replacement or gold plating; corporate guarantees kept off the balance sheet as contingent liabilities.
- **When to use:** On cash-rich companies that pay little, and on any company with guarantees to related entities.
- **Data it needs:** Cash and investments against total assets, loans to subsidiaries, contingent liabilities against net worth, capex split.
- **How to score (guidance):** Be cautious if contingent liabilities exceed 20% of net worth, a caution level the instructor states (S049, MODULG 00:25:55). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: one company held contingent liabilities of 479% of net worth, mostly guarantees to relatives and subsidiaries (S049). Cash hoarding without dividends is an amber flag (S001 F26).
- **Quote:** "gold plating" (S049, MODULE 00:35:25)
- **Sources:** 4 (S045, S049, S058, S001)

## 14. Pre-IPO window dressing, goodwill and Ind AS 116
- **What it is:** How reported numbers around a listing and an acquisition can mislead.
- **Parts:** Window-dressing sequence (S053): strategic drift and narrative; pre-IPO cost suppression; post-IPO rebound of costs; inorganic growth and goodwill; goodwill assumption manipulation and kitchen sinking. Goodwill mechanics (S038): excess of price over net assets, impaired only, tested every year. Ind AS 116 (S038): leases go on the balance sheet, EBITDA rises, amortisation and interest are charged, net profit falls. The elevated pre-IPO base makes the first 3 to 4 post-IPO quarters look weak (S507).
- **When to use:** On recent IPOs, serial acquirers, and lease-heavy businesses.
- **Data it needs:** Cost lines before and after listing, goodwill and impairment assumptions, lease notes.
- **How to score (guidance):** Impairment assumptions: a discount rate of 12% or higher is called standard and terminal growth of 2 to 4% historically normal; far more aggressive assumptions are a red flag (S053, MODULH 00:18:07), RULE reference. EXAMPLE (S038): a target with 20 crores of net assets bought for 50 crores leaves 30 crores of goodwill. For lease-heavy businesses (airlines, retailers, QSR) the instructor says EBITDA margin comparison is "null and void for us as investors" and PAT margin should be used (S038, MODULD 01:16:46).
- **Quote:** "come back with the vengeance" (S053, MODULH 00:11:15)
- **Sources:** 3 (S038, S053, S507)

# Ratios and capital efficiency

## 17. Working-capital cycle and activity ratios
- **What it is:** How fast a business turns inventory and receivables into cash. Inventory days is 365 over inventory turnover; receivable days is 365 over sales to receivables; payable days is 365 over COGS or purchases to payables; the cycle is receivable days plus inventory days minus payable days (S057). Net working capital is trade receivables plus inventory minus trade payables (S050).
- **Direction rule (S036):** current assets such as inventory and receivables move inversely to cash flow; payables move directly. Memory aid in S050: "Ram is direct, Sunny is inverse" (S050, MODULC 00:14:25).
- **When to use:** Any non-lender, especially B2G, custom-engineering and fixed-price businesses.
- **Data it needs:** Multi-year balance sheet and P&L, peer days.
- **How to score (guidance):** The check is receivable days against peers (S036, S050, S057). EXAMPLE (S452): inventory 60 days, customers pay in 90, suppliers are paid in 2 gives 148 days for cash to return. B2G money gets stuck with the government, so higher working capital there is business model, and slow-paid firms get lower valuations (S036, S050). A fixed price for a year with no raw-material pass-through leads to full-year stocking, long working-capital days, negative CFO, term loans, then an equity raise (S348). Customised products need high inventory (S161). The course does not give a day-count limit.
- **Quote:** "trade receivables plus inventory minus trade payables is the formula for networking capital" (S050, MODULC 00:22:25)
- **Sources:** 6 (S036, S050, S057, S161, S348, S452)

## 18. F22 DuPont ROE-quality decomposition gate
- **What it is:** ROE equals net profit margin times asset turnover times financial leverage (assets over equity). Margin times turnover is ROA (S061). ROCE splits the same way: EBIT margin (the demand-side advantage) times capital-employed turnover (the supply-side advantage) (S058).
- **When to use:** Any quality thesis resting on a headline ROE or ROCE, and any peer ranking inside a sector.
- **Data it needs:** Net margin, sales, total assets, equity, debt, multi-year.
- **How to score (guidance):** The course states the direction only: prefer ROE from margins and turns over ROE inflated by debt. It states no numeric leverage-share ceiling, so the gate lowers conviction directionally and cannot give a hard pass or fail (S001). For sectors flagged cyclical the gate abstains and entry 55 applies, because ROE is deceptive at the peak and the trough. Context benchmarks, EXAMPLE: cost of capital about 10% in India, ROE of 25% is phenomenal, Nifty 500 average ROE about 14% (S061). Rising EPS with falling ROCE degrades earnings quality and signals a value trap (S058). PERISHABLE peer cases (S001, MODULH 01:01:00; note date 2026-07-28): APL Apollo ROE about 24.64% from about 3x asset turnover; Vedant Fashions 28% ROE from about 31% net margin; CEAT ROE fell to about 6% on raw-material costs.
- **Quote:** "an ROE driven by strong profit margins and efficient asset turnover rather than one inflated primarily by debt" (S001, MODULE 00:19:37)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S058, S061, S450)

## 19. ROIC, ROIIC and intrinsic compounding
- **What it is:** ROIC is PAT or NOPAT over invested capital. ROIIC is incremental earnings over incremental invested capital. Reinvestment rate is incremental invested capital over cumulative earnings. Intrinsic compounding rate is reinvestment rate times ROIIC (S059).
- **Parts (screener-sheet steps, S046):** sum profit over the period; incremental capital employed is end capital minus start capital; reinvestment rate is incremental capital over cumulative profit; return is profit growth (end year minus start year) over incremental capital.
- **When to use:** To test whether growth was bought with capital at good returns, and in reinvestment-moat cases (entry 6).
- **Data it needs:** Ten years of profit and capital employed.
- **How to score (guidance):** The course gives no pass mark. EXAMPLE (S046): the worked sheet returns 17% reinvestment and 49% return, but the source numbers are garbled, so do not cite them. ROCE can mislead at both ends for cyclicals: gestation after big capex depresses it and operating leverage later lifts it (S058). Backward integration lowers ROIC in the short run but cuts volatility (S059). A QIP inflates capital and gives a "skewed picture" (S059, MODULH 02:44:21).
- **Quote:** "leaving the question of price aside, the best business to own is the one that over an extended period of time can employ large amounts of incremental capital at very high rates of return" (S059, MODULH 02:35:42)
- **Sources:** 4 (S046, S058, S059, S084)

## 20. F27 Balance-sheet 7-point health checklist, with CWIP
- **What it is:** Seven checks read straight from the balance sheet (S001, S451): (1) D/E strength; (2) is capex expanding the fixed-asset base; (3) working-capital direction, inventory plus receivables against short-term borrowing; (4) fixed-asset turnover, sales over fixed assets, to class asset-light or asset-heavy; (5) cash cushion; (6) total debt burden; (7) multi-year direction.
- **When to use:** As the general balance-sheet read on any company.
- **Data it needs:** Multi-year balance sheet, D/E, fixed-asset turnover, cash.
- **How to score (guidance):** D/E below 0.7x is the healthy threshold, RULE (S001, MASTEA 01:52:15). D/E above 1x is treated with caution in entry 3. A balance sheet deleveraging toward zero long-term debt often precedes a major move; debt ballooning against negative reserves is "good money after bad money" (S001, MASTEA 01:24:19). CWIP lifecycle (S034): funds are applied, the unfinished project sits in CWIP, completion moves it to fixed assets, then staff, power and depreciation hit the P&L. A high CWIP to fixed-asset ratio signals capex nearly done; the course states it as a general reading, not a number. S112 says CWIP can reach about 50% of fixed assets in the build "tunnel", EXAMPLE. A buyback creates value only at a reasonable valuation (S035).
- **Quote:** "rate of change in fundamentals" (S001, MASTEA 01:53:17)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S034, S035, S112, S450, S451)

## 21. Volume times realisation
- **What it is:** Sales equal volume times realisation. Split growth into value-led, volume-led, both, or volume up with value down (S039).
- **When to use:** Every time a growth number is read, and in agro, consumer, cement and commodity names.
- **Data it needs:** Volume and realisation per year from decks and calls.
- **How to score (guidance):** EXAMPLE (S039): 10 packets at Rs. 10 is 100; at Rs. 12 is 120; 12 packets at Rs. 10 is 120; 12 at Rs. 12 is 144. Pure volume-led growth is the highest quality; value-led growth is good when it comes from a better mix; sudden value growth is often cyclical (S039). Rankings differ by source: S342 puts both rising best, then double-digit volume with stable realisation, and calls volume up with realisation down the trap; S008 puts double-digit volume with stable realisation as best and most durable, double-digit volume and realisation together as epic but often temporary pricing disruption, and volume up with falling realisation as the Kaveri Seeds trap. S002 notes that F8 states a 10% volume-growth figure and that value growth must not be substituted for volume growth. EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE (S009): FY25 export growth of 5% was volume plus 7 and price minus 2.
- **Quote:** "Volume is more important than value" (S039, MODULA 01:00:39)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S005, S008, S009, S039, S223, S342)

## 22. F7 Operating-leverage ramp, with the pre-opex cycle
- **What it is:** When a new unit goes live, fixed costs are already in the P&L and extra utilisation converts at a high contribution margin (S001).
- **Parts:** Capex phase, idle-asset phase (operating deleverage), demand turnaround (operating leverage), cash-flow acceleration (S453). Order-book and pre-opex cycle (S112): a build "tunnel", margin depression often to about the fifth quarter, order book builds, commercialisation, peak execution and peak PAT.
- **When to use:** Plant ramps, PEB, hotels, hospitals, any fixed-cost business.
- **Data it needs:** Utilisation and capacity, employee cost and depreciation lines, order book, quarterly margins.
- **How to score (guidance):** Growth above about 15 to 20% is where leverage kicks in; below it top-line growth is roughly bottom-line growth, the stated downside case (S001), RULE grade; the compiled rule set (S002) sets quarterly sales growth YoY at or above 20, a COMPILED RULE against the lecture's 15 to 20 band. Entry: two approaches, not one rule, buy at the worst pre-opex quarters or wait for de-risked entry at 30 to 40% utilisation (S112, FRAMEB 00:15:48). EXAMPLE, not a rule (S449): sales 1 crore, operating cost 90 lakh, 20% of cost fixed; sales up 20% lifts margin from 10% to 13%, another 20% to 15%. EXAMPLE (S325): revenue up 15% and opex up 13% added 2% to EBITDA and PAT. Leverage has a limit: "operating leverages never infinite" (S063, MODULH 02:27:35). Hotels: financial deleveraging plus operating leverage is called a double engine (S330).
- **Quote:** "whenever the demand comes back then there is an operating leverage time bomb that the company is sitting on" (S453, MASTEB 01:26:18)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S063, S112, S291, S325, S330, S449, S453)

## 23. Segment ROCE, unit economics and steady-state ROC
- **What it is:** Returns read at the level of a division or a single project, not only the company.
- **Parts:** Division ROCE (S040): segment EBIT over segment assets minus liabilities, with a quarter annualised by four, which assumes the segment stays this profitable all year. Project unit economics (S063): gross block, gross asset turns, EBIT margin, working capital, capital employed equal to gross block plus working capital, unit ROCE equal to EBIT over capital employed. Hospitals (S351): occupancy, average length of stay, average revenue per occupied bed.
- **When to use:** Multi-segment firms, capex-heavy firms, sectors where reported ROC swings with the capex cycle.
- **Data it needs:** Segment disclosure, capacity and utilisation, capex timing.
- **How to score (guidance):** Gross margin is mix, operating margin is utilisation, and ROC swings with capex (S170). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE (S170): gross margin about 55% with full utilisation gives about 25% operating margin; steady-state ROC for that business type around 25 to 30%. Use steady-state utilisation and margins, because reported ROC normalises as companies keep adding capex. Sector-norm EXAMPLES (S062): FMCG operating margin mid-teens to early twenties; specialty chemicals above 15 to 18% with net fixed-asset turns under 2x; pharma 1 to 1.5x.
- **Quote:** "normal steady state asset utilization on steady state operating margins" (S170, CDMO 00:33:06)
- **Sources:** 6 (S040, S062, S063, S170, S351, S406)

## 24. F28 Bank and NBFC ROA tree, with the NBFC checklist
- **What it is:** The lender's profit build: net interest income, plus non-interest income, minus OPEX gives PPOP; minus provisions gives PBT; minus tax gives PAT; PAT over average assets is ROA; ROA times leverage is ROE (S001, S334, S426). Book value absorbs retained PAT, so ROE and ROA drive the P/B the market assigns.
- **Parts:** NBFC 20-point checklist in five steps (S335): business model, liability profile and funding, asset profile, profitability and risk metrics (spread, NIM, GNPA, NNPA, credit cost, cost to income, ROA, ROE, leverage), valuation. Bank value creation in four steps (S336): shift to retail and secured, improve asset quality, let operating leverage act, shift to retail deposits. Turnaround sequence for regional and PSU banks (S332): new book against old book, recoveries above slippages, credit cost falls.
- **When to use:** Any bank or NBFC, where entry 10 does not apply.
- **Data it needs:** NII, fees, OPEX, provisions, GNPA, NNPA, average assets, funding mix.
- **How to score (guidance):** A low P/B such as 0.5x is usually not a bargain; it usually reflects weak asset quality (S001, TVGPG 00:36:19). Read P/B with ROE, ROA and NPAs, never alone. Match liability duration to asset maturity (S335). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: cost-to-income of 76% at one small finance bank went with falling ROA and ROE (S001, TVGPG 00:29:35); GNPA peaked at 13.4% in 2018 (S339); private banks went from 26% to 40% of deposits (S339). EXAMPLE (S336): retain 10 rupees to make equity of 110, take 9x leverage, and the book grows from 1000 to 1100.
- **Quote:** "This is like EBITDA for a bank" (S334, DECODC 00:03:44)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S332, S333, S334, S335, S336, S339, S426)

## 25. F24 Sector-metric routing: which valuation lens is valid
- **What it is:** A routing rule, not a signal. Pick the metric the sector's accounting supports before any band check (S001).
- **Parts (routing):** banks and financials to P/B; asset-heavy (hotels, hospitals, QSR, telecom) to EV/EBITDA; cement to EV per ton or EV/EBITDA; real estate to market cap over pre-sales or imputed EBITDA; asset-light IT and FMCG to plain P/E and cash-flow metrics; deeply cyclical (auto-ancillaries, chemical makers) to price-to-sales or price-to-book; life insurance to price-to-embedded-value; contractors to market cap to order book, P/E or P/CF; conglomerates to sum of the parts (S446).
- **When to use:** Always, as the first valuation step.
- **Data it needs:** Sector class, EV components (market cap plus total debt minus cash), EBITDA, pre-sales, embedded value, order book.
- **How to score (guidance):** Output is only the metric key. Real estate EXAMPLE (S374): EV over projected pre-sales under bear, base and bull cases (3,000 crores up to 4,500 to 5,000 crores). EV/EBITDA roughly signifies payback period (S446). Compare cyclicals to their own historical median, not an absolute level. EXAMPLES, PERISHABLE dated calibration, not durable thresholds: hospitals about 30x EV/EBITDA expensive and below 20x cheaper; cement ceiling about 21x; a hotel group's trailing market cap to EBITDA of 42 (S001, CLASSF 01:52:09 and 02:01:19; S091). Asset-light platforms lose about 3.6 to 3.7% of sales to depreciation against about 20% of the P&L for asset-heavy operators (S001, CLASSF 01:52:27). No threshold may be sourced from garbled passages.
- **Quote:** "by their overall report card percentage" (S001, HOWE 00:01:25)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S042, S047, S091, S319, S329, S374, S432, S446)

## 26. F10 DCF sanity and growth threshold, with direct and reverse DCF
- **What it is:** The course's valuation discipline: a DCF, or a reverse-PE at a 15 to 35 times band, gives intrinsic value; the usable output is the growth threshold. If the discount to intrinsic value is thin, returns are mediocre unless growth exceeds the modelled rate, about 20 to 25% (S001).
- **Parts:** Direct DCF (S044, S088): free cash flow is CFO minus net capex; growth for years 1 to 5 and 5 to 10; discount rate; terminal growth; terminal value is the payment over discount rate minus growth. Reverse DCF: set present value to today's market cap, let goal seek find the growth already priced in, then verify 0% over- or undervaluation (S044).
- **When to use:** After sector routing (entry 25), as a sanity check on what the price demands.
- **Data it needs:** Live price, EPS, growth, free cash flow. Recompute; never reuse a stored DCF value (S001).
- **How to score (guidance):** The 15 to 35 band is a modelling band, not a knockout; the compiled rule set pairs it with 3-year profit growth at or above 20 (S002). The band appears only in the compiled lists and the worked case (S001, S002, S010), so it is a COMPILED RULE, not stated in a lecture note. ASSUMPTION inputs in the worked models: discount rate typically around 7% or 10%, 10 years at 10 to 12%, terminal growth 4% or 5% (S088); S053 calls 12% or higher standard and 2 to 4% terminal growth historically normal. Terminal value is 55 to 60 percent of valuations (S088, HOWD 01:46:42). Reverse DCF EXAMPLES: a 10% rate and avoid if five years at 50 to 60% growth are needed (S443); 30% for 15 to 20 years is hyper overvaluation (S088). Limits: "all of your model will go for a toss" (S088, HOWD 02:15:46); DCF fails for cyclicals; a DCF is a froth gauge more than a precise output (S001, SOICC 00:07:35).
- **Quote:** "interest rates are like gravity to stock valuations" (S088, HOWD 01:30:47)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S010, S044, S053, S088, S443)

## 27. Bull, base and bear scenario modelling
- **What it is:** Returns come from earnings growth and from re-rating by perception, so the exit PE is a manual input per scenario (S042).
- **Parts:** (1) Project revenue or earnings growth for 3 to 5 years in three cases; (2) exit margin from commentary and operating leverage; (3) check the median PE over the last three years; (4) set a different exit PE per case; (5) read the compounding return per scenario (S042). S428 gives six steps: deconstruct the income statement, gather history and guidance, estimate non-core lines, compute forward PBT and PAT, set PAT growth and exit P/E per case, optionally automate.
- **When to use:** After the ballpark check, on candidates worth deeper work; update every 6 to 12 months (S089).
- **Data it needs:** Latest results, concall guidance, historical PE.
- **How to score (guidance):** All numbers are EXAMPLE or ASSUMPTION: growth cases of 10%, 9% and 7% (S042); PBT of Rs. 290 at 25% tax gives PAT of Rs. 217.5, with PE 30 base, 20 bear, 40 bull (S089); base case 20% growth against a bear case of 15 to 18% growth with contracted margins, where a base case earning more than a 15% CAGR gives a safety net (S443); risk-reward EXAMPLE of bear 12 to 13%, base 18%, bull 30 to 35% (S089). Hikal case (S407): check that the expected value of the bet is positive. Apply the same stress to exits: model bull, base and bear to see which exit multiples are baked into the market cap (S111). The course does not say how to weight the three cases.
- **Quote:** "the expected value of the bet is positive" (S407, APIH 00:17:24)

## 28. Ballpark valuation instead of a DCF
- **What it is:** The Value leg of TVGP for turnarounds: skip a DCF and decimal precision, and ask one question: does the business look reasonable at its current valuation (S520, S527, S528).
- **Parts:** Forward numbers, not trailing; the ratio chosen after studying the business (P/E, EV/EBITDA, or EV to operating cash flow when EBITDA is a poor proxy for cash, as in domestic pharma); a value-trap check (S005, S006).

Precision does not exist; be roughly right, not precisely wrong (S058, MODULH 02:52:59; S439). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE (S005, section 3.4): a hotel owner with 1,600cr debt at 9 to 10x EV/EBITDA against peers at 20 to 30x. Sources say a business that is merely cheap is not yet investable (S513).

## 29. F29 Value trap, growth trap and bubble recognition
- **What it is:** Three mispricing patterns, all resolved by tracking the rate of change in earnings, not the multiple alone (S001). Bubbles: price rises on expectation of further price rises, often with circular financing. Growth traps: 30 to 50x plus PE assuming supernormal growth; the base effect makes the same growth rate harder and deceleration derates the multiple. Value traps: a low single-digit PE with no catalyst; in cyclicals the PE looks cheap or infinite at the wrong points.
- **When to use:** As a screen-out beside entries 22 and 26, on both the expensive and the cheap side.
- **Data it needs:** PE and its own history, PAT growth trend, cycle position.
- **How to score (guidance):** The 30x figure is the stated lower bound of the growth-trap band and is a question prompt, not a score: the compiled rule set (S002) keeps a PE of 30 or more as an advisory flag only, because calling a trap early is often indistinguishable from being wrong. EXAMPLE (S001, TVGPG 01:12:26): a sample of 500 low-PE names found only about 10 to 11 beat the index without a growth catalyst. Cyclicals: buying at the peak is the second most common way investors destroy themselves, with crashes of 70 to 80% (S031, MODULB 00:02:19). Growth without a moat or a market becomes a value trap (S180, S342). "PE ratio is not valuation" (S028, MODULB 00:29:15).
- **Quote:** "valuation viewed in conjunction with the growth rates of a company matter" (S430, TVGPF 00:32:52)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S028, S031, S083, S180, S342, S430)

## 30. P/E as perception, the double engine and re-rating
- **What it is:** Price is earnings multiplied by a perception multiple. The course says P/E reflects intrinsic value (cash flows) plus transactional value (sentiment and demand) and calls it a perception-to-earnings ratio (S028, S360, S448).
- **Parts:** Price drivers: low interest rates, global liquidity, economic cycle, sentiment, global events, expected growth and duration (S028). Double engine: EPS growth and P/E re-rating together (S429, S432). Reflexivity: price creates narrative, narrative creates events (S360).
- **When to use:** To explain a move that earnings alone do not, and to size what a multiple has to do.
- **Data it needs:** EPS, P/E history, sector multiple.
- **How to score (guidance):** EXAMPLE (S432): EPS 20 and P/E 10 is price 200; EPS up 20% to 24 and P/E to 20 gives 480, a 140% return on 20% earnings growth. A sector can grow earnings with poor perception and lag, or double its PE with no earnings growth (S042). A stable company is valued like art or gold because buyers expect it to hold value 20 to 50 years (S360). Evidence (S448): consistent companies in volatile sectors delivered the highest returns over 2007 to 2022.
- **Quote:** "price creates narrative which in further creates events which in further drives the price" (S360, MASTEC 00:05:16)
- **Sources:** 6 (S028, S042, S360, S429, S432, S448)

## 31. F19 Sum of the parts and conglomerate stub-valuation
- **What it is:** Value the legacy or cyclical segments conservatively against pure-play peers, subtract from market cap, and read the residual stub as what the market pays for the growth core (S001). ## 32. F13 Relative valuation for near-identical peers: P/E versus P/B gap
- **What it is:** When two same-model companies show a persistent P/E and P/B gap not explained by growth or ROE, either the market is mispricing one or there is an unpriced qualitative difference (governance, disclosure, diversification into riskier lending). Investigate before assuming the cheaper one is undervalued (S001).
- **Parts:** Check whether ROE or growth explains the gap; name the qualitative difference; look for a re-rating catalyst or accept the gap as structural. For lenders the P/E or P/B choice depends on competition: with low competitive pressure and entry barriers the course credits Prof. Sanjay Bakshi with focusing on P/E (S388). Perception cycle (S359): a higher price to book means less dilution when raising capital.
- **When to use:** Two close listed peers with different multiples, mainly lenders.
- **Data it needs:** P/E, P/B, ROE for both; recompute, since worked numbers go stale (entry 16).

## 36. F23 Weinstein stage analysis: stages, entry screen and limits
- **What it is:** Four stages tie price and volume to earnings momentum. Stage 1 is a base around a flat 30-weekly average, with ownership moving "weak hands to strong hands". Stage 2 is the markup: a breakout above the base and the 30-weekly average on high volume, driven by a positive earnings surprise and rising estimates. Stage 3 is topping. Stage 4 is the markdown, price below the 30-weekly average with loss of EPS momentum (S001, S099, S122, S431, S447).
- **When to use:** To sequence an entry or exit on a thesis a fundamental framework already likes. The course says timing never originates a thesis; it sequences one (S010, section 5). The exit system is recommended mainly for satellite holdings.
- **Data it needs:** Weekly price and volume, 30-week EMA, relative strength against the Nifty, ADX and RSI.
- **How to score (guidance):** Entry screen, RULE as screen settings: weekly ADX crossing above 20 and weekly RSI crossing 50 (S122, HOWB 00:01:55; S488). A second variant (S095): volatility stop turns green (length 10; multiplier 2.5 or 3 for structural compounders, 2 for cyclicals), ADX above 20, RSI above 45 or 50. A pullback in stage 2 on very low volume shows sellers are missing (S122). Base count: a third or later base is increasingly risky and four or five bases invite loss of EPS momentum (S001, CLASSG 03:07:05). Macro stage-3 tells: a large number of IPOs, historically high PE, amateur investors entering (S001, CLASSG 03:10:50). A stock often falls 10 to 15% without hitting its stop on the weekly chart, which is why the weekly chart is used (S259, CAPITC 00:35:35). Limits: the stage 3 to 4 turn is "again something that can be said in hindsight" (S122, HOWD 00:11:45); sideways markets give false breakdowns; a recent IPO lacks 30 weeks of history, so use the 10-weekly or 21-weekly (S114, FRAMEC 01:58:56); overhead supply slows recovering charts. The course does not give a way to tell stages 1, 3 and 4 apart ahead of time beyond price against the 30-weekly average. EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE (S010): RSI 56.6 and ADX 29.4 must not change the call.
- **Quote:** "weak hands to strong hands" (S122, HOWD 00:22:34)

## 51. F12 Lending safety: how a lending business dies
- **What it is:** A lender fails by (1) asset-liability tenure mismatch, short funding against long collateral that can fall faster than the loan amortises, or (2) dependency risk, repayment resting on one external factor that can move 50% or more overnight (S001, S392).

# Capital cycle and cyclicality

## 54. Cyclical versus durable, and the capital cycle
- **What it is:** A test of whether margins are structural or cyclical, plus the supply-side loop behind cycles. Capital cycle (S375): rising prospects bring competition; returns fall below the cost of capital; weak players exit and investors turn pessimistic; supply goes cold; returns rise above the cost of capital and the share price outperforms. S427 gives five steps: attraction and optimism, overbuilding and glut, margin deterioration, capitulation and consolidation, recovery; start from supply, not demand.
- **Parts:** Pull 10 to 11 years of margin data across all peers; if they move in lockstep it is an industry cycle, not execution; decompose growth into price times volume every time (S005). Supply-shock cycle (S076): shock cuts supply, input prices spike, weak players fail, supply normalises, prices crash and margins reset. Over-earning versus under-earning (S319): judge whether a B2B firm is earning abnormal margins from a temporary tailwind; best winners started under-earning. Know the game first: cyclical, turnaround, slow grower or structural compounder (S031).
- **When to use:** Before paying for a margin or a PE in any commodity, capital-goods, chemical, auto or property name.
- **Data it needs:** Long margin history for the node and its peers, capacity additions, price and volume series.
- **How to score (guidance):** Cycles run about 4 to 6 quarters; commodity down-cycles can last a decade; buy a cyclical when capex is done and a new cycle is starting (S005). Value on long-term average margins (S087, HOWB 00:29:44). Use price to book or price to sales against own history (entry 25). Buying cyclicals at the peak is the second most common way investors destroy themselves, with crashes of 70 to 80% (S031); cyclicals can compound 100 to 200% for 3 to 4 years then fall 90 to 95% (S020). Cycle length follows build time: a hotel takes 3 to 5 years, an ethanol plant 6 to 9 months (S326). Without a moat, growth is borrowed time (S184).
- **Quote:** "I've seen gluts not followed by shortages, but I've never seen a shortage not followed by a glut." (S427, TVGPD 00:31:26)
- **Sources:** 10 (S005, S020, S031, S076, S087, S184, S319, S326, S375, S427)

## 55. F5 Cyclical entry on observable signals (replacement-cycle industries)
- **What it is:** Deep cyclicals such as commercial vehicles and hotels run multi-year cycles, with down-cycles that can run 7 to 8 years. Time entries on observable signals, not forecasts (S001). At the trough ROE and ROC look terrible; that is timing, not disqualification.
- **Parts:** Commercial vehicles (S189, S190): fleet age, freight rates, monthly OEM volumes, pre-buying before regulatory changes; financiers move with the truck cycle (S191). Hotels: demand outpaces supply, construction delay limits new rooms, pricing power, rising occupancy and rates, operating leverage (S294). Six-parameter hotel filter (S329): operating leverage, iconic assets, can occupancy rise, can ARR rise, debt, when new rooms and capex arrive. Ownership structures: owned, leased, managed, franchise (S327).
- **When to use:** Replacement-cycle and long-build industries.
- **Data it needs:** Fleet age, freight index, OEM volumes, occupancy and ARR, supply pipeline, debt.
- **How to score (guidance):** EXAMPLES: the commercial-vehicle cycle is "12 to 18 months cycle then three four years down cycle"; a fleet older than 8 to 10 years triggers replacement; pre-buying compresses two years of demand into six to eight months (S189, CV 00:18:40 and 00:26:44). RevPAR is occupancy times ARR; at 80% occupancy and Rs. 10,000 ARR it is Rs. 8,000 (S326). Hotels are valued on EV/EBITDA, with ESOP cost adjusted out (S329). PERISHABLE: hotel demand growth of 8 to 10% against a 4 to 6 year supply lag (S243).
- **Quote:** "typically when the fleet age becomes more than 8 to 10 years old that triggers the replacement cycle" (S189, CV 00:18:40)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S189, S190, S191, S243, S294, S325, S326, S327, S328, S329, S330)

## 63. Special situations, self-help, speed breaker and pessimism approaches
- **What it is:** Four ways the course frames a mispricing around an event or a hated sector (S113, S116, S231).

## 64. Bombed-out IPO framework

- **What it is:** Reading a business that looks bad on the P&L but is repairing underneath (S293, S006).
