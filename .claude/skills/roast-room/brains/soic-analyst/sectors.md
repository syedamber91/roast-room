## 1. How the course reads any sector

**Order of work.** Map the whole chain first, judge companies second. The course's five-step value chain drill, taught in S254 (note dated 2026-07-28), is: map who gets paid at every step, find who gets the right to win, compare the profit pool peer to peer, find the sources of competitive advantage, find unique businesses. In its words: "who gets paid at every step" (S254, HIGHLA 00:01:27). The method loop in S007 (2026-07-14) repeats four steps for every industry: pull peer margins for each node, do the same for every named peer in the node, compare nodes to find where money concentrates, check whether the sub-industry is growing (S007, P1 00:20). Taught in S005, S007, S254, S078 (4 sources).

**Where the money sits.** Branded, asset-light nodes closest to the consumer with pricing power earn most. Technically differentiated recurring-revenue nodes (water chemicals, aviation parts, speciality API) earn more than one-time capex nodes (EPC, trading, generic manufacturing). Government-protected commodity nodes can earn abnormal margins that are not structural (S007, section 2). Reduce each industry to 3 to 5 trackable KPIs (S007, P3 01:00), or 4 to 5 (S005, section 2.2); two sources, two counts, both stated.

**Node quality.** At each node ask whether the listed Indian company does IP-led work or commodity, EPC or distribution work. Frothy multiples on a commodity node are a trap. PERISHABLE example in S005: Indian data-center proxies at 100 to 300x P/E against 20 to 36x for international technology leaders (S005, section 2.4; S007, P2).

**Cyclical or durable.** Pull 10 to 11 years of margin data across all peers. If peers move in lockstep it is an industry cycle, not execution. Split growth into price times volume every time. Cycles run about 4 to 6 quarters and commodity down-cycles can last a decade (S005, section 3.1). The cycle length follows how long supply takes to build: "So there are two types of cycles. One is long dated cycle and second is a short dated cycle" (S326, HOTEL 00:00:54). Hotels take 3 to 5 years to build; an ethanol plant "Maximum 6 to 9 months" (S326, HOTEL 00:00:54). Power, hotels, hospitals and real estate share long cycles (S326, HOTEL 00:04:00). The most common analyst mistake named is reading a cyclical tailwind as structural (S007, section 6).

**Capital cycle.** Five steps: rising prospects bring competition; returns fall below cost of capital; weak players exit and investors turn pessimistic; supply goes cold; returns rise above cost of capital and the share price outperforms (S375, REAL 00:11:21). Start from supply, not demand: "I've seen gluts not followed by shortages, but I've never seen a shortage not followed by a glut." (S427, TVGPD 00:31:26). Taught in S375, S427 (2 sources).

**Margin-source test.** Ask whether each fat margin is structural or policy or cycle. "Policy/cycle margins are NOT moats." (S005, section 4). Example, PERISHABLE: solar cells at 56 to 60 percent EBITDA exist only because of anti-dumping duty (S005, section 4). A cheap P/E on peak-cycle earnings is a value trap (S005, section 4); the course's cyclical study rule runs the other way: "When their P/E ratio is getting higher, that is the time to study these companies" (S007, P3 00:41), a taught rule for cyclicals with depressed trough earnings.

**Moats.** Named sources in the lecture list: switching cost, scale, property assets, learning curve, license, brand power, early mover, customer lock-in, network effect, becoming a verb, location, lowest cost, integration, technical know-how (S179, MOATA 00:36:02). Rule of one hand: "if I can count more than five competitors on my fingers for a sector, that is a sector which I don't want to invest in" (S179, MOATA 00:21:20). "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all" (S179, MOATA 00:22:56). Three-point check: market dominance, a niche and unique company, higher margin than peers (S180). "moat without growth also becomes a value trap." (S180, MOATB 01:02:49). The course says the moat framework is not formulaic and rejects rigid rules such as a required 50 percent ROC (S006, section 6).

**Scalability.** The course's own mechanical screen is quarterly sales growth above 15 percent AND PAT growth above 20 percent, with P/E below 50, or tighter below 40 (S005, S006, S228, S522, S525, S506; 6 sources). Seven growth drivers: self-help turnaround, share gain via bolt-on acquisition, new-category creation, under-penetration with a decade tailwind, capacity-led scale, acquisition-led capability infill, regulatory tailwind (S006, section 2). Execution timelines for an order differ by sector. S232 (2026-07-28): defence electronics or rail electrification six to twelve months, road corridors three to four years, shipbuilding seven to ten years. S006 (2026-07-14): electrification 6 to 9 months, a road corridor or ship order 3 to 10 years. S524 (2026-07-14): six to nine months against three to four years. All three are stated; none is a rule. "You can't be long term in a down cycle in an order book driven business" (S232, IDENTA 01:34:39).

**Four phases of a theme.** Emerging, established, re-established, mature; use the phase to set growth expectations (S233, IDENTA 00:53:25). Examples at the lesson date, PERISHABLE: emerging, quick commerce and CDMO; established, hospitals and NBFCs; re-established, hotels after COVID; mature, refiners and large IT (S233).

**Turnarounds and TVGP.** Theme, Value, Growth, Promoter, with risks first: "But before we start this analysis, we'll start with the key risk in this turnaround because the understanding risk is really, really important" (S298, SPOTT 00:04:44). Five turnaround patterns: one-off costs end, cash flow healthy despite accounting losses, debt falls through asset sales or fresh capital, industry cycle turns, operating leverage and acquisitions (S293, SPOTT 00:01:33). Valuation is ballpark: "if the business looks reasonable in ballpark numbers, that's the type of valuation I like to do" (S006, TURN L165-183). Taught in S006, S293, S297, S298, S505, S528 (6 sources).

**Which valuation lens fits which sector (F24 routing, S001).** Banks and financials to price to book; asset-heavy (hotels, hospitals, QSR, telecom) to EV/EBITDA; cement to EV per ton or EV/EBITDA; real estate to market cap over pre-sales or imputed EBITDA; asset-light IT and FMCG to P/E and cash flow; deeply cyclical (auto ancillaries, chemical makers) to price to sales or price to book; life insurance to price to embedded value; contractors to market cap to order book, P/E or price to cash flow (S001 F24; S446). The course adds the limit: "Any valuation that you do will be incorrect" (S446, CLASSF 01:03:06). Sector EV/EBITDA bands in S001 (hospitals about 30x expensive, below 20x cheaper; cement ceiling about 21x) are dated calibration, PERISHABLE, not durable thresholds.

**Limits the course states on itself.** "This is not a webinar for entry-exit criteria or valuations" (S007, section 5): value chains teach how to look, not when to buy. "Nothing is a buy sell recommendation" (S155, NUKE 00:00:10). The method refuses to opine on sectors not studied: edible oil, fertiliser and downstream oil and gas (S005, dated 2026-07-14); the later oil and gas module (S309, dated 2026-07-26) does cover refining and marketing, so the newer note prevails for refining. Numbers are lecture-date snapshots and some are marked approximate by the notes themselves (S006).

### Hotels (hotel module, note dated 2026-07-26; turnaround lessons dated 2026-07-28; sources S294 to S298, S325 to S330)
- **Value chain.** Owned, leased, managed and franchised hotels (S327). Revenue is rooms 60 percent, food and beverage 33 percent and rising, other 7 percent (S330, HOTEL 00:12:29). EXAMPLE.
- **Watch.** Occupancy, average room rate (ARR), RevPAR (average occupancy times ARR), debt, new rooms and the capex plan. The course ranks hotels on six parameters: operating leverage, iconic assets, can occupancy rise, can ARR rise, debt, new rooms (S329, HOTEL 01:07:11). Value on EV/EBITDA, not P/E, because assets are heavy (S329, HOTEL 01:14:22). Preferred return metric is ROE, after adjusting ROC for lease expense (S329, HOTEL 02:09:40; HOTEL 02:09:34).
- **Cycle logic.** A local supply business with a long cycle. A demand-supply gap lifts rates; build takes 3 to 4 years: "three to four years to build a 4-star or 5-star hotel" (S294, SPOTT 00:11:56). Build time runs from about 51 months (upper scale, "basically 4 years to build a hotel", S328, HOTEL 00:23:34) down to 30 months for budget; luxury costs "1.9 crore per room to create" (S328, HOTEL 00:24:20), PERISHABLE. Cycles last "minimum 3-5 years" (S326, HOTEL 00:30:57).
- **Thresholds.** EXAMPLE: "real operating leverage kicks in when you go from 65-67% occupancy to 78-80% or even 85%" (S330, HOTEL 00:40:00). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: average occupancy 67 to 68 percent; 7 to 8 percent price rises are sustainable, a 20 to 25 percent hike risks guests leaving (S328, HOTEL 00:34:56; S330, HOTEL 00:51:05). Exit multiple assumption 18 to 20 times EV/EBITDA; similar assets at 15 to 17 times (S325, HOTEL 02:01:36), PERISHABLE.
- **Risks.** "Hotel sector is hospital sector on steroids either on the way up or on the way down" (S329, HOTEL 01:59:22). Debt in a cyclical sector. Private-equity overhang. A new hotel carries pre-operating cost, so near-term operating leverage is missing (S325, HOTEL 01:41:13).
- **What would change the view.** Supply growth overtaking demand growth; the cycle's last down leg followed an oversupply of new rooms (S294). The course adds a technical overlay: "30 weekly EMA do not forget it" (S329, HOTEL 01:59:45).

### Banks, small finance banks, NBFCs and cards (banking module, note dated 2026-07-26; sources S332 to S339, S426)
- **Value chain.** Large private banks, old private banks, small finance banks (SFBs) and public sector banks. Large private banks are the lowest cost manufacturers through high CASA (S339, DECODA 00:04:57). SFBs combine "origination muscle of an NBFC and cost of funds of a bank" (S339, DECODA 00:14:13).
- **Watch.** The ROA tree: interest income less interest expense gives net interest income; add other income; subtract operating expense for pre-provision profit; subtract provisions for profit before tax (S334, DECODC 00:06:02). Net interest margin works like gross margin; cost to income falling signals operating leverage (S334); slippage ratio is fresh NPAs over standard advances; GNPA means "greater than 90 days" (S334, DECODC 00:20:21). For NBFCs a 20-point checklist in five steps: business model, liabilities, assets, profitability and risk metrics, valuation (S335, DECODF 02:59:09). Lenders value on price to book (S001 F24). A price to book near 0.5x is usually not a bargain for a lender because it reflects weak asset quality (S001).
- **Cycle logic.** The asset-quality cycle: GNPA peaked at 13.4 percent in 2018 against a low of 2.3 percent (S339, DECODA 00:30:37), PERISHABLE. Value unlocks when recoveries exceed slippages (S332, DECODE 00:03:04). Old private banks are "cyclical institutions" with a viable entry only in a benign credit cycle (S339, DECODA 00:08:49). Large private banks with ROA above 1.8 percent and 15 to 20 percent loan growth are "compounder type institutions" (S339, DECODA 00:05:48). Four value-creation steps: retail and secured mix, better asset quality, operating leverage, retail deposits (S336, DECODB 00:08:13).
- **Risks.** Unsecured microfinance: "deeply cyclical industry" (S333, DECODA 00:19:37). Asset-liability mismatch (DHFL is the named failure, S335). Credit cards: transactors are about 35 to 40 percent of a book, revolvers pay about 42 percent interest, and NIM can look high before NPAs spike (S338, DECODF 00:55:24; 00:55:54; 00:56:59). PERISHABLE.
- **What would change the view.** Technical exit: a volatility stop or a break of the 30-week average on high volume (S332, DECODE 00:06:53). A smaller bank growing 10 to 12 percent against 18 to 20 percent for the leaders, and not cheap, needs no further look (S336, DECODB 00:21:07).
- **Thresholds.** RULE from the regulator: priority sector lending is 75 percent of SFB advances (S339, DECODA 00:19:52). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: PSU leverage 14x to 16x (S332, DECODE 00:19:47); South Indian Bank price to book 0.61 (S332, DECODE 00:18:30); SFB cost to income "usually north of 60%" (S333, DECODA 00:20:16).
- **Quote.** "lending is a business of leverage" (S336, DECODB 00:24:52).

### Insurance (insurance module, note dated 2026-07-26; sources S377 to S384)
- **Value chain.** Premium, reinsurance, net premium earned, float, claims. General insurance is motor (own damage and third party) and health (retail, group, government) (S384). Life insurance sells through banks, agents and others; bancassurance is "a tie up between an insurance company and a bank" (S379, UNDERB2 01:11:14).
- **Watch.** General: combined ratio (below 100 percent is operating profit), reserving triangle, float quality. Life: solvency, persistency, complaint and total cost ratios (S380); VNB margin and embedded value (S381, S382). Value life insurers on "price to embedded value" (S382, UNDERB2 02:53:56).
- **Moat or cycle logic.** Float: "Most of the companies except one or two few make bulk of the money on your float income rather than underwriting profit" (S378, UNDERA2 01:54:52). Bank brand and trust protect distribution: "trust is an important factor." (S379, UNDERB2 01:15:41). Motor is "a highly cyclic industry" (S384, UNDERA2 02:46:57).
- **Risks.** Open architecture lets banks sell any insurer's product (S379, UNDERB2 01:35:55). Prolonged low rates hit guarantee products (S381, UNDERB2 03:04:10). Aggressive accounting and pre-IPO assumption changes are red flags (S383, UNDERB2 02:59:32). A low claims ratio "may not be always good" (S380, UNDERA2 01:57:30).
- **What would change the view.** A history of negative deficiency in the triangle is the green flag; under-provisioning is the red flag (S377, UNDERA2 02:12:24). Product ban risk on credit protect plans (S381). A tax rise from 12.5 to 25 percent costs "15% on their embedded value" (S382, UNDERB2 03:05:10), EXAMPLE.
- **Thresholds.** RULE from the regulator: solvency "at least a solvency ratio of 1.5 plus" (S380, UNDERA2 01:55:23). Market practice: insurers raise funds "whenever it dips below 200%" (S380, UNDERB2 02:07:02). First-year commission about 18.5 percent (S380, UNDERB2 02:08:33), PERISHABLE. ULIP margin lowest, protection highest (S381).

### Gold NBFCs (gold-loan module, note dated 2026-07-26; sources S386 to S397)
- **Value chain.** Borrower pledges gold, NBFC lends against it; funding from banks, bonds and deposits. Customers are "generally rejected for loan by the banks because there is less data available for them or the credit score is bad" (S386, GOLDA 00:19:52).
- **Watch.** Loan to value (LTV), gold price, ticket size, branch count, online gold share, gross NPA, diversification into microfinance. Value on P/E against P/B (S388).
- **Moat or cycle logic.** Specialisation against banks: "it is what they make money from, and it is their cash cow, and they want to grow there." (S395, GOLDA 00:25:42). Versus moneylenders: "NBFCs are way ahead" (S396, GOLDA 00:33:24).
- **Risks.** A gold price fall. Microfinance diversification: "it seems that the market is punishing Manopura [Manappuram] for aggressively diversifying into fragile... business that is MFI and moving away from anti fragile that is gold business" (S388, GOLDB 01:00:42), in the lecturer's own opinion.
- **Thresholds.** RULE (S001 F12, S002): loan to value at or below 75 percent. The interest is outside the LTV, EXAMPLE: "Interest, so interest is not included in the LTV, remember that." (S391, GOLDB 00:15:00). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: lenders give about 59 to 60 percent in practice (S391, GOLDB 00:14:34); "we can expect a 10% fall in period of one year" (S391, GOLDB 00:15:48); gross NPA standard about 2.2 (S391, GOLDB 00:18:45).

### Hospitals (hospital module, note dated 2026-07-26; sources S350 to S355)
- **Value chain.** Premium tier-1 chains in metros (tertiary and quaternary care, specialists, brownfield expansion); regional and affordable models (clusters, owned or perpetual-lease land, doctor equity) (S354, S355).
- **Watch.** Occupancy, average length of stay, average revenue per occupied bed (ARPOB), capex trend against ROC, payer mix, EBITDA per bed. "If a bed goes vacant for the night, then that capacity is perishable" (S351, SOICA 00:48:33). Value on EV/EBITDA; the course notes building depreciation is overstated because hospital buildings last 20 to 40 years (S091).
- **Moat or cycle logic.** Capex cycle: greenfield spend depresses margins and returns, maturation lifts them, a capex halt releases cash (S351, S350). "Now KPEX starts declining. Your ROC starts expanding" (S351, SOICB 00:06:23). Land: "moat, land, land and land" (S354, SOICB 00:39:12). Demand: "Demand is never an issue in this sector" (S352, SOICA 00:15:52).
- **Risks.** Price caps (the 2017 cardiac stent cap, S351, S353); oncology overcapacity (S350, SOICC 00:31:22); key-doctor exit (S352); related-party transactions and a slump sale (S354, SOICD 00:14:43); government scheme margins eroded by wage and consumable inflation (S355, SOICC 00:42:43); affordability for premium tiers (S354).
- **What would change the view.** The course's exit cue: when hospitals run out of land and restart greenfield, "that is the time to basically get out of the sector" (S351, SOICB 00:11:59). At the lesson date it said valuations "are not favorable for Apollo" (S354, SOICD 00:15:14), PERISHABLE. An undisciplined capex name stays cheap for a reason (S350).
- **Thresholds.** EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: capex split equipment 30 to 40 percent, land and building 35 to 40 percent (S351, SOICB 00:04:22); the instructor's own estimate of capex against stock returns "70 to 80% correlated" (S351, SOICB 00:09:58); EV/EBITDA about 30x expensive and below 20x cheaper (S001 F24).

### Real estate (real estate module, note dated 2026-07-26; sources S370 to S375)
- **Value chain.** Land, approvals, construction funded by customer advances, delivery. Branded builders and asset-light joint development with landowners: "construction charges are paid by the builder" model where the landowner keeps land and a revenue share (S370). EXAMPLE: "26% of the top line is shared with the landlord" (S370, REAL 01:42:42).
- **Watch.** Pre-sales ("sales that are made before the project is completed", S371, REAL 00:59:08), average realisation, equivalent area constructed, collection efficiency, inventory quarters, advances (S371, S372). P/E is not suitable: "P ratio is not like, is not a relevant metric" (S371, REAL 00:43:00), because profit shows projects delivered 3 or 4 years ago (S371, REAL 00:49:28). Use EV over projected pre-sales under bear, base and bull cases (S374, REAL 02:38:25).
- **Moat or cycle logic.** Capital cycle plus consolidation: demonetisation, RERA and the NBFC liquidity crisis purged weak supply and moved capital to branded builders (S372, REAL 00:25:44). "gruesome business, which sometimes turns into a great business because the cycle has turned" (S372, REAL 02:44:57). Brand matters: "no customer will today come up and give you booking amount" (S370, REAL 00:33:52).
- **Risks.** Net asset value and sum-of-the-parts break if one or two projects slip (S374, REAL 02:43:50). Execution, dispute, discounts. Special situations such as a reverse merger carry promoter reclassification risk (S373, REAL 01:08:20).
- **What would change the view.** A return to oversupply and falling returns under the capital cycle (S375). Asset-light share falling (S374).
- **Thresholds.** RULE (RERA): "70% of customer advances to be maintained in escrow account" (S371, REAL 00:36:44). EXAMPLE, PERISHABLE: 80 percent of one builder's launches through asset-light development (S374, REAL 02:15:08).

Market-linked platforms lose earnings in a bear market (S412, MODERE 00:19:24). Wealth management is "people's business" (S270, THEC 00:36:44).

### Speciality chemicals (speciality chemical module, note dated 2026-07-26; sources S417 to S424, S518)
- **Value chain.** Feedstock, bulk chemical, intermediate, technical grade or active ingredient, formulation. "very high in volume and low in value" at the bulk end (S423, UNDERA 00:12:29). Most of the supply chain shift is in the intermediate space (S423, UNDERA 00:13:44).
- **Watch.** Number of makers, product count, working capital, ROC direction, customer tenure, compliance (look for zero liquid discharge, S418, UNDERD 00:42:45). The Five Bucket framework sorts by ROC and earnings growth, not multiple; "quality should invariably be the first filter" (S422, UNDERC 00:47:16). Bucket 1 is growth above 15 to 20 percent with ROC above 20 to 25 percent (S422, UNDERD 00:00:20), the working bar the instructor states; the others are described in words.
- **Moat or cycle logic.** Hazard and technology barriers: "very high entry barrier business" (S417, UNDERC 00:26:57). China Plus One: an environmental crackdown removed China's low-cost edge and MNCs reroute supply (S421, UNDERA 00:22:40). Niche chemistry plus integration lifts margin through mix (S518, S508). Companies need the "capacity to suffer" in low-margin bulk before integrating forward (S423, UNDERB 00:01:44).
- **Risks.** "shallow cyclical industry" (S418, UNDERD 00:32:29): fire, structure change, end-user slowdown, imports from China. Margin spikes from upstream shocks are not structure: "artificial expansion in margins" (S420, UNDERD 00:11:48), EXAMPLE, an amines operating margin from about 18 to almost 33 percent. Bucket 5 is "gruesome business" (S422, UNDERD 00:02:14).
