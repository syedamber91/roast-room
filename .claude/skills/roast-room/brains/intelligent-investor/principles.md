1. **Never overpay: the margin of safety, the gap between value and price, is the central test of every decision.** Graham, with Zweig. "we venture the motto, MARGIN OF SAFETY" (S020) [full: S020#framework-1]. "By refusing to pay too much for an investment, you minimize the chances that your wealth will ever disappear or suddenly be destroyed." (C020) [full: C020#takeaway-1]. Also S011#framework-7, S014#rule-23, X-preface#rule-3. Sections: 5 (S011, S014, S020, C020, X-preface). Confidence: high.

2. **The margin of safety exists only at a price; the same security can be safe at one price and unsafe at a higher one.** Graham. "The margin of safety is always dependent on the price paid." (S020) [full: S020#takeaway-3]. Zweig: "At some point in its life, almost every stock is a bargain; at another time, it will be expensive." (C018) [full: C018#rule-4]. Also S008#warning-10. Sections: 3 (S008, S020, C018). Confidence: high.

3. **Call an operation an investment only if thorough analysis promises safety of principal and an adequate return; everything else is speculation.** Graham, restated by Zweig. "Operations not meeting these requirements are speculative." (S001) [full: S001#framework-1]. Zweig splits it into three requirements: analysis, protection against serious loss, adequate aims [full: C001#rule-1] [full: C001#rule-2] [full: C001#rule-3]. Also S020#framework-6. Sections: 3 (S001, S020, C001). Confidence: high.

4. **Keep any speculation small and completely separate from the investment account.** Graham and Zweig. "Never mingle your speculative and investment operations in the same account, nor in any part of your thinking." (S001) [full: S001#warning-5]. Zweig puts the ceiling at 10% of overall wealth [full: C001#rule-7]. Also S008#rule-1. Sections: 3 (S001, S008, C001). Confidence: high.

5. **Choose your role first: most people should be defensive investors, and only those with real knowledge and time should be enterprising.** Graham. "The majority of security owners should elect the defensive classification." (S007) [full: S007#rule-9]. Zweig describes the active route as one that "takes lots of time and loads of energy" (C004) [full: C004#framework-1]. Also S004#rule-11, S007#rule-25, S010#takeaway-6. Sections: 4 (S004, S007, S010, C004). Confidence: high.

6. **Keep stocks between 25% and 75% of the fund, starting from 50-50, and lean toward caution when the market looks high.** Graham, kept by Zweig. "never less than 25% or more than 75%" (S001) [full: S001#rule-1]. Zweig: "Graham insists that everyone should keep a minimum of 25% in bonds" (C004) [full: C004#rule-2]. 7. **Act on price against value, never on forecasts of where the market is going.** Graham. "We are convinced that the average investor cannot deal successfully with price movements by endeavoring to forecast them." (S008) [full: S008#takeaway-2]. Graham: "The intelligent investor excels by making decisions that are not dependent on the accuracy of anybody's forecasts" (S011) [full: S011#takeaway-5]; the store marks this Graham, but its present-tense wording may be Zweig's footnote (unchecked). Also S001#takeaway-4. Sections: 3 (S001, S008, S011). Confidence: high.

8. **Treat the market price as an offer you may take or ignore; a fall in price is not a loss unless the business's value has shrunk.** Graham and Zweig. "He should always remember that market quotations are there for his convenience, either to be taken advantage of or to be ignored." (S008) [full: S008#rule-18]. Zweig: "You do not have to trade with him just because he constantly begs you to." (C008) [full: C008#rule-1]. Also S008#rule-7, C001#rule-5. Sections: 3 (S008, C001, C008). Confidence: high.

9. **Never buy a stock because it has risen or sell one because it has fallen.** Graham, echoed by Zweig. "He should never buy a stock because it has gone up or sell one because it has gone down." (S008) [full: S008#rule-11]. Zweig: "The intelligent investor never dumps a stock purely because its share price has fallen" (C001) [full: C001#rule-5]. Sections: 2 (S008, C001). Confidence: medium.

10. **Expect large price swings in everything you own and be ready for them in money and in nerve.** Graham. "The investor should know about these possibilities and should be prepared for them both financially and psychologically." (S008) [full: S008#takeaway-10]. 11. **A defensive investor buys only large, prominent, conservatively financed companies with long records of earnings and dividends.** Graham, with Zweig's updates. "The defensive investor must confine himself to the shares of important companies with a long record of profitable operations and in strong financial condition." (S001) [full: S001#rule-6]. 12. **Pay a moderate multiple of average earnings, not of one good year, and a moderate price against book value.** Graham, restated by Zweig. "Current price should not be more than 15 times average earnings of the past three years." (S014) [full: S014#rule-4]. Zweig: "Notice that Graham insists on calculating the price/earnings ratio based on a multiyear average of past earnings." Sections: 5 (S005, S007, S013, S014, C014). Confidence: high.

13. **Diversify adequately: the margin of safety works on a group, so a defensive portfolio holds roughly ten to thirty issues and a bargain portfolio many more.** Graham, kept by Zweig. "If such a margin is present in each of a diversified list of twenty or more stocks, the probability of a favorable result under" (S020) fairly normal conditions becomes very large [full: S020#rule-3]. Zweig: "Graham's guideline of owning between 10 and 30 stocks remains a good starting point" (C005) [full: C005#rule-1]. Also S005#rule-1, S015#rule-2, S020#framework-5, C020#takeaway-3. Sections: 5 (S005, S015, S020, C005, C020). Confidence: high.

14. **For the defensive investor, diversification matters more than clever selection.** Graham. "Let him emphasize diversification more than individual selection." (S014) [full: S014#rule-14]. Also S001#takeaway-6, S011#framework-6. Sections: 3 (S001, S011, S014). Confidence: high.

15. **An enterprising investor's method must be both sound and unpopular; following the crowd cannot beat the crowd.** Graham. "It must meet objective or rational tests of underlying soundness; and (2) it must be different from the policy followed by most investors or speculators." (S007) [full: S007#framework-1]. Also S001#rule-8, S015#takeaway-3. Zweig: managers who succeed are "disciplined and consistent, refusing to change their approach even when it is unfashionable" (C015) [full: C015#rule-9]. Sections: 4 (S001, S007, S015, C015). Confidence: high.

16. **A bargain needs a wide gap: value well above price, shown by figures.** Graham. a true bargain needs "the indicated value is at least 50% more than the price" (S007) [full: S007#rule-5]; secondary stocks at "two-thirds or less of their indicated value" (S020) [full: S020#rule-9]. Also S007#rule-14, S015#rule-1, S020#takeaway-5. Sections: 3 (S007, S015, S020). Confidence: high.

17. **The cheapest identifiable bargain is a stock below its net current assets after all debts, bought as a diversified group.** Graham, with Zweig. 18. **Do not pay up for growth: growth stocks are too uncertain for the defensive investor, and a high multiple leaves no margin.** Graham, with Zweig. 19. **Estimates of the future should err on the low side, and valuations of fast growers are not dependable.** Graham. 20. **Never buy a bond or preferred for its yield alone; buy safety, or buy inferior issues only at deep discounts.** Graham. 21. **Judge a bond by how many times earnings covered its fixed charges over years, including the poorest year.** Graham, with Zweig. 24. **Act as an owner: hold management to a fair payout or proof that retained profits raised per-share earnings.** Graham, with Zweig. 25. **Value a holding from the company's reports, not from the ticker.** Graham. 27. **Most investors should own a low-cost index fund as the core and pick stocks only with a small slice.** Zweig.
