# Commentary: The Intelligent Investor (Graham, with Zweig's commentary)

Built from 2,291 verified items in the private store (roast-room-private/intelligent-investor); 765 of them come from Jason Zweig's commentary (C001 to C020). Every quote below is Zweig's, not Graham's. Zweig wrote around 2002 to 2003, so his figures and products (funds, fees, tax rules, websites) are dated too. Each block says where Zweig agrees with Graham, where he updates him for modern markets, and where he softens or departs from him.

## C001: Investment versus speculation
- **Agrees.** Zweig keeps Graham's line: "Confusing speculation with investment, Graham warns, is always a mistake." (C001) [full: C001#warning-3]
- **Restates it simply.** "An investor calculates what a stock is worth, based on the value of its businesses." (C001) [full: C001#takeaway-4] and "A speculator gambles that a stock will go up in price because somebody else will pay even more for it." (C001) [full: C001#takeaway-5]
- **Updates.** Day trading, the January effect and formula funds of the 1990s, which he says failed Graham's three criteria. [full: C001#framework-3] [full: C001#example-6]
- **Adds.** "Wall Street perennially downplays the durable virtues of investing and hypes the gaudy appeal of speculation." (C001) [full: C001#warning-14]

## C002: Inflation
- **Agrees.** Stocks are not a perfect inflation hedge: "When inflation shot above 6%, as in the years on the right end of the graph, stocks also stank." (C002) [full: C002#takeaway-4]
- **Updates.** Instruments Graham did not have: TIPS and REITs. "TIPS are the ideal substitute for the proportion of your retirement funds you would otherwise keep in cash." (C002) [full: C002#takeaway-2] [full: C002#takeaway-6]
- **Adds.** Measure success "not just by what you make, but by how much you keep after inflation." (C002) [full: C002#rule-1]

## C003: Market history and forecasts
- **Agrees.** "The intelligent investor must never forecast the future exclusively by extrapolating the past." (C003) [full: C003#warning-9]
- **Updates.** The 2000 to 2002 crash, which erased $7.41 trillion, and the strategists who said high prices did not add risk. [full: C003#example-4] [full: C003#example-1] [full: C003#example-2]
- **Challenges a modern claim, not Graham.** On the long-run record he writes: "In the real world, then, stocks did no better than cash and bonds" (C003) [full: C003#takeaway-1]
- **Tempers expectations.** "Compared to the 1990s, 6% is chicken feed." (C003) [full: C003#takeaway-3]

## C004: Portfolio policy
- **Agrees.** He keeps Graham's floor: "Graham insists that everyone should keep a minimum of 25% in bonds." (C004) [full: C004#rule-2] [full: C004#rule-3]

## C005: The defensive investor and common stocks
- **Agrees, with a caveat.** "Graham's guideline of owning between 10 and 30 stocks remains a good starting point for investors who want to pick their own stocks" (C005) [full: C005#rule-1]
- **Updates.** Index funds, automatic monthly buying and the autopilot portfolio. [full: C005#rule-3] [full: C005#method-3] [full: C005#example-5]

## C006: The enterprising investor, what not to do

## C007: The enterprising investor, what to do
- **Agrees.** On price: "A great company is not a great investment if you pay too much for the stock." (C007) [full: C007#takeaway-2]

## C008: Market fluctuations
- **Agrees.** Mr. Market: "Mr. Market's job is to provide you with prices; your job is to decide whether it is to your advantage to act on them." (C008) [full: C008#rule-1]
- **Updates.** Psychology research: "the pain of financial loss is more than twice as intense as the pleasure of an equivalent gain" (C008) [full: C008#takeaway-5]. He adds a signed investment owner's contract. [full: C008#method-3]
- **Qualifies the metaphor.** "Most of the time, the market is mostly accurate in pricing most stocks." (C008) [full: C008#rule-11]
- **Reframes the goal.** "the whole point of investing is not to earn more money than average, but to earn enough money to meet your own needs" (C008) [full: C008#takeaway-4]

## C009: Investment funds
- **Agrees and goes further.** "Late in his life, Graham praised index funds as the best choice for individual investors, as does Warren Buffett." (C009) [full: C009#example-3]

## C011: Security analysis for the lay investor
- **Updates Graham's tests with modern ones.** Serial acquirers: "An average of more than two or three acquisitions a year is a sign of potential trouble." (C011) [full: C011#rule-1]. Companies hooked on outside financing. [full: C011#warning-1]
- **Adds bars of his own.** "in general, long-term debt should be under 50% of total capital." (C011) [full: C011#rule-4] [full: C011#rule-3]
- **Adds.** "In the long run, a company that spends nothing on R & D is at least as vulnerable as one that spends too much." (C011) [full: C011#takeaway-1]
- **On dividends.** "The burden of proof is on the company to show that you are better off if it does not pay a dividend." (C011) [full: C011#rule-7]

- **Agrees, and replaces stock picking.** "A low-cost index fund is the best tool ever created for low-maintenance stock investing" (C014) [full: C014#takeaway-1]
