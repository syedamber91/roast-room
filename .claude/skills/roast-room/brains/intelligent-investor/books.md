- **Earlier editions.** The introduction quotes a paragraph "that we included first in the 1949 edition of this" (X-introduction) [full: X-introduction#warning-5]. It also says earlier editions urged at least 25% of a conservative portfolio in stocks [full: X-introduction#rule-1].
- **Graham's text.** Its own figures date it to 1971 and 1972: it speaks of DJIA stocks "purchased at the late 1971 price level" (S002) [full: S002#rule-5], and of the margin of safety "now" in 1972 [full: S020#takeaway-2]. - **Zweig's commentary.** Written as of early 2003, for example "As of 2003, anything above 6.5% is implausible, while a rising rate is downright delusional." (C012) [full: C012#rule-4]. The revised edition keeps Graham's chapters and adds Zweig's commentary after each one: "The goal of this revised edition of The Intelligent Investor is to apply Graham’s ideas to today’s financial markets while leaving his text entirely" (X-preface) [full: X-preface#framework-5].
- **Footnotes.** Some items tagged as Graham's text read as later footnotes (for example a note calling one forecast "one of Graham’s rare misjudgments." (S002) [full: S002#warning-16]). Treat a dated, post-1972 remark as Zweig's note, not Graham's.

### Core thesis, in the seat's words

Investing is buying a share of a business at a price that leaves room for error. The investor's real enemy is his own behaviour, not the market. Two roads are open: a defensive road that needs little effort and gives average results, and an enterprising road that needs real work and may give a little more. On either road, price decides the outcome, forecasts are unreliable, and the margin of safety is the one idea that holds everything together (Chapter 20, [full: S020#framework-1]). Zweig's commentary keeps the thesis and updates the tools: index funds, fund costs, accounting tricks of the late 1990s, and investor psychology.

### What the book is not

- Not a forecasting guide: it argues against forecasting throughout [full: S011#warning-4] [full: C003#rule-6].
- Not current figures: its thresholds are 1972 United States numbers and Zweig's 2003 updates, not bars for an Indian market today.
- Not advice: it describes a method. This seat never tells anyone to buy, sell or hold anything.
