# Warnings: The Intelligent Investor (Graham, with Zweig's commentary)

Built from 2,291 verified items in the private store (roast-room-private/intelligent-investor); 780 of them are warnings. Below are the ones the book repeats most, grouped. Each line is labelled Graham (chapters S001 to S020 and the introduction) or Zweig (commentary C001 to C020). Dated figures are historical. A warning is a thing to check for, not a verdict on any company today.

## 1. Speculation and market timing
- **Graham.** The three classic mistakes: "(1) speculating when you think you are investing; (2) speculating seriously instead of as a pastime, when you lack proper knowledge and skill for it; and (3) risking more money in speculation than you can afford to lose." (S001) [full: S001#warning-3]
- **Graham.** "Never mingle your speculative and investment operations in the same account, nor in any part of your thinking." (S001) [full: S001#warning-5]
- **Graham.** "if he places his emphasis on timing, in the sense of forecasting, he will end up as a speculator" (S008) [full: S008#warning-2]
- **Graham.** Trading formulas stop working once they spread: "It demonstrates an inherent characteristic of forecasting and trading formulas in the fields of business and finance." (S008) [full: S008#warning-23]
- **Graham.** "it appears to be almost impossible to distinguish in advance between those individual forecasts which can be relied upon and those which are subject to a large chance of error" (S011) [full: S011#warning-11]
- **Graham, in the appendix.** "Whenever calculus is brought in, or higher algebra, you could take it as a warning signal that the operator was trying to substitute theory for experience" (X-appendixes) [full: X-appendixes#warning-14]
- **Zweig.** "In the financial markets, hindsight is forever 20/20, but foresight is legally blind." (C007) [full: C007#warning-1]
- **Zweig.** "Wall Street perennially downplays the durable virtues of investing and hypes the gaudy appeal of speculation." (C001) [full: C001#warning-14]

## 3. Overpaying for growth
- **Graham.** "we regard growth stocks as a whole as too uncertain and risky a vehicle for the defensive investor" (S005) [full: S005#warning-4]
- **Graham.** "The striking thing about growth stocks as a class is their tendency toward wide swings in market price" (S007) [full: S007#warning-6]
- **Graham.** "High valuations entail high risks." (S013) [full: S013#warning-3] and "It often proves much more difficult to continue to grow at a high rate after volume and profits have already expanded to big totals." (S013) [full: S013#warning-4]
- **Graham.** The danger lies in "the absence of an adequate factor of safety when too large a portion of the price must depend on ever-increasing earnings in the future." (S014) [full: S014#warning-1]
- **Graham.** "the market has a tendency to set prices that will not be adequately protected by a conservative projection of future earnings" (S020) [full: S020#warning-5]
- **Zweig.** "The bigger they get, the slower they grow." (C007) [full: C007#warning-2]
- **Zweig.** "it's nonsensical to derive a price/earnings ratio by dividing the known current price by unknown future earnings." (C014) [full: C014#warning-5]
- **Zweig.** "no company as big as Cisco had ever been able to grow fast enough to justify a price/earnings ratio above 60" (C018) [full: C018#warning-3]

## 4. Leverage, debt and weak coverage
- **Graham.** "a second-grade 5.5 or 6% bond selling at par is almost always a bad purchase." (S006) [full: S006#warning-4]
- **Graham.** On Penn Central: "The fact that the company paid no income taxes over so long a period should have raised serious questions about the validity of its reported earnings." (S017) [full: S017#warning-2] [full: S017#rule-1]
- **Graham.** On NVF: the market value of stock and warrants "was about $14 million against a bonded debt of $135 million" (S017) [full: S017#warning-12]
- **Graham.** Too little common stock against bonds and preferred can, in good conditions, "make for a huge speculative profit in the common." (S011) That is leverage, and it cuts both ways. [full: S011#warning-31]
- **Zweig.** Watch for a company that is "borrowing debt or selling stock to raise boatloads of Other People's Money." (C011) [full: C011#warning-1]
- **Zweig.** "WorldCom could cover those bond payments only by borrowing more money from banks." (C006) [full: C006#warning-12]
- **Preface (on Graham's life).** His mother, "borrowing money to trade stocks" (X-preface) on margin, was wiped out in 1907. [full: X-preface#example-3]
