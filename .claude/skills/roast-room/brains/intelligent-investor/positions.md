## Stocks against bonds
- **Stance:** keep both, never less than 25% or more than 75% in either.
- **Graham:** "the proportion held in bonds be never less than 25% or more than 75%, with the converse being necessarily true for the common-stock component" (S001) [full: S001#rule-1]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** keep the split you set and change it only when your life changes: "Once you set these target percentages, change them only as your life circumstances change. Do not buy more stocks because the stock market has gone up; do not sell them because it has gone down." (C004) [full: C004#rule-4]. Author: Zweig.
- **Agree:** yes. Zweig keeps Graham's band [full: C004#rule-3].

## Rebalancing
- **Stance:** restore a fixed split when it drifts, not when the market feels right.
- **Graham:** "Under this plan the guiding rule is to maintain as nearly as practicable an equal division between bond and stock holdings." (S004) [full: S004#rule-10]; the item records the triggers as 55% and 45% stocks. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "I suggest that you rebalance every six months, no more and no less, on easy-to-remember dates like New Year's and" (C004) [full: C004#method-2]. Author: Zweig.
- **Differ:** Graham rebalances on drift (about 5%, [full: S001#method-1]); Zweig on fixed dates.

## Age and allocation
- **Stance:** the book does not tie the split to age.
- **Graham:** no recorded position on age in his chapters. Zweig notes it: "The most striking thing about Graham’s discussion of how to allocate your assets between stocks and bonds is that he never mentions the word" (C004) [full: C004#takeaway-1], namely age. The introduction's commentary makes one exception for someone late in retirement [full: X-introduction#rule-7].
- **Zweig:** rejects the old rule of thumb: "A traditional rule of thumb was to subtract your age from 100 and invest that percentage of your assets in stocks, with the rest in bonds or cash." (C004) [full: C004#warning-2] [full: C004#warning-3]. Author: Zweig.

## Market timing
- **Stance:** price the market, do not time it.
- **Graham:** "We are convinced that the intelligent investor can derive satisfactory results from pricing of either type. We are equally sure that if he places his emphasis on timing, in the sense of forecasting, he will end up as a speculator" (S008) [full: S008#takeaway-1]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "In the financial markets, hindsight is forever 20/20, but foresight is legally blind." (C007) [full: C007#warning-1]. Author: Zweig.

## Forecasting
- **Stance:** distrust long forecasts, and make decisions that survive a wrong one.
- **Graham:** "The higher the growth rate you project, and the longer the future period over which you project it, the more sensitive your forecast becomes to the slightest error." (S011) [full: S011#warning-4]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "Staying humble about your forecasting powers, as Graham did, will keep you from risking too much on a view of the future that may well turn out to be wrong." (C003) [full: C003#rule-6]. Author: Zweig.

## Expected returns and the market level
- **Stance:** expect modest returns when the market is high; check earnings yield against bond yield.
- **Graham:** "the investor has no sound basis for expecting more than an average overall return of, say, 8% on a portfolio of DJIA-type common stocks" (S002) [full: S002#rule-5]. He also tracks "the ratio of stock returns (earnings/price) to bond returns has grown worse during the entire period" (S003) [full: S003#framework-5]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "In the long run, that means you can reasonably expect stocks to average roughly a 6% return (or 4% after inflation)." (C003) [full: C003#rule-2] He uses Shiller's ten-year earnings ratio [full: C003#method-1], and calls anything over 8% to 10% from an adviser unrealistic [full: C010#rule-8]. Author: Zweig.

## Number of holdings
- **Stance:** diversify, but not without limit.
- **Graham:** "There should be adequate though not excessive diversification. This might mean a minimum of ten different issues and a maximum of about thirty." (S005) [full: S005#rule-1] For margin-of-safety buying, he wants the margin present in "a diversified list of twenty or more stocks" (S020) [full: S020#rule-3]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "remains a good starting point for investors who want to pick their own stocks, but you must make sure that you are not overexposed to one industry." (C005) [full: C005#rule-1] Author: Zweig.

## Concentration
- **Stance:** one great holding can outweigh decades of work, yet the book still says diversify.
- **Postscript (items tag it "other"):** "the aggregate of profits accruing from this single investment decision far exceeded the sum of all the others realized through 20 years of wide-ranging operations" (X-postscript) [full: X-postscript#takeaway-1] And: "To keep investing from decaying into gambling, you must diversify." (X-postscript) [full: X-postscript#rule-1].
- **Zweig:** "No matter how confident we feel, there's no way to find out whether a stock will go up until after we buy it. Therefore" (C014) [full: C014#warning-2] [full: C007#warning-4]. Author: Zweig.

## How much to speculate
- **Stance:** keep speculation small and separate.
- **Graham:** "Never mingle your speculative and investment operations in the same account, nor in any part of your thinking." (S001) [full: S001#warning-5]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "For most of us, 10% of our overall wealth is the maximum permissible amount to put at speculative risk." (C001) [full: C001#rule-7]. Author: Zweig.
- **Differ:** Graham gives no percentage cap in these items; the 10% is Zweig's.

## IPOs and new issues
- **Stance:** stay out of new issues, above all late in a bull market.
- **Graham:** "Even if one or two can be found that can pass severe tests of quality and value, it is probably bad policy to get mixed up in this sort of" (S006) [full: S006#rule-8] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "From 1980 through 2001, if you had bought the average IPO at its first public closing price and held on for three years, you would have underperformed the market by more than 23 percentage points annually." (C006) [full: C006#warning-10] Author: Zweig.

## Growth stocks
- **Stance:** growth is real but usually overpriced; the price decides.
- **Graham:** "we regard growth stocks as a whole as too uncertain and risky a vehicle for the defensive" (S005) [full: S005#warning-4] [full: S007#warning-5]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "Growth stocks are worth buying when their prices are reasonable, but when their price/earnings ratios go much above 25 or 30 the odds get" (C007) [full: C007#rule-1]. Author: Zweig.
- **Differ:** Graham advises against the usual growth-stock commitment; Zweig says growth stocks are worth owning at a reasonable price, with odds turning bad above a P/E of 25 or 30.

## Unpopular companies
- **Stance:** large companies out of favour are a hunting ground.
- **Graham:** "The key requirement here is that the enterprising investor concentrate on the larger companies that are going through a period of unpopularity." (S007) [full: S007#framework-2]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "This kind of temporary unpopularity can create lasting wealth by enabling you to buy a great company at a good price." (C007) [full: C007#takeaway-3] [full: C014#takeaway-5]. Author: Zweig.

## Net-current-asset bargains
- **Stance:** a share priced below working capital less all debts is the clearest bargain.
- **Graham:** "The type of bargain issue that can be most readily identified is a common stock that sells for less than the company's net working capital alone, after deducting all prior obligations." (S007) [full: S007#rule-7] His firm's purchases were "made typically at two-thirds or less of such stripped-down asset value." (S015) [full: S015#rule-1] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** search the new-lows lists, then check net working capital against price [full: C007#method-1] [full: C007#method-2]. Author: Zweig.

## Mutual funds
- **Stance:** funds suit most people better than picking stocks; few beat the market.
- **Graham:** "the average individual who put his money exclusively in investment-fund shares in the past ten years has fared better than the average person who made his common-stock purchases directly" (S009) [full: S009#takeaway-2]. "All financial experience up to now indicates that large funds, soundly managed, can produce at best only slightly better than average results over the years." (S009) [full: S009#takeaway-6]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "the average fund does not pick stocks well enough to overcome its costs of researching and trading them" (C009) [full: C009#rule-17] [full: C009#warning-3]. Author: Zweig.

## Index funds
- **Stance (Zweig):** the best tool for a defensive investor.
- **Graham:** not in the extracted items as Graham's 1972 view. One item tagged Graham [full: S014#method-4] refers to the 2003 commentaries and reads as Zweig's note. Graham does tell the defensive investor: "Let him emphasize diversification more than individual selection." (S014) [full: S014#rule-14]
- **Zweig:** A low-cost index fund is the best tool for low-maintenance stock investing [full: C014#takeaway-1]. And: "Get yourself out of the stock-picking business and into an index fund, where you belong." (C014) [full: C014#rule-14]. Author: Zweig.

## Fund costs
- **Stance:** costs are the most predictable part of a fund's return.
- **Graham:** prefer no-load funds [full: S009#rule-1]; closed-end funds at a discount: "If you want to put money in investment funds, buy a group of closed-end shares at a discount of, say, 10% to 15% from asset value" (S009) [full: S009#rule-4]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "With operating expenses averaging 1.5%, and trading costs at around 2%, the typical fund has to beat the market by 3.5 percentage points per year before costs just to match" (C009) [full: C009#rule-5] Author: Zweig.

## Advisers
- **Stance:** use advice you understand; check the adviser before trusting.
- **Graham:** "Only in the exceptional case, where the integrity and competence of the advisers have been thoroughly demonstrated, should the investor act upon the advice of others without understanding and approving the decision made." (S010) [full: S010#rule-7] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "If fees will consume more than 1% of your assets annually, you should probably shop for another adviser." (C010) [full: C010#rule-7] [full: C010#framework-1]. Author: Zweig.

## Inflation
- **Stance:** stocks are a partial, uncertain shield; keep some of both.
- **Graham:** "There is no close time connection between inflationary (or deflationary) conditions and the movement of common-stock earnings and prices." (S002) [full: S002#rule-3]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "The stock market lost money in eight of the 14 years in which inflation exceeded 6%; the average return for those 14 years was a measly 2.6%." (C002) [full: C002#warning-5] He adds inflation-linked bonds [full: C002#rule-4]. Author: Zweig.

## Gold
- **Stance:** Graham saw no case for gold as a hedge for Americans in 1972.
- **Graham:** "The standard policy of people all over the world who mistrust their currency has been to buy and hold gold." (S002) [full: S002#warning-17] An item tagged Graham gives a 2% limit (5% over age 65) [full: S002#rule-9]; its wording reads as a later note. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** no recorded position.

## Borrowing to invest
- **Stance:** do not borrow to own securities.
- **Graham:** "No borrowing to buy or hold securities." (S003) [full: S003#rule-2]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** no recorded position on personal borrowing. For companies, he flags debt above half of capital [full: C011#rule-4].

## High-yield and second-grade bonds
- **Stance:** not at full price; only at a deep discount, if at all.
- **Graham:** "the ordinary investor is wiser to keep away from such high-yield bonds." (S004) [full: S004#warning-5] "it is mere common sense to abstain from buying securities at around 100 if long experience indicates that they can probably be bought at 70 or less" (S006) [full: S006#rule-4]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "Buying a bond only for its yield is like getting married only for the sex." (C006) [full: C006#warning-1] Cap the spicy part: "No sane investor would put more than 10% of a total bond portfolio in spicy holdings like these." (C006) [full: C006#rule-3]. Author: Zweig.

## Preferred stocks
- **Stance:** a weak form; only at a bargain.
- **Graham:** "Really good preferred stocks can and do exist, but they are good in spite of their investment form, which is an inherently bad one." (S004) [full: S004#warning-7] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "his bias against high-yield preferred stock remains valid, since there remains no cheap and widely available way to spread their risks" (C006) [full: C006#rule-2]. Author: Zweig.

## Convertibles
- **Stance:** no free lunch; judge them in falling markets.
- **Graham:** "you cannot by a mere ingenious device make a bargain much better for both sides. In exchange for the conversion privilege the investor usually gives up something important in quality or yield, or both." (S016) [full: S016#warning-1]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "For conservative investors with most or all of their assets in bonds, adding a diversified bundle of converts is a sensible way to seek stock-like returns without having to take the scary step of investing in stocks directly." (C016) [full: C016#method-1] Author: Zweig.
- **Differ:** Graham is mostly sceptical; Zweig sees a use for converts in a bond-heavy portfolio, through a cheap fund [full: C016#rule-1].

## Warrants and options
- **Stance:** warrants and large option grants dilute owners.
- **Graham:** "We consider the recent development of stock-option warrants as a near fraud, an existing menace, and a potential disaster." (S016) [full: S016#warning-11] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "the intelligent owner will vote against any executive compensation plan that uses option grants to turn more than 3% of the company's shares outstanding over to the managers." (C019) [full: C019#rule-3] Author: Zweig.

## Dividends
- **Stance:** ask for a payout, or proof that kept profits earn more.
- **Graham:** shareholders should ask for a normal payout of about two-thirds of earnings, or "a clear-cut demonstration that the reinvested profits have produced a satisfactory increase in per-share earnings" (S019) [full: S019#rule-1]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "Paying out a dividend does not guarantee great results, but it does improve the return of the typical stock by yanking at least some cash out of the managers' hands before they can either squander it or squirrel it away." (C019) [full: C019#takeaway-3] Never own a stock for dividend income alone [full: C004#rule-10]. Author: Zweig.

## Buybacks
- **Stance (Zweig):** good when shares are cheap, value-destroying when they are not.
- **Graham:** no recorded position on buybacks in his own text in the items.
- **Zweig:** "If the shares are cheap, then spending spare cash to repurchase them is an excellent use of the company’s capital." (C019) [full: C019#takeaway-9] Against that, Oracle: "Oracle issued the stock to insiders at an average price of $3.53 per share and repurchased it at an average price of $18.26. Sell low, buy high: Is" (C019) [full: C019#takeaway-4] Author: Zweig.

## Management
- **Stance:** judge management by results and alignment, not by talk.
- **Graham:** "Good managements produce a good average market price, and bad managements produce bad market prices." (S008) [full: S008#rule-13] The factor is most useful after a recent change [full: S011#rule-10]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "Executives should spend most of their time managing their company in private, not promoting it to the investing public." (C011) [full: C011#rule-5] "A manager can't legitimately be your partner if he keeps selling while you're buying." (C011) [full: C011#warning-9] Author: Zweig.

## Acquisitions
- **Stance:** growth bought through deals often reverses.
- **Graham:** "The more companies LTV acquired, the higher its stock went; the higher its stock went, the more companies it could afford to acquire." (S017) [full: S017#takeaway-1] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "An average of more than two or three acquisitions a year is a sign of potential trouble." (C011) [full: C011#rule-1] Author: Zweig.

## Reported earnings
- **Stance:** distrust one year's figure; read averages and footnotes.
- **Graham:** "Don't take a single year's earnings seriously." (S012) [full: S012#warning-1] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "As an intelligent investor, the only thing you should do with pro forma earnings is ignore them." (C012) [full: C012#rule-1] "Never buy a stock without reading the footnotes to the financial statements in the annual report." (C012) [full: C012#rule-5] Author: Zweig.

## Trading and day trading
- **Stance:** trading is not investing; the less turnover, the better.
- **Graham:** stock trading does not meet his definition of an investment [full: S001#warning-13]; neither do hot issues bought for a quick profit [full: S001#warning-11]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** day trading is "one of the best weapons ever invented for committing financial suicide." (C006) [full: C006#warning-4] [full: C005#rule-2]. Author: Zweig.

## Short selling
- **Stance:** a test of nerve and money even when right.
- **Graham:** "selling short a too popular and therefore overvalued issue is apt to be a test not only of one's courage and stamina but also of the depth of one's pocketbook" (S001) [full: S001#warning-17] He also warns against shorting good companies at high prices [full: S018#warning-9]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** no recorded position.

## Foreign investing
- **Stance:** Graham avoided foreign bonds; Zweig adds foreign stock funds.
- **Graham:** foreign bonds lack enforcement [full: S006#warning-5], and the investor would gain "the latter would benefit both his country and himself if he declined these opportunities" (S006) [full: S006#rule-5]. Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "Putting up to a third of your stock money in mutual funds that hold foreign stocks (including those in emerging markets) helps insure against the risk that our own backyard may not always be the best place in the world to invest." (C007) [full: C007#rule-3] Author: Zweig.
- **Differ:** they discuss different assets (bonds in 1972, stock funds in 2003).

## Falling markets
- **Stance:** a fall is a chance to act on value, not a reason to flee.
- **Graham:** "Basically, price fluctuations have only one significant meaning for the true investor. They provide him with an opportunity to buy wisely when prices fall sharply and to sell wisely when they advance a great deal." (S008) [full: S008#rule-8] "He should never buy a stock because it has gone up or sell one because it has gone down." (S008) [full: S008#rule-11] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "For anyone who will be investing for years to come, falling stock prices are good news, not bad, since they enable you to buy more for less money." (C008) [full: C008#takeaway-6] Author: Zweig.

## Taxes
- **Stance:** taxes shape the choice of bond and the holding period.
- **Graham:** municipal bonds won for those above a 30% bracket in 1972: "Hence if the investor was in a maximum tax bracket higher than 30% he would have a net saving after taxes by choosing the municipal bonds; the opposite" (S004) [full: S004#rule-3] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** a holding period of at least a year, ideally five, cuts capital-gains tax [full: C008#rule-5]; losses offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income [full: C008#rule-7]. Author: Zweig.

## Margin of safety
- **Stance:** the central idea; it depends entirely on the price paid.
- **Graham:** "The margin of safety is always dependent on the price paid. It will be large at one price, small at some higher price, nonexistent at some still higher price" (S020) [full: S020#takeaway-3] "Here the function of the margin of safety is, in essence, that of rendering unnecessary an accurate estimate of the future." (S020) [full: S020#takeaway-1] Author: Graham.
- **Zweig:** "By refusing to pay too much for an investment, you minimize the chances that your wealth will ever disappear or suddenly be destroyed." (C020) [full: C020#takeaway-1] Author: Zweig.
