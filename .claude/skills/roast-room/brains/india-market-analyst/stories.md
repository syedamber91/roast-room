# Stories: India Market Analyst

Built from the 55 public sources: 23 earnings-call transcripts (2024 to 2026), SEBI and NFRA orders, the LODR master circular, the Kotak committee report, IiAS guidelines, SEBI studies and five recent articles. Every company figure is PERISHABLE and is true only on the date given. Every order entry keeps the allegation apart from the finding. Nothing here says to buy, sell or hold any named company. Governance cases come first, then calls, then the retail cases.

## A. Governance and enforcement cases

## 1. Gensol Engineering: strong EBITDA, thin profit, rising pledge, then SEBI orders
- **What happened.** On the Q1 FY25 call (2024-08-13) the founder reported consolidated revenue up 105% to Rs 297 crores and EBITDA near Rs 89 crores, a 30% margin, against profit after tax of about Rs 15 crores and gross debt of Rs 1,363 crores (S021). SEBI's interim order (2025-04-15) and confirmatory order (2025-07-30) then traced INR 977.75 Crore of IREDA and PFC term loans, of which INR 663.89 Crore was for 6,400 electric vehicles. INR 775 Crore was paid to the dealer for 4,704 vehicles that cost INR 567.73 Crore, and INR 262.13 Crore stayed unaccounted (S038). The company entered insolvency on 2025-06-13 (S038).
- **What was visible earlier.** On the August 2024 call: a 30% EBITDA margin that became a thin profit; an investor asking what the promoters were doing about a rising pledge and getting a "taper down over the 3-4 year time period" with no percentage; subsidiary losses of Rs 17 crores called investments; an EV launch delayed; a warrant round that closed at 60 lakh against about 1 crore planned (S021). In the order record: a first loan default on 2024-12-31 while no-default statements went to rating agencies, both agencies cutting the company to D in March 2025, and 75.74 lakh promoter shares pledged (S038). Only the founder spoke for management on the call (S021).
- **Allegation or finding.** The order is an interim and confirmatory order, so the findings are prima facie. SEBI confirmed its directions because the promoters, "except for denying the allegations in general terms, have failed to substantiate their submissions" (S038). SEBI called its own observations tentative pending a forensic audit. It declined to draw a negative inference on the 30,000-vehicle pre-order claim and left the plant-activity claim for the investigating authority. It also said it may have misread the nature of two expenses (S038). A 2026-10-27 article lists the case among reported allegations, not final findings (S049).
- **Lesson for a retail holder.** The checks that would have mattered were the EBITDA to profit gap, the pledge trend, the borrowings note against the asset count, and whether a big order has a price and a delivery schedule. Lender statements and rating actions led; promoter denials without documents did not hold up at this stage. No final finding of fraud exists in these sources.
- **Sources (3):** S021 (2024-08-13), S038 (2025-07-30), S049 (2026-10-27).

## 2. Satyam: family selling and pledges were visible before the books fraud
- **What happened.** The chairman confessed on 2009-01-07 to false books: non-existent bank balances and accrued interest, understated liabilities, overstated debtors (S041). The stock fell 36% in one day on 2008-12-17 on rumours, and about 93.5% correlation with the IT index over ten years fell to 36.0% after the fraud news (S041). A December 2008 plan to buy two firms linked to the chairman's family for about US$1.6 billion was withdrawn after investors objected (S049).
- **What was visible earlier.** The Securities Appellate Tribunal found the relatives had virtually liquidated their holdings from 2001 to 2008 while investors were told the company was rising (S040). Promoter shares moved into a private holding company in September 2006 and were pledged from October 2007 to September 2008 for loans to ten group entities (S040).
- **Allegation or finding.** These are findings, upheld by the Tribunal and the Supreme Court on the merits, with only the money and restraint amounts sent back. The 2018-11-02 order set disgorgement at INR 813.40 crore in total (S040). That order was set aside, and the 2023-11-30 order set unlawful gains at INR 624.10 crore with 12% interest from 2009-01-07, using an intrinsic value of 23.25% of traded price that the member called an estimate (S041). Everything stays subject to Supreme Court appeals (S041). Do not quote the 2018 amounts as final.
- **Quote.** "the nature of the admissions by the promoters in January 2009 were so shocking" (S041)
- **Lesson.** A books fraud shows late. Steady family selling, pledges through a holding vehicle, and a puzzling related acquisition were visible earlier. A pledge held in a holding vehicle may not show in the listed company's own pledge column (S040).
- **Sources (3):** S040 (2018-11-02), S041 (2023-11-30), S049 (2026-10-27).

## 3. IndusInd Bank: derivative accounting, a first number as a percentage, then an insider-trading order
- **What happened.** On 2025-03-10 the bank disclosed accounting discrepancies in its derivatives book with an estimated adverse impact of 2.35% of net worth as of December 2024 (S004). On the special analyst call, management said the issue ran about five to seven years, that the fix would go through the profit and loss, and that the figure was approximate and unvalidated (S004).
- **What was visible earlier.** An analyst raised a CFO resignation "a couple of months back" and a one-year CEO term where the board had approved three (S004). SEBI's order says the information existed on or before 2023-12-04 and was disclosed 2025-03-10, about 15 months later, and that five senior officers sold shares in the period and none bought (S054).
- **Allegation or finding.** S054 (2025-05-28) is an ex parte interim order. The allegation is that the Deputy CEO, the MD and CEO and three other officers sold while holding unpublished price sensitive information, with loss avoided put at INR 19.78 crore in total. The five had not been heard and had 21 days to reply. SEBI states it gave no clean chit on the disclosure failure. The management description (internal check, regulator informed) is management's own and no auditor or regulator finding is in S004. The stock fell 27.165% on the next trading day (S054).
- **Quote.** "it's over a period of 5-7 years with no change in the schema" (S004)
- **Lesson.** Read a first loss number as provisional. Track the outside report and restated results. Note exits and term lengths that came before. Ask when management first knew.
- **Sources (2):** S004 (2025-03-10), S054 (2025-05-28).

## 4. CARE Ratings and the DHFL rating, with IL&FS and Yes Bank in the same order
- **What happened.** SEBI's order of 2023-04-20 found that the MD and CEO of CARE Ratings interfered in the DHFL rating. Interference was not established for Yes Bank, IL&FS or Patanjali, nor were the document-gap and committee-independence charges. Charges against the Chairman were not established (S042).
- **What was visible earlier.** DHFL kept AAA while its 10-year bonds yielded 9.9245% against 9% for HDFC, another AAA housing lender. The share fell 42% in a day. CARE downgraded only after the 2019-01-29 press article. The AAA was revalidated on 2018-11-01 by a committee changed days before, which approved in under 30 minutes. DHFL raised INR 2,577.50 crore on that rating and defaulted to D on 2019-06-05 (S042).
- **Allegation or finding.** The notice alleged interference in ratings of three issuers; the order found it only for DHFL. The Srikrishna report went the other way, and SEBI agreed with it except on DHFL. Interference is hard to prove because it is mostly verbal, and the order leaned on chat records. Direction: the MD may not be associated with a SEBI-registered intermediary for two years (S042).
- **Quote.** "This would indicate that markets were already pricing in a much lower credit quality for DHFL." (S042)
- **Lesson.** A rating is a report written by someone paid by the issuer. Compare the rating with the bond yield and the share price. The order notes earlier SEBI penalties on CARE over IL&FS and Reliance Communications ratings; the notes hold nothing else on IL&FS.
- **Sources (1):** S042 (2023-04-20).

## 5. DHFL: the branch audit
- **What happened.** NFRA's order of 2023-04-13 on the FY2017-18 audit found that none of the 33 branch auditors was approved at the AGM as the Act requires, with 33 engagement partners covering nearly 250 branches. The audit file lacked branch understanding, materiality, IT controls and loan testing, and papers were added after the notice (S047).
- **Allegation or finding.** A finding, about one branch auditor's conduct: Rs 1 lakh penalty and a one-year debarment. The order does not say whether any loan was bad, and its summary names a different auditor and number of branches than its body (S047). Reports of siphoning at DHFL are allegations; a 2026-10-27 article says to check them against the SEBI final order, which is unchecked here (S049).
- **Quote.** "NFRA's investigations revealed that the appointment of none of the 33 branch auditors was approved at the Annual General Meeting (AGM) of DHFL, as required by the Act." (S047)
- **Lesson.** For a lender, ask who audited the branches, whether shareholders appointed them, and how the main auditor relied on them.
- **Sources (2):** S047 (2023-04-13), S049 (2026-10-27).

## 6. Yes Bank: a recovering bank reads differently from a steady one
- **What happened.** On the Q4 FY26 call (2026-04-18) the new MD and CEO described a multi-year turnaround after the earlier reconstruction. FY26 net profit was INR 3,476 crore against INR 2,406 crore, ROA 0.8%, gross NPA 1.3%. A INR 341 crore step-up provision came in a quarter with a INR 288 crore write-back, and profit leaned on security-receipt recoveries of above INR 1,550 crore in FY26, guided to INR 800 crore to INR 1,000 crore in FY27 (S003).
- **What was visible earlier.** The reconstruction was under regulator direction (management's account). The AT1 bond case was before the Supreme Court with no number given. The growth range moved inside one call from 14% to 15% to 13% to 15%, and the core ROA gain from 25 to 30 basis points to 25 to 50 (S003).
- **Allegation or finding.** None. The figures are management's. Separately, the CARE order did not establish rating interference for Yes Bank (S042).
- **Quote.** "we expect recoveries to the tune of INR 800 crores to INR 1,000 crores from SRs in FY27." (S003)
- **Lesson.** Separate recoveries and write-backs from core profit. Compare the targets a bank gives different questioners in one call. Retail questioners got no near-term guidance.
- **Sources (2):** S003 (2026-04-18), S042 (2023-04-20).

## 7. Future Retail: the audit committee approved Rs 300 crore, Rs 3,148.58 crore moved
- **What happened.** SEBI's order of 2026-05-12 found undisclosed and unapproved related party transactions. The company raised US$500 million of notes in January 2020 and paid Rs 3,559.70 crore to a promoter-linked company as an advance for store assets it already leased; about half, Rs 1,770 crore, repaid its own debt. The audit committee's omnibus approval was Rs 300 crore while Rs 3,148.58 crore went to one related party. Seven entities controlled through the chairman were left out of the annual report list (S043).
- **What was visible earlier.** The January 2020 disclosure said the proceeds were for in-store assets. Security deposits and advances rose from Rs 1,935.45 crore to Rs 4,390.38 crore in a year, with ten entities holding about 53% (S043).
- **Allegation or finding.** Found: breaches of the related party approval and annual report disclosure rules. Not established: a fraudulent hidden-debt scheme, diversion of Rs 412.35 crore, and the late-disclosure allegations about the Reliance scheme. Penalties were Rs 20 lakh each on the executive chairman and the joint managing director and Rs 10 lakh on the CFO; no directions under Sections 11 and 11B. Three audit committee members and four auditor noticees were cleared. Appeal status is unchecked (S043). The order's own figures (Rs 1,347 crore plus Rs 521 crore against Rs 1,770 crore) do not reconcile (S043).
- **Quote.** "Thus, Rs. 1,770 Crore (around 50% of the total advance of Rs. 3559.7 Crore) was utilized by FRL towards repayment of its own debt." (S043)
- **Lesson.** Read where a fresh raise ends up, not its stated object. A mild penalty years later, on a company in insolvency, tells a holder the regulator can arrive long after the loss.
- **Sources (1):** S043 (2026-05-12).

## 8. Suzlon: a Rs 77.08 crore business sold to a subsidiary for Rs 2,000 crore
- **What happened.** The SEBI Whole Time Member's order of 2026-05-29 reviewed and set aside a lenient adjudication order of 2025-06-27. It found the FY2014 profit of Rs 1,922.92 crore on the sale of a business with a net book value of about Rs 77.08 crore was not backed by real realisation: Rs 700 crore was paid over three years and Rs 1,300 crore was cleared by same-day group entries in March 2017 (S044).
- **What was visible earlier.** Three years of an unpaid group receivable with the default waived. A contingent liability of about Rs 4,050 crore dropped out of the FY2018 note with unchanged terms. Rs 1,200 crore of loans to subsidiaries became equity through sixteen same-day entries of Rs 75 crore, then sold for Rs 191.60 crore (S044).
- **Allegation or finding.** Findings on review of misleading financial statements. Penalties of Rs 15.95 crore on the company, Rs 5.75 crore and Rs 5.45 crore on two promoter directors, Rs 1.5 crore on a group CFO and Rs 30 lakh on the CFO for FY2014. The order admits it cannot quantify investor loss. Appeal status is unchecked. Events run from FY2014 to FY2020; nothing says how the company stands now (S044).
- **Quote.** "The record indicates that the OMS business, having a net book value of about Rs.77.08 crore, was transferred for a consideration of Rs.2,000 crore." (S044)
- **Lesson.** A gain on a sale to a group company needs a cash check, year after year. A fall in contingent liabilities needs a reason.
- **Sources (1):** S044 (2026-05-29).

## 9. The Adani related-party orders on Hindenburg's allegations
- **What happened.** Hindenburg Research published on 2023-01-24. SEBI's show cause notice (2024-01-15) alleged that loans routed through small firms (Milestone Tradelinks and Rehvar Infrastructure in one order, Adicorp Enterprises in the other) were undisclosed related party transactions that avoided audit committee and shareholder approval (S039, S055).
- **What was visible earlier.** Small intermediaries with tiny net worth moving large sums; INR 1,282 crore moved from Adani Ports to Adicorp and on to Adani Power on the same day or the next; the intermediary kept about 20 basis points. Two CFOs said in recorded statements that the loans were not disclosed in the related party notes (S055).
- **Allegation or finding.** Both final orders of 2025-09-18 found the allegations not established and disposed of the proceedings without direction. The reasons: the firms were not related parties under the rules then in force; all loans were repaid with interest before the investigation; no diversion was alleged. The 2021 rule widening the definition took effect 2022-04-01 with a glide path to 2023-04-01 and does not reach back (S039, S055). The orders do not say the structure was fair to minority holders and do not clear the substance of the dealings (S039, S055).
- **Quote.** "I find that the allegations made against Noticees in the SCN are not established." (S039)
- **Lesson.** A legal win on definitions is not proof the dealings were commercially sensible. Today's definition would catch such routes. Check peak balance outstanding, not the yearly sum of loans.
- **Sources (2):** S039 (2025-09-18), S055 (2025-09-18).

## 10. Adani Enterprises and Adani Ports on their calls
- **What happened.** On the Q2 FY26 call (2025-11-04) half-year profit before tax was INR 5,864 crore, of which INR 3,583 crore was an exceptional item; a partly paid rights issue of up to INR 25,000 crore was approved that day; promoter-family loans of about INR 20,000 crore were to become equity; the capex parts added to 34,500 against a 36,000 total; nobody asked about the exceptional item (S022). On the Q4 FY26 call (2026-04-30) consolidated EBITDA margin was about 56% against a usual 59% to 60%, capex ran above guidance, and the CEO said logistics return on capital was raised by mix: "We wanted to demonstrate that we can give you double-digit return on capital employed." (S017)
- **What was visible earlier.** A margin slip at four named ports, an episodic SEZ income line of INR 891 crores, a leverage ceiling that moved from "2.5x is the ceiling but even if I go 3" (S017), and rights issue timing asked twice and not answered (S022).
- **Allegation or finding.** None on these calls. Neither call touches related-party dealings, pledges, auditors or legal matters (S017, S022). The rights price, dates and conversion terms are not in S022, so dilution cannot be judged from it.
- **Lesson.** Read the exceptional item, cash flow after working capital and the rights terms before the EBITDA. A target built on a commodity mix is a hypothesis, as the CEO said of the five-year numbers.
- **Sources (2):** S022 (2025-11-04), S017 (2026-04-30).

## B. Lenders

## 11. Paytm after the business shock
- **What happened.** On the Q1 FY25 call (2024-07-19) wallet, FASTag, postpaid loans and new UPI onboarding were stopped or paused, and an analyst put consumer payments revenue down 80% on GMV down 20%. The call never names the cause or the regulator (S019).
- **What was visible earlier.** Nothing on this call; the company referred only to "the situation that we got in". It narrowed its profit promise to EBITDA before ESOP cost and before UPI incentive, hoped for a lump-sum UPI incentive, and gave no wallet timeline (S019).
- **Allegation or finding.** None in the source. What came after July 2024 is not in it (S019).
- **Quote.** "We hope and wish that it should come back, but we don't have a direction towards it." (S019)
- **Lesson.** Ask which revenue lines stop if one permission or one bank partner is withdrawn, and which profit a promise means.
- **Sources (1):** S019 (2024-07-19).

## 12. Ola Electric: margin up, share down
- **What happened.** On the Q2 FY26 call (2025-11-06) gross margin was 30.7%, EBITDA turned positive, and market share fell. Incentive money (PLI) was only two points of the margin, with bike certification still expected for January or February. A new battery-storage revenue target was called a median, a range and a minimum in turn (S018).
- **What was visible earlier.** Warranty cost had dragged for quarters and management gave no figure when asked. The service backlog count was refused. The auto business sat below its own breakeven of about 20,000 a month (S018).
- **Allegation or finding.** None. Management claims on defect rates could not be checked (S018).
- **Quote.** "it appears to have come at a cost of sacrificing volume" (S018)
- **Lesson.** Split gross margin into core and subsidy. Ask for warranty cost and complaint counts. Ask which breakeven is meant.
- **Sources (1):** S018 (2025-11-06).

## 13. Manappuram Finance and the Asirvad microfinance curb
- **What happened.** On the call of 2024-11-05, the RBI had told the microfinance subsidiary to stop disbursing from 22 October 2024. The subsidiary was 12,149 crore of 45,716 crore group assets and its profit fell 25% on the quarter. Group gross NPA rose from 1.96% to 2.42% (S006).
- **What was visible earlier.** Microfinance collections had weakened; an inspection that ended in September found a digital personal loan link to gold customers, stopped only afterward; one debenture covenant breach existed. Management kept a 2% group credit cost guide while an analyst put the subsidiary near 7% (S006).
- **Allegation or finding.** The reasons are management's summary; the RBI order is not in the source. Management expected the curb to last 3 to 4 months (S006).
- **Quote.** "RBI has directed our major subsidiary, Asirvad Microfinance to desist from disbursement from 22 October '24" (S006)
- **Lesson.** Read what the regulator raised in inspection and whether the company acted only afterward. Several basic data questions (provision stocks, covenant amounts) were deferred.
- **Sources (1):** S006 (2024-11-05).

## 14. Muthoot Finance: yield that includes one-off recoveries
- **What happened.** On the call of 2025-11-13 an analyst estimated Rs 550 to Rs 600 crores of one-off recovery inside the higher yield; the CFO said about Rs 300 crores plus. Interest income was up 14% on a portfolio up 10% after that adjustment (S007).
- **What was visible earlier.** A one-off benefit of INR 350 crore to INR 400 crore the quarter before. A microfinance subsidiary lost INR 160 crore. A retail investor complaint on the exchange was not explained (S007).
- **Allegation or finding.** None. Management also said no gold loan NPA has ever caused a loss, though it had sold part of its non-performing gold loans to an asset reconstruction company (S007).
- **Quote.** "adjusted for that, the core yield was somewhere around 18%-18.5%." (S007)
- **Lesson.** Read profit and yield net of recoveries. A high gold price is a tailwind that can reverse.
- **Sources (1):** S007 (2025-11-13).

## 15. HDFC Bank: a governance event that drew no questions
- **What happened.** On the Q4 FY26 call (2026-04-18) the opening remarks covered the part-time chairman's exit and a Dubai branch matter of March 2026. The CEO and CFO said a legal review was in process. No analyst raised either in the question round (S001).
- **What was visible earlier.** Nothing in this call. The bank's stated numbers: loan growth 12% against about 5%, capital adequacy 19.7%, gross NPA 1.15%. Management disputed an analyst's industry growth figure with RBI period-end data (S001).
- **Allegation or finding.** Only management's statements; the source gives no regulator finding (S001).
- **Quote.** "The legal review, which is what we had committed at the time when we went to the press, is in process." (S001)
- **Lesson.** A governance event can sit in the opening remarks and get no questions. The holder reads the notes to accounts and any later legal summary.
- **Sources (1):** S001 (2026-04-18).

## 16. Bajaj Finance and HDB Financial: credit cost creeping up
- **What happened.** Bajaj Finance, call 2024-07-23: AUM up 31%, gross loan loss rate 2.12%, stage 2 rising, collections muted, growth guide of 26% to 28% kept, a one-time gain of about 45 crore in other income (S005). HDB Financial, first call after listing, 2025-07-15: gross stage 3 up to 2.56% from 2.26%, credit cost 2.5%, ROA 1.94% (2.02% adjusted), no AUM guidance (S002).
- **What was visible earlier.** At Bajaj: collection efficiency and stage 2 moving before default rates; rural lending held back for months. At HDB: credit cost "inching up over the last five-odd quarters" in the analyst's words, and a customer count of 20 million that is life-to-date (S005, S002).
- **Allegation or finding.** None. Whether second-half improvement came is not in either source.
- **Quote.** "default rates were not a problem even in April measure. It's collection efficiency." (S005)
- **Lesson.** Record what management expects and check the next quarter. Read stage 2, coverage and credit cost together.
- **Sources (2):** S005 (2024-07-23), S002 (2025-07-15).

## C. Earnings quality and disclosure on calls

## 17. Titan and HCLTech: gains that reverse or net out
- **What happened.** Titan, Q1 FY26 call (2025-08-07): about INR 100 crores of one-time benefit in margins, split between watch inventory revaluation and a jewellery hedge mark-to-market, to reverse in the next two quarters (S012). HCLTech, Q2 FY26 call (2025-10-13): an analyst said adjusted for 55 basis points of restructuring the margin was back in the range, but the CFO's own bridge also held a 56 basis point forex gain (S009).
- **Allegation or finding.** None. Titan's own market-size and share figures were called "very wild" estimates by its jewellery head (S012). HCLTech's bookings exclude renewals, where five of the top ten deals showed deflation in some work (S009).
- **Quote.** "It has to reverse. there is no way." (S012)
- **Lesson.** Ask for the amount and timing of any gain behind a margin beat, and whether the matching gains were stripped out when the cost was.
- **Sources (2):** S012 (2025-08-07), S009 (2025-10-13).

## 18. Sun Pharma and Dr. Reddy's: the line below EBITDA
- **What happened.** Sun Pharma, Q2 FY26 call (2025-11-05): EBITDA up 14.9% but net profit up 2.6%, with the tax rate up from 15.8% to 24.7%, a settlement sitting in escrow, a large acquisition and rising short-term debt (S010). Dr. Reddy's, Q2 FY26 call (2025-10-24): margin held by other income and a one-time liability reversal while gross margin fell, per its own reporting (paraphrase of S011).
- **What was visible earlier.** Sun had been guiding to a higher tax rate. At Dr. Reddy's the end date of the high-margin product was known in advance (paraphrase of S011).
- **Allegation or finding.** None.
- **Quote.** "Effective tax rate for the quarter was 24.7% vis-a-vis 15.8% in Q2 FY '25." (S010)
- **Lesson.** Read below EBITDA and match every large balance-sheet move to its cause. Strip other income from margins before comparing.
- **Sources (2):** S010 (2025-11-05), S011 (2025-10-24, paraphrase only).

## 19. Delhivery: a thin profit and a reversed provision
- **What happened.** Q2 FY25 call (2024-11-14): profit after tax of Rs 10 crores against Rs 5,488 crores of cash, Express Parcel margin 15.1% against 18.2% the previous quarter, and a Rs 21 crore reversal of a COVID-era provision booked in other expenses and not as an exceptional item (S020).
- **What was visible earlier.** Customers building their own networks, flat parcel volumes, and a growth guide of 15% to 20% that was not reaffirmed (S020).
- **Allegation or finding.** None. The call does not define adjusted EBITDA (S020).
- **Quote.** "Because it was charged to the other expenses when we made the provision." (S020)
- **Lesson.** A reversal adds to profit in the period it is booked with no current activity. Ask how much of profit is treasury income.
- **Sources (1):** S020 (2024-11-14).

## 20. Zee Entertainment: first digital profit, helped by a catch-up
- **What happened.** Q3 FY26 call (2026-01-22): digital revenue up 73% to INR 4,180 million and first positive digital EBITDA, aided by syndication and a catch-up from a revised telecom pricing agreement, with the one-off not sized. - **Sources (1):** S023 (2026-01-22).

## 21. Pennar Industries: a fix announced before it was proven

## 23. ABB India: orders up, margin down

- **Sources (1):** S014 (2026-05-08).

## D. Retail cases

## 24. IPOs: Unicommerce, anchor books and grey market premium
- **What happened.** Unicommerce (2024 IPO) priced at Rs 108 as a pure offer for sale of Rs 276.57 crore, was 168.35 times subscribed, opened at Rs 235 and closed at Rs 82.52 on 2026-10-19. A one-lot allottee was down 23.6%, an opening-price buyer down 64.9%. The source says none of this suggests wrongdoing (S051). Across 294 listings in 2026 to 2026-10-28, a grey market premium above 25% never led to a listing below issue price (0 of 55), and a zero premium did in 44% of cases (S052).
- **Allegation or finding.** An SME case: SEBI froze Trafiksol's issue proceeds in October 2024 and ordered refunds on 2024-12-03; a report of diversion across about 20 SME issues of one merchant banker is an allegation (S051).
- **Quote.** "oversubscription measures how many people wanted in at a given price, not whether that price was sound or whether the demand was genuine" (S051)
- **Lesson.** Compare the issue price with each seller's cost, and read the fresh issue against offer for sale split (S024). The premium is sentiment, not a forecast (S051).
- **Sources (3):** S051 (October 2026), S052 (2026-10-28), S024 (undated).

## 25. Retail derivatives: the base rate
- **What happened.** SEBI's August 2026 studies find around 91% of individual traders lost money across FY25 to FY26, aggregate net losses of INR 91,685 crore in FY26 against a revised higher figure for FY25, and only about 1.1% earned more than Rs 1 lakh (S030). Of traders active across FY22 to FY26, 0.5% were profitable in all five years (S031).
- **What was visible earlier.** The July 2025 note already put FY25 net losses at INR 1,05,603 crore, up 41% (S032).
- **Allegation or finding.** The studies are descriptive and do not establish cause and effect (S030, S031). They cover derivatives, not cash-market stock picking.
- **Quote.** "Individual traders incurred net losses in almost every quarter, regardless of market direction." (S030)
- **Lesson.** Before any futures or options idea on a stock, the base rate is loss, losses cluster among people trading far above their holdings, and experience does not help (S030, S031). A magazine report (2026-08-20) repeats the figures with a lower trader count and should be checked against the studies (S048).
- **Sources (4):** S030, S031 (August 2026), S032 (July 2025), S048 (2026-08-20).
