# Frameworks: India Market Analyst

Built from 55 public sources: 23 earnings-call transcripts (S001 to S023, calls from July 2024 to May 2026), SEBI and NFRA documents and orders, the Kotak committee report (2017), IiAS voting guidelines (2023-24 edition) and five recent articles. It lists the methods the seat reaches for, most-used first, as shown by how many sources use them.

## How to read this file

- Most frameworks are the seat's own names for methods the sources show. They are marked (unnamed). A framework with a name from a source says so.
- The How to score line is guidance for the seat's Buy, Sell (do not buy) or Wait call. It is not a rule from any source, it names no company, and it is not advice to buy, sell or hold. The seat's actual scoring rules live elsewhere.
- Every number or rule carries the date of its call or document. Company figures are PERISHABLE and true only on that date. Rules change; check the current text.
- Quotation marks mean verbatim. S011 and S025 are paraphrased only, never quoted.
- Where the sources give no threshold, the entry says so. Nothing is filled in from memory.
- Interim orders and show cause notices record allegations. Final and confirmatory orders record findings. The entries keep them apart.

## 1. Guidance-versus-delivery ledger (unnamed)
- What it is: a written log of every number management promises (growth, margin, credit cost, capex, a break-even date), the date it was said, and what the next result showed.
- Steps: (1) record each promise with its date and its definition; (2) on the next call, ask whether it stands, as analysts did on S006 and S020; (3) note any range that moved inside one call or between calls; (4) note any guide that vanished without comment; (5) compare the stated run rate with what the year needs.
- When to use: before every quarterly read of a company, and before scoring any seat that quotes management's outlook.
- How to score (guidance): a company that restates its numbers and then delivers supports Buy-lean only if other checks agree. A range that moves with each questioner, a guide dropped without a word, or a fix announced before it is proven pushes toward Wait. Two guides that cannot both be true pushes toward do not buy. The sources give no cut-off; the seat counts misses and moves.
- Quotes: "does it still stand or we are changing it?" (S006, call 2024-11-05). "We have not changed it." (S006). "I wouldn't want to double guess something" (S002, call 2025-07-15). On the 2026-04-18 call (S003) one range went from 14% to 15% to 13% to 15% inside the call.
- Sources: 23 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S017, S018, S019, S020, S021, S022, S023).

## 2. One-off and quality-of-earnings strip (unnamed)
- What it is: removing one-time gains, recoveries, write-backs, reversals, exchange gains, other income and exceptional items from a reported margin or profit before judging the quarter.
- Steps: (1) ask for the one-off in rupees; (2) ask which quarters it reverses in; (3) strip other income and exceptional items from the margin; (4) check whether a restructuring cost was removed but its matching gain left in; (5) recompute net worth or profit without a group deal; (6) note where a reversal sits in the profit and loss.
- When to use: when a margin or profit beats expectations, when a line jumps, or when management says adjusted.
- How to score (guidance): profit or yield growth that is mostly non-recurring pushes toward Wait. A beat management itself says will reverse is not a trend. Support for Buy-lean needs the stripped figure to still hold. The sources state no percentage cut-off.
- Quotes: "It has to reverse. there is no way." (S012, call 2025-08-07). "adjusted for that, the core yield was somewhere around 18%-18.5%." (S007, call 2025-11-13).
- Sources: 18 (S003, S004, S005, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S015, S017, S018, S019, S020, S022, S023, S044).

## 3. Allegation-versus-finding ladder (unnamed)
- What it is: placing any regulator action on a stage before weighing it: complaint or show cause notice (allegation), interim or ex parte order (prima facie), confirmatory order, final order (finding), appeal.
- Steps: (1) name the document and its date; (2) name who decided it; (3) state what was proven and what was only alleged; (4) note appeal status; (5) note what rules applied at the time.
- When to use: whenever a seat quotes SEBI, NFRA or a court. S054 (interim order, 2025-05-28) records allegations and says no one has been given a clean chit. S038 (confirmatory order, 2025-07-30) is a prima facie stage with a forensic audit pending. S039 and S055 (final orders, 2025-09-18) found the allegations not established. S043 (order, 2026-05-12) found disclosure and approval defaults and did not establish most fraud and diversion allegations. S044 (order, 2026-05-29) reversed a lenient adjudication on review. Appeal status of the 2026 orders is unchecked.
- How to score (guidance): an interim order alone moves the score toward Wait, not toward a conclusion. A final dismissal on the wording of the rules then in force is not a clean bill. A finding of misleading statements weighs more than an allegation of the same facts.
- Quotes: "Such cases need to be kept separate by stage, because allegation, interim order, final order and appeal before the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) carry different weight." (S051). "The observations made in the present Order are tentative in nature and pending" (S038).
- Sources: 13 (S033, S038, S039, S040, S041, S042, S043, S044, S047, S049, S051, S054, S055).

## 4. Related-party test under the listing rules (unnamed)
- What it is: reconciling who the company says is related with who is paid, who approved it, and whether the price is fair.
- Steps: (1) compare the annual report related-party list with the audit committee's approvals; (2) compare the approved ceiling with the amount that moved; (3) ask who owns each small counterparty and its net worth; (4) check guarantees to group entities; (5) check half-yearly disclosure of loans and the end use of funds; (6) ask whether terms are no worse than between unrelated parties.
- Rules as the sources state them, dated: all related-party deals need audit committee approval unless an omnibus approval applies, reviewed at least quarterly (S043, order of 2026-05-12, events of 2019 to 2021). From 2022-04-01 the definition covers transactions whose purpose and effect benefit a related party, with a glide path to 2023-04-01 (S039, order of 2025-09-18). Half-yearly disclosure, with a fine of Rs. 5,000 a day for failing Regulation 23(9), is in S053 (master circular, 2023-07-11). A material deal from 2022-04-01 is Rs. 1000 crores or 10% of consolidated turnover, the lower of the two (S046, 2023-24 edition). Rules change.
- When to use: any group or promoter-linked counterparty, any subsidiary loan, any gain on a sale to a group entity.
- How to score (guidance): a counterparty list that does not reconcile, or flows far above an approved ceiling, pushes toward do not buy. A legal win on old wording is not a clean bill. An approved and disclosed deal can still be unfair (S049).
- Quotes: "related party transactions by themselves are not prohibited in law and are a common form of business transactions" (S039). "A transaction may be disclosed and formally approved without necessarily being fair to the company." (S049).
- Sources: 13 (S010, S016, S021, S022, S038, S039, S043, S044, S045, S046, S049, S053, S055).

## 5. Cash-conversion check (unnamed)
- What it is: comparing cash from operations with profit and EBITDA over several years, and checking what a rising profit leaves in receivables, inventory or escrow.
- Steps: (1) divide operating cash flow by net income and by EBITDA; (2) ask for cash flow after working capital, not before; (3) read receivable days including unbilled; (4) check restricted cash and escrow; (5) check whether cash earns the interest it should; (6) check free cash flow against dividends and capex.
- When to use: every quarterly and annual read.
- How to score (guidance): conversion at or above 100% for the year, as one IT call reported on 2025-10-13 (S009), supports a clean read. Falling conversion while receivables grow pushes toward Wait. The 55 sources state no cut-off for cash conversion; the Kotak committee only asked for a half-yearly cash flow statement (S045, 2017, a recommendation).
- Quotes: "Operating Cash Flow to Net Income conversion is at 133%, and Free Cash Flow to Net Income is at 125%." (S009, call 2025-10-13). "it will be better to look at operating profit before working capital changes." (S022, call 2025-11-04).
- Sources: 12 (S009, S010, S014, S015, S017, S019, S020, S021, S022, S023, S041, S045).

## 6. Base and denominator check (unnamed)
- What it is: asking what a growth rate or headline count is measured against.
- Steps: (1) find the base period and whether it was distorted by a duty cut or a destocking; (2) separate price, volume, mix and acquisition; (3) check exclusions, such as growth without a seasonal portfolio; (4) check whether a customer count is cumulative; (5) check whether a ratio is shown on an adjusted base; (6) note a figure of 30% peak to non-peak is not year-on-year.
- When to use: any growth headline, any small-base segment, any customer or user count.
- How to score (guidance): headline growth that is mostly price, base effect or a part-year acquisition pushes toward Wait. Growth that survives the split supports Buy-lean.
- Quotes: "Growth in consolidated sales was around 7% excluding the seasonal portfolio, while the reported growth was 1.7%, impacted by the seasonal portfolio." (S013, call 2025-07-31). "30% peak to non-peak period" (S020, call 2024-11-14).
- Sources: 11 (S002, S005, S008, S009, S012, S013, S014, S015, S017, S019, S020).

## 7. Lender asset-quality ladder (unnamed)
- What it is: reading a bank or finance company in a fixed order, because credit cost alone can improve for the wrong reasons.
- Steps: (1) deposit stability and how much is volatile (S001, S008); (2) credit cost together with stage 2, stage 3 and coverage (S002, S005, S008); (3) strip recoveries, write-backs and used provisions (S003, S007, S008); (4) collateral loan to value and auction volumes (S006, S007); (5) regulator inspection or business halts (S006); (6) divergence disclosure when the regulator's view differs by more than 5% of pre-provision profit (S053, 2023-07-11); (7) cross-check the rating and the audit (S042, S047).
- When to use: any lender, any quarter where credit cost fell.
- How to score (guidance): an improvement built only on cover, seasonality or recoveries is not yet a trend, and pushes toward Wait. Stage 2 rising while default rates look fine, a segment halted by the regulator, or an unvalidated first estimate of a loss pushes toward do not buy until the outside review lands.
- Quotes: "the large reduction of credit cost happened on account of lower slippages and higher coverage of guarantee." (S008, call 2026-01-20). "default rates were not a problem even in April measure. It's collection efficiency." (S005, call 2024-07-23).
- Sources: 10 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S006, S007, S008, S042, S047, S053).

## 8. Use-of-funds trace (unnamed)
- What it is: comparing the purpose a company gave for money it raised with where the cash went next.
- Steps: (1) read the offer document or filing for the stated object; (2) read the quarterly deviation statement for issues (S053); (3) follow large advances and deposits for return flows; (4) check whether loan money for an asset bought that asset; (5) check what happens to asset sale proceeds (S046); (6) check whether a raise was drawn in full.
- When to use: any IPO, QIP, preferential issue, rights issue, bond or term loan.
- How to score (guidance): a stated purpose that differs from the actual use pushes toward do not buy. A raise drawn at 10% of its approval, with the rest deferred until use is clear, is neutral and worth tracking.
- Quotes: "This was in variance to the disclosure dated January 15, 2020 made by FRL to the exchanges that the proceeds will be utilized to meet the capital expenditure for the acquisition of in-store retail infrastructure assets." (S043, order 2026-05-12). "unless we have full visibility on the deployment of those proceeds, we are not going to go for any drawdowns." (S023, call 2026-01-22).
- Sources: 10 (S022, S023, S024, S038, S043, S044, S045, S046, S051, S053).

## 9. Regulatory-dependency check (unnamed)
- What it is: listing every approval, subsidy, tariff, duty or licence the plan or the margin depends on, with a date for each.
- Steps: (1) name the body and the step; (2) ask for the date; (3) compute the margin without the incentive; (4) ask what the plan looks like if refused; (5) check the base effect of last year's rule change.
- When to use: any company whose growth or margin rests on a third party's decision.
- How to score (guidance): dependence with no date pushes toward Wait. A margin that needs an incentive not yet certified is scored without it. The sources give no threshold.
- Quotes: "We control the content of the discussion, but we don't control the timing." (S017, call 2026-04-30). "PLI in the 30.7% gross margin was only two points." (S018, call 2025-11-06).
- Sources: 10 (S005, S006, S010, S011, S012, S014, S017, S018, S019, S022).

## 10. Promoter pledge, encumbrance and insider-selling watch (unnamed)
- What it is: reading the promoter's pledged share, any invocation, and insider sales in the months before news.
- Steps: (1) read the quarterly shareholding pattern for pledge; (2) read the stated reason once pledge reaches the SEBI line; (3) watch invocations by a lender; (4) check whether insider sales had a public trading plan and the 120-day cooling off; (5) check six-month contra trades and gifts; (6) check pledge through family holding vehicles.
- Rules as the sources state them, dated: reasons are required when encumbrance reaches 50% of the promoter holding or 20% of total capital (S034, FAQ updated to 2022-03-30). Disclosure of an encumbrance is due within 7 working days (S034). A trading plan has a 120-day cooling off (S029, FAQ dated 2024-12-31). Rules change.
- When to use: any promoter-led company, especially after a fall in price.
- How to score (guidance): a rising pledge in a growing company pushes toward Wait. Pledges invoked while the stock falls, or insider selling with no plan on record before bad news, push toward do not buy. A single filing is a prompt, not a finding.
- Quotes: "specifically disclose detailed reasons for encumbrance if the combined encumbrance by the promoter along with PACs with him equals or exceeds either 50% of their shareholding in the company or 20% of the total share capital of the company" (S034). "a cooling off period of 120 calendar days is mandated from the date of public disclosure of the trading plan till the commencement of trading by the insider" (S029).
- Sources: 9 (S021, S028, S029, S034, S038, S040, S041, S053, S054).

## 11. Debt, covenant and leverage-ceiling read (unnamed)
- What it is: reading debt as a number, a ceiling, a rate and a set of lender conditions.
- Steps: (1) debt and interest against EBITDA; (2) whether the stated leverage target is a hard cap; (3) covenant breaches, their amount and waiver; (4) the rate and use of new loans; (5) what full use of a borrowing limit would do to ratios (S046); (6) a default beyond 30 days must be disclosed within 24 hours of the 30th day (S053, 2023-07-11); (7) rating action and bond yield (S042).
- When to use: any company with rising debt, a raise, or a new business funded by borrowing.
- How to score (guidance): a cap that management says can be broken pushes toward Wait. A covenant breach tied to bad loans, or a rating cut with a doubt about papers, pushes toward do not buy until the lender's position is clear.
- Quotes: "2.5x is the ceiling but even if I go 3" (S017, call 2026-04-30). "there is only one covenant breach on NCD, which is a very small amount." (S006, call 2024-11-05).
- Sources: 9 (S006, S010, S015, S017, S021, S038, S042, S046, S053).

## 12. Governance checklist from the Kotak committee and IiAS (named sources)
- What it is: a board-and-auditor checklist from the SEBI committee report of October 2017 (recommendations, with proposed dates of 2018 to 2020) and the IiAS voting guidelines 2023-24 (a proxy adviser's views unless the text says law).
- Steps and thresholds, dated: (1) board independence: half the board where the chair is executive or promoter group (S046, 2023-24); (2) independent director tenure of two terms of five years, and IiAS treats a director as non-independent after 10 years (S046); (3) attendance: IiAS accepts a minimum 75% for a director up for re-appointment (S046); (4) audit firm tenure over 10 years (3 for banks) is an IiAS red flag (S046); (5) royalty to a parent above 5% of consolidated turnover needs shareholder approval (S045, S046); (6) executive promoter pay above Rs 5 crore or 2.5% of net profit, or all promoter directors above 5% (S045, S046); (7) preferential issues: allottees, dilution, urgency (S046); (8) a 25% change in a management-discussion ratio should be explained (S045). Rules change; several have been amended since.
- When to use: at each annual general meeting and when a board event happens.
- How to score (guidance): several flags together push toward Wait. The checklist says nothing about price or valuation, and IiAS states its recommendations carry no buy, sell or price view.
- Quotes: "good news may be revealed aggressively while bad news may be allowed to percolate slowly or remain undisclosed" (S045). "The recommendations are non-binding and the advisory reports do not carry any buy, sell, or price recommendation." (S046).
- Sources: 8 (S027, S042, S043, S045, S046, S047, S050, S053).

## 13. Disclosure-cut and segment-visibility check (unnamed)
- What it is: noticing what the company stopped showing, which segment profit is hidden, and where a large business sits in Others.
- Steps: (1) compare this deck with last year's; (2) ask for segment profit when a segment is a large share of revenue; (3) ask for balance sheet and investment values when a pack has none; (4) ask where a growing business is reported; (5) note what is declined as competitive or confidential.
- When to use: when headline growth weakens at the same time as disclosure shrinks.
- How to score (guidance): shrinking disclosure with weak growth pushes toward Wait. Confidentiality is a fair limit for a customer name and a poor one for a segment profit that peers publish.
- Quotes: "we have streamlined certain disclosures in the Investor Presentation. But this has been based on benchmarking with industry peers." (S013, call 2025-07-31). "we are not yet reporting it as a significant independent segment." (S022, call 2025-11-04).
- Sources: 8 (S012, S013, S015, S017, S019, S021, S022, S023).

## 14. Order book, bookings and capacity-claim check (unnamed)
- What it is: asking what an order book, a bookings figure or a capacity number includes and leaves out.
- Steps: (1) what is excluded (renewals, rate card deals); (2) is the order a binding contract or an MOU; (3) how much converts this year; (4) is quoted capacity theoretical; (5) does the deck and the release use the same order book.
- When to use: capital goods, services, EVs, ports, anything announced as an order.
- How to score (guidance): a headline order without price or delivery schedule is a sentiment, not revenue. A figure whose definition differs between documents pushes toward Wait.
- Quotes: "new bookings we disclose do not include rate card deals or renewals" (S009, call 2025-10-13). "no reference to the price of the vehicle or delivery schedules" (S038, order 2025-07-30, prima facie stage).
- Sources: 7 (S009, S014, S015, S017, S021, S022, S038).

## 15. Tip, adviser and finfluencer check (unnamed)
- What it is: asking who is paying the person recommending a stock, whether they are registered and whether the return claim is credible.
- Steps: (1) look up the SEBI registration number; (2) check for undisclosed paid promotion; (3) treat assured or unusually steady returns as a red flag (paraphrase of S025); (4) never act on a hot tip; (5) verify the payee account before paying.
- Rules and proposals, dated: SEBI's board memo of July 2024 proposed that regulated firms not associate with unregistered persons who give advice or claim returns. It is a proposal; what was notified is not in the file (S033). Advice in widely available media is carved out of regulated advice (S024).
- When to use: any idea that reaches a retail holder from a social post or a message.
- How to score (guidance): an unregistered source, a return claim or paid promotion pushes toward do not buy. A registered adviser is a minimum, not a reason.
- Quotes: "Do not rely on making your investment decisions on hot tips as a person who wants to offload securities which may not be marketable may be indulging in it." (S024). "Assured returns being offered by the investment advisers to the clients." (S024).
- Sources: 7 (S024, S025, S027, S028, S033, S035, S037).

## 16. Auditor and resignation signal (unnamed)
- What it is: reading auditor and director exits and audit quality as early signals.
- Steps: (1) read the filed reasons for an auditor resignation, due within 24 hours (S053, 2023-07-11); (2) read an independent director's reasons and whether they confirm no others (S045, 2017 recommendation); (3) check branch and component auditors were approved by shareholders (S047); (4) check audit file integrity; (5) check tenure and rotation (S046).
- When to use: any resignation, any qualified audit opinion, any restatement.
- How to score (guidance): an early auditor exit close to results, or one that cites missing information, pushes toward Wait or do not buy until the reasons are read. Tenure on its own is a flag, not a finding.
- Quotes: "The resignation of an auditor before the expiry of the term may be a cause for concern." (S045). "Oral explanations by the auditor, on their own, do not represent adequate support for the work auditor performed or conclusions the auditor reached" (S047, order 2023-04-13).
- Sources: 6 (S004, S043, S045, S046, S047, S053).

## 17. F&O odds for individual traders (named source: SEBI studies)
- What it is: the base rate for retail derivatives, stated before any strategy.
- Steps: (1) quote the loss rate and its dates; (2) compare position size with holdings; (3) count transaction costs; (4) check expiry-day concentration; (5) for option selling, check the tail, not the win rate.
- Data, dated: FY25 to FY26 SEBI study of 15 brokers: around 91% of individual traders lost (S030, August 2026). FY26 alone: 87.7% lost, net loss about Rs 91,685 crore (S048, magazine report of the studies, 2026-08-20). Study descriptive, not causal (S030, S031). Perishable.
- When to use: any retail suggestion to use futures or options.
- How to score (guidance): the base rate puts the burden of proof on the trade. With no stock held behind it, or size far above holdings, push toward do not buy.
- Quotes: "Around 91% of individual traders incurred net losses during FY25-FY26" (S030). "Longer trading experience was not associated with improved outcomes" (S031).
- Sources: 5 (S024, S030, S031, S032, S048).

## 18. Retail recourse and shareholder rights (unnamed)
- What it is: knowing which door fits which problem and which rights a holder has.
- Steps: (1) complaint ladder: company, exchange or broker first, then SCORES; (2) check what SCORES will not take (mismanagement and company performance go elsewhere); (3) time limits and reply times; (4) rights: e-voting, postal ballot, requisitioning a meeting; (5) broker default compensation limits.
- Dated figures that disagree: a one-year complaint window and a 21-day reply (S035, October 2024) against three years and 30 days (S036, September 2022). Use the later. Retail voting is about 20% (S050, December 2025).
- When to use: after a trade you did not place, a missed dividend, or a governance concern.
- How to score (guidance): not a buy or sell input. A company with many unresolved complaints at the exchanges is a prompt to read them (S037, FY2025-26 counts, perishable).
- Quotes: "SEBI SCORES platform is a facilitator of redressal of complainant’s grievance from SEBI regulated entities and does not adjudicate disputes between the complainant and the entity." (S035). "within a period of one year from the date of occurrence of the cause of action" (S035).
- Sources: 5 (S027, S035, S036, S037, S050).

## 19. IPO hype check (unnamed)
- What it is: reading an IPO from the offer document, not from subscription or the grey market.
- Steps: (1) fresh issue against offer for sale; (2) each seller's cost against the issue price; (3) anchor book and late orders; (4) regulation under which it lists; (5) treat the grey market premium as sentiment; (6) check what happened to buyers at listing and a week later.
- Rules and data, dated: retail bidding capped at Rs. 2,00,000 (S027); half of each anchor allotment has a 90-day lock-in for issues opening on or after 2022-04-01 (S051); grey market premium correlation with listing gain was 0.88 with a typical miss of 8.6 points across 294 listings in 2026 (S052, 2026-10-28, vendor data, unaudited). Perishable.
- When to use: any IPO.
- How to score (guidance): all offer for sale with seller cost far below issue price pushes toward do not buy unless the business case stands without the listing. Oversubscription is not a score.
- Quotes: "A GMP is an unregulated, unaudited quote from an informal market, not a forecast" (S051). "oversubscription measures how many people wanted in at a given price, not whether that price was sound or whether the demand was genuine" (S051).
- Sources: 4 (S024, S027, S051, S052).
