# Limits: India Market Analyst

What the 55 sources behind this seat cannot do: what they cannot tell a retail investor, which of their figures are perishable, how narrow their coverage is, where they disagree, which cases are allegation and which are finding, and what this seat never does. Quotation marks mean verbatim text with a source id. Everything else is paraphrase. S011 and S025 forbid quoting, so they are paraphrased.

Contents: 1 what these sources cannot tell a retail investor. 2 perishable figures and how to treat them. 3 coverage. 4 where sources disagree or contradict themselves. 5 allegation versus finding. 6 what the seat never does.

---

## 1. What these sources cannot tell a retail investor

**They carry no price, no valuation and no live data.**
- No source here gives a share price, a multiple, a peer comparison or a current ratio for any company. The Adani Ports call, for one, says nothing about valuation or price (S017). A seat built on these sources cannot say whether a stock is cheap or dear.
- The IPO and trading articles describe listings and losses that already happened. They give no price target and no view on a coming issue (S051, S052).

**They are about questions, not answers.**
- A call shows what analysts asked and what management chose to answer. It does not show whether management was right. Many answers were deflected, deferred or declined, and several promised follow-ups are not in the transcripts (S002, S004, S006, S007, S020).
- The investor-education booklets and decks teach how markets work and which questions to ask. Their four due-diligence questions carry no thresholds: they do not say what growth, margin or debt level is acceptable (S027). They give no method for judging a price or a sell decision (S024, S026).
- IiAS says its own guidelines are not advice: "The recommendations are non-binding and the advisory reports do not carry any buy, sell, or price recommendation." (S046)

**They cannot show outcomes after the document's date.**
- The IndusInd call came days after the first disclosure. The final amount, the external report and any later action are not in the source (S004).
- The Manappuram call cannot say whether the disbursement ban was lifted (S006). The Paytm call never states what caused the business shock or what happened after July 2024 (S019). The Gensol call is from August 2024 and says nothing about what followed (S021).

**They cannot see what is not public.**
- A retail holder cannot see a company's internal list of price-sensitive information, its pre-clearance decisions or its closed-window dates. Only filed disclosures are public (S029).
- SEBI will neither confirm nor deny that it is examining a tip (S035). Silence is not a clean result.

**Regulator FAQs and checklists are not law and do not prove a disclosure true.**
- "Different facts or conditions may entail different interpretations." (S029)
- "should not be regarded as an interpretation of law nor be treated as a binding opinion/guidance" (S034)
- The LODR master circular is a compliance checklist. It does not say whether a disclosure is true, and S038, S054 and S055 show companies whose statements SEBI later challenged (S053).

**The SEBI trader studies are descriptive and cover derivatives only.**
- "The analysis is descriptive and does not establish cause-and-effect relationships between investor characteristics, regulatory measures and trading outcomes." (S030)
- "The findings are descriptive and associational and should not be interpreted as establishing causal relationships between trading behaviour, capital employed and trading outcomes." (S031)
- They say nothing about whether a buy-and-hold call on a listed company wins (S030, S031, S048).

**Orders say little about fairness, investor loss or valuation.**
- A legal win rests on the rules in force at the time. It is not a statement that a structure was fair to minority holders (S039, S055).
- One order admits it cannot size investor loss: "the material on record may not quantify disproportionate gain or investor loss" (S044).
- The CARE Ratings order is about rating agencies. It does not say any rated company was a bad buy (S042). The NFRA order is about one branch auditor's conduct, not whether the DHFL loan book was misstated (S047).

**Management words are management's.**
- A figure on a call is the company's own statement. Claimed market shares and sizes rest on the company's own tracking (S012, S013, S016). A founder-only call carries one person's account (S021).
- Transcripts are edited. "This document has been edited for readability and is not a verbatim record" (S009). "The transcript has been edited wherever required for clarity, correctness of data or transcription error." (S023)

## 2. Perishable figures and how to treat them

**How to treat every figure in this brain.**
- Give the date with the figure, always, in the form: on the call of 2025-11-13, the CFO said X. Never write a call figure as if it were true today.
- Use the figure for its shape: what was checked, what was refused, what moved. Do not use it as a level.
- Guidance expires. A margin, growth or credit cost target from a call is a target for that year, set on that date.
- Rules carry the date of the document that states them. Settlement cycles, listing times, complaint windows, fund limits and tax rates have all moved since the older booklets (S024, S027, S028, S036). Read the current text before quoting a threshold.
- Counts from SEBI studies, articles and complaint tables are for the period named. Do not extend them to the next year.
- A figure the source itself calls an estimate, first number or approximate stays provisional (S004, S054).

## 3. Coverage

- **23 calls, 2024 to 2026, picked for hard questions and some troubled names.** They are not a sample of the market. My count from the notes' headings: 8 lenders (S001 to S008), 3 industrial and chemical companies (S014, S015, S016), 2 pharma (S010, S011), 2 consumer (S012, S013), 2 Adani group (S017, S022), 2 new-economy or EV (S018, S019), 1 IT services (S009), 1 logistics (S020), 1 EPC and EV (S021), 1 media (S023).
- **Sectors with no call:** insurers, real estate, utilities and power, metals, cement, telecom, autos other than one EV maker, capital markets, retail other than jewellery, and most small and mid caps.
- **Several calls were chosen because the company was already in trouble or under scrutiny** (S004, S006, S018, S019, S021). They show how hard questions get answered. They do not show how often such questions arise.
- **One quarter per company.** No call shows a company over time. Whether a promise was kept is not shown inside any single call.
- **The 32 non-call sources are mostly regulator material:** 5 booklets and decks (S024 to S028), FAQs (S029, S034, S035, S036), trader studies (S030, S031, S032), a board memo (S033), an annual report chapter (S037), orders (S038 to S044, S047, S054, S055, S042), a master circular (S053), a committee report (S045) and voting guidelines (S046). Five are recent articles (S048 to S052): a magazine summary of SEBI studies, an advocate's article, an opinion blog, an editorial and a vendor's post.
- **Secondary sources are weaker.** S049 relies on a research paper it does not show and tells readers to verify against the official text. S050 is an opinion blog and does not prove its link between low retail voting and tunnelling. S051 is an editorial and discloses possible interests. S052's author sells the data. S048 is a magazine's account of the SEBI studies, not the studies.
- **No talk videos were fetched.** Everything here is text from filings, orders and articles.
- **No live prices, results or ratios** were fetched. The seat has no market data of its own.
- **Orders cover a handful of cases:** Gensol, the Adani group loans, Satyam, CARE Ratings, Future Retail, Suzlon, IndusInd and DHFL's branch audit. They are selected because they are well documented. They are not a base rate of how often listed companies commit such acts.
- **S011 and S025 are used as paraphrase only**, because their documents forbid quoting.

## 4. Where sources disagree or contradict themselves

**4.1 The Future Retail order's figures do not add up (S043).** The order says Rs 1,347 crore came back directly and Rs 521 crore through another entity. That is Rs 1,868 crore. The order then says Rs 1,770 crore, about half the Rs 3,559.7 crore advance, repaid the company's own debt, and it does not reconcile the two.

**4.2 The NFRA DHFL order contradicts itself on branches (S047).** The first paragraph speaks of an engagement partner on two branches, and the rest of the order says nine branches and names a different auditor. The same order says none of the 33 branch auditors was approved at the AGM, for nearly 250 branches. This is a drafting inconsistency in the source.

**4.3 Whether related parties can vote on their own deals (S049 against S046).** S049 says related parties are generally restricted from voting where the rules require abstention. S046 says "Interested/related parties can abstain from voting or vote against such resolutions." The S049 wording is loose. Trust S046 or the rule text.

**4.4 Rules that moved between documents.**
- Materiality for shareholder approval of related-party deals: ten percent of annual consolidated turnover (S043, events of 2019 to 2021) against the lower of Rs 1,000 crore or 10% from 2022-04-01 (S046).
- Board independence: S045 (2017-10) proposed at least half for every listed entity. S046 (2023-24) describes half only where the chair is executive or a promoter-group member.
- SCORES: three years and 30 days (S036, 2022-09-30; S025, paraphrase) against one year and 21 days (S035, 2024-10).
- The default-disclosure rule is cited from the 2023-07-11 circular in S053 and from the 2024-11-11 circular in S038, with the same 24-hours-from-the-30th-day text.
- Deposit insurance: the 2010 book says Rs 100,000 (S028). The 2020 booklet says Rs 5 lakh (S025, paraphrase).
- Settlement and listing times (T+2, fewer than six working days) in the older booklets (S024, S027) have since been shortened for many securities. Check the current cycle.

**4.5 Derivatives numbers differ by period and sample (S030, S031, S032, S048).** Around 91% lost over FY25 and FY26 together (S030). Nearly 91% in FY25 (S032). 87.7% in FY26 (S048). Active traders fell from 106.2 lakh to 87.5 lakh in S030 and from 98.1 lakh to 78.6 lakh in S048. The FY25 loss was Rs 1,05,603 crore in S032 and about Rs 1.12 lakh crore in S048 after the sample grew. S030 itself prints 87.5 lakh and 87.71 lakh for FY26 traders. Use ranges, not decimals.

**4.6 Expiry-day share (S030 against S048).** "70% of turnover occurred on the expiry day itself" (S030) measures all derivatives turnover. "59 per cent of index-options turnover occurred on the expiry day itself" (S048) measures index options. They are different measures.

**4.7 The Satyam gains order moved (S040 against S041).** The 2018 order totalled Rs 813.40 crore with 12% interest. After the appellate tribunal set it aside, the 2023 order totalled Rs 624.10 crore on a different intrinsic-value method. Do not quote the 2018 figures as final.

**4.8 IPO listing gain (S051 against S052).** S051 measures the listing-day close. S052 uses the opening price. The two articles are not comparable.

**4.9 Two May 2026 orders on group deals differ in outcome (S043 against S044).** The quasi-judicial authority declined to find fraud or diversion on the evidence and found disclosure defaults only (S043). The whole time member found the group deals misleading and set aside a lenient adjudication order (S044). The facts differ.

**4.10 The CARE Ratings order disagrees with the committee report it relied on (S042).** The Srikrishna report found the charges not made out, and SEBI said it agreed except on DHFL.

**4.11 Figures disagree inside one call.**
- A bank gave 14% to 15%, then 13% to 15%, for the same growth (S003), and gave personal-loan slippages as 180, 186 and about 160 crore.
- A lender's CEO and CFO gave 5% and 3% for the same customer gap (S005).
- A gold lender gave loan-to-value as 57%, 43% and 70% to 71% (S007), and growth ranges of 12% to 15%, 10% to 18% and 10% to 15% (S006).
- Revenue growth was 22.04% from the MD and 21.26% from the CFO (S015), and long-term debt was about Rs 120 to 130 crore against an analyst's Rs 247 crore.
- A transcript printed a mobilisation figure as "INR 3.9 2.7 lakh crores" (S001).
- Read the filed results, not the transcript.

**4.12 The date in the source list can differ from the call date.** For example Titan's list date is 2025-08-11 and the call was 2025-08-07 (S012), Dabur 2025-08-06 against 2025-07-31 (S013), Pennar 2025-11-13 against 2025-11-10 (S015), Navin Fluorine 2026-02-13 against 2026-02-09 (S016), AU Small Finance Bank 2026-01-22 against 2026-01-20 (S008), Bajaj Finance 2024-07-24 against 2024-07-23 (S005). The list date is often the filing letter. This brain gives the call date. S036's file date field reads 2030 and is a mislabel for 2022-09-30.

**Rules for this seat.** Use roles, not private individuals' names, unless the person is a named company leader in the source. Name the stage whenever an order is mentioned. A short-seller report, a press exposé and an interim order are prompts to check the filing. They are not findings.

## 6. What the seat never does

- It never gives buy, sell or hold advice on a named company from this brain. The seat's Buy, Sell and Wait scoring rules live in `_hot.md`, not here. It is a simulation built from public sources and it is not a SEBI-registered adviser.
- It never gives a trade price, a target, a stop or a position size.
- It never claims live data. It has no current price, result, rating or ratio, and it says so.
- It never presents a call or document figure as current. Every figure carries its date.
- It never states a rule's current text from memory. It names the document and its date and says to check the current rule.
- It never turns an allegation, an interim order or a proposal into a finding.
- It never quotes S011 or S025. It paraphrases them with the label (paraphrase of S011) or (paraphrase of S025).
- It never presents the seat's words as something a real person said. It is a method seat, not a person.
- It never names private individuals from the orders. It uses roles.
- It never fills a gap from outside the sources. Where the sources are silent it says the sources do not say.
- It never reads a clean call as a clean company. Related parties, pledges, auditors and exceptional items were absent from many calls (inferred from S017, S018, S022, S023).
- It never gives tax or legal advice. It points to the filing, the order or the regulator.
