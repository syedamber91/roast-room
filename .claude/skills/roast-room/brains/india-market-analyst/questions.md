# Questions: India Market Analyst

Built from 55 public sources: 23 earnings-call transcripts (S001 to S023, calls from July 2024 to May 2026), SEBI and NFRA documents and orders, the Kotak committee report (2017), IiAS voting guidelines (2023-24 edition) and five recent articles. A question bank for cross-examining a listed Indian company and the other seats; it scores nothing and advises nothing.

## How to read this file

- Each question gives: the question in the seat's plain words, who it is for, verbatim analyst examples with source id and call date, what a straight answer and an evasive answer look like on these calls, and the answer that should worry a retail holder.
- Quotation marks mean verbatim from the source. Everything else is paraphrase or the seat's own wording.
- Company figures are PERISHABLE: each is true on the stated call or document date only. Rules change; each rule carries its document date.
- Regulator orders: an interim order or show cause notice records an allegation. A confirmatory or final order records a finding. The two are kept apart below.
- S011 (Dr. Reddy's call) forbids quoting without the company's written consent and S025 (SEBI booklet) forbids reproduction. Both are paraphrased only.
- Where no analyst on the 23 calls asked the question, the entry says so and rests on the cited document instead.
- Lines marked inferred from are the seat's own reading of several sources.

## 1. Management credibility (guidance versus delivery)

### Q1.1 Did last quarter's guidance hold?
- Ask: Is the range you gave for growth, margin or credit cost unchanged, and if it moved, what changed?
- For: management (CEO, CFO).
- Analyst examples: "does it still stand or we are changing it?" (S006, call 2024-11-05). "Or do you think we can start with 15% plus growth?" (S003, call 2026-04-18). "is it fair to assume that your earlier guidance of 15% to 20% kind of revenue growth in Express Parcel can be achieved from H2 onwards or would you rather not go back to that?" (S020, call 2024-11-14).
- Straight answer: a restated number with its base. On the 2024-11-05 call the CFO said "We have not changed it." (S006) on a 2% group credit cost guide, though an analyst put one subsidiary near 7%.
- Evasive answer: "it's hard to comment, because it will also depend on what happens to the market overall" (S020), then "I don't really know" (S020). The 15% to 20% range was not reaffirmed.
- Should worry a retail holder: a range that moves with each questioner. On the 2026-04-18 call (S003) the growth view went from 14% to 15% to 13% to 15% inside one call, and the core return on assets aim from 25 to 30 basis points to 25 to 50.

### Q1.2 Do your guides agree with each other?
- Ask: Do the revenue, margin and profit numbers you guided add up, and does the quarter's run rate reach the year's target?
- For: management (CEO, CFO), and for a small company the founder alone.
- Analyst examples: "you guided for the OPM of 15% this year, but in this quarter, we did 30% already. So, is it a conservative guidance or how you are looking at the situation, sir?" (S021, call 2024-08-13). "we have to do Rs.1,700 Cr in next 9 months that means the quarterly run rate of Rs. 550 Cr. But currently we are at a run rate of Rs. 300 Cr." (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Straight answer: the arithmetic that links the quarter to the year. The founder said Q1 is usually 10% to 15% of annual revenue and Q4 was close to 40% the year before (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Evasive answer: "We absolutely stand by the Rs. 2,000 crore guidance" (S021) with no first-half and second-half split, which was also asked.
- Should worry a retail holder: margin guided at 15% to 18% for the year while a separate consolidated EBITDA guide of "north of Rs. 400 crores" (S021) implies 20% on Rs 2,000 crore of revenue (the note-taker's division). Two guides that cannot both hold.

### Q1.3 What in the next filing will show this has normalised?
- Ask: You say this fixes itself next quarter. Which line in the next result proves it, and what number do we hold you to?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "Because this is not typical seasonality, because even if you look at quarter-on-quarter, it has been just inching up over the last five-odd quarters." (S002, call 2025-07-15). "the net cash generated Y-o-Y has declined despite similar profits" (S010, call 2025-11-05).
- Straight answer: a number and a date. The Sun Pharma CFO said working capital "will get normalized in this quarter" (S010) on the 2025-11-05 call, which a reader can test against the next cash-flow statement.
- Evasive answer: "I wouldn't want to double guess something" (S002), given when asked where credit cost would peak after five quarters of increases.
- Should worry a retail holder: a promised normalisation with no figure, or one that cannot be seen in the next filing. Write the promise down and check it (inferred from S002, S010).

### Q1.4 Last time you said the problem was solved. What happened?
- Ask: What did you say on the previous call, what did the result show, and why should the new guidance be trusted?
- For: CEO, MD.
- Analyst examples: "I read your last conference call transcript. So, what I can perceive by reading this is that you were quite confident about this labor issue solution. And then we came across this result." (S015, call 2025-11-10).
- Straight answer: admit the gap. The MD said "We did not miss our guidance." (S015) and then conceded "the primary difference that I see between us and our competitors is perhaps a 300 basis point margin" (S015).
- Evasive answer: the first reply only, which defends the guide and does not engage with the labour shortfall the analyst named.
- Should worry a retail holder: a fix announced before it is proven, followed by a commitment such as "My team and I will commit to both the value of PAT and the margin growing in Q3." (S015, call 2025-11-10). Check the Q3 result against that sentence.

### Q1.5 What are the building blocks of a new-business number?
- Ask: For a product not yet on sale, what price, units, orders or deposits sit under the revenue target?
- For: founder, CEO.
- Analyst examples: "I just want to understand what the building blocks of the assumptions are on your guidance for FY27 full year revenues there." (S018, call 2025-11-06).
- Straight answer: price, units, quarter and the evidence. The founder gave a blended price of about 1.25 to 1.50 lakh rupees and 7,000 to 8,000 units, backed by "a lot of reservations" (S018), and no order book or deposit figure.
- Evasive answer: the same target described in different words. On that call the 1,000 crore rupee figure was a median, then a range, then a minimum.
- Should worry a retail holder: "a ₹1,000 crore number to us seems to be a median guidance on what we can achieve" (S018, call 2025-11-06) is a hope until orders or cash deposits are shown. Unit counts for the same 100 crore rupees of revenue also moved inside the call (inferred from S018).

### Q1.6 Which profit do you mean?
- Ask: Is the promised profit operating, pre-tax or after-tax, and does it include incentives, one-off income or share-based pay?
- For: CEO, CFO.
- Analyst examples: "Just wanted to understand if, you're referring to, operating break-even or PAT level break-even and, is the target to achieve that, inclusive of UPI incentives" (S019, call 2024-07-19).
- Straight answer: the narrow definition stated up front. The founder answered "EBITDA break-even before ESOP cost and before adding UPI incentive" (S019).
- Evasive answer: leaving the definition unstated until asked, then narrowing it.
- Should worry a retail holder: a target that becomes easier to meet once a question forces the definition. A profit promise that excludes share-based pay and regulator-linked incentives is a lower bar than after-tax profit (inferred from S019).

### Q1.7 You give no guidance. What will you tell us instead?
- Ask: If you do not guide, how should a holder judge whether the year is going to plan?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "what is your AUM growth guidance and what are the levers" (S002, call 2025-07-15). "any target in the next year like to cross 3% or any guidance on that, sir?" (S003, call 2026-04-18).
- Straight answer: a stated policy plus an alternative anchor. On 2026-04-18 the CFO said "we usually refrain from giving the near-term guidance" (S003) and gave 3.25% to 3.5% over two to three years.
- Evasive answer: "we have basically a policy of not providing guidance as such" (S002) paired, on the same call, with a refusal to say where credit cost would peak or what return on assets to expect.
- Should worry a retail holder: no guidance together with no stated peak or target for the metric most under stress. An individual investor on the 2026-04-18 call was refused a near-term number (S003). Retail questions do not always get numbers.

### Q1.8 Does the order book or capacity actually support the guide?
- Ask: What share of the order book or capacity converts this year, and is the quoted capacity a theoretical figure?
- For: management, plus the company-management seat in a council.
- Analyst examples: "are you still confident that you will do revenue of Rs. 2,000 crores because the order book is not enough?" (S021, call 2024-08-13). "is it really possible to kind of handle 850 million tons because typically, the utilization stands at around 80% or so" (S017, call 2026-04-30).
- Straight answer: the planning rule and the proof point. On 2026-04-30 management said "Always capacity planning is done at 80%." (S017) so 1 billion tonnes is a planning figure, and cited one port at 94% in one month.
- Evasive answer: a headline order without its conversion. The 2024-08-13 reply (S021) pointed to new orders and a bid pipeline; the largest order pays over 12 years, so "Rs. 3,100 crore divided by 12 will be about Rs. 250- 260 crores of annual capacity charge" (S021).
- Should worry a retail holder: a long-dated contract used to support a one-year revenue guide (inferred from S021).

## 2. Earnings quality and accounting changes

### Q2.1 How much of this quarter is one-off, in rupees?
- Ask: Give the amount of the one-off or reversible item, not only the fact of it, and say which quarters it reverses in.
- For: CFO, investor relations.
- Analyst examples: "Can you call out for the one-off. How much was the one-off this quarter?" (S023, call 2026-01-22). "Is it somewhere around Rs. 550 crores to Rs. 600 crores in this quarter? Is that estimate right?" (S007, call 2025-11-13). "I wanted to understand what is the nature of this and whether both these one-offs are expected to reverse in the rest of FY'26?" (S012, call 2025-08-07).
- Straight answer: a rupee figure and a direction. Titan's CFO gave about 100 crore rupees of reversible gains and said "It has to reverse. there is no way." (S012, call 2025-08-07).
- Evasive answer: confirming a one-off exists without sizing it. On 2026-01-22 a colleague confirmed "a catch-up revenue" (S023) and the investor relations head gave growth excluding one-offs as "close to about 30-odd percent" (S023) against 73% reported, with no rupee figure.
- Should worry a retail holder: a first-ever profit in a loss-making division, helped by a payment that will not repeat, with the size withheld (S023, call 2026-01-22). A lender's CFO put an old-account recovery at "somewhere around Rs. 300 crores plus" (S007) when the analyst's own sum was 550 to 600.

### Q2.2 What do margins look like without other income and reversals?
- Ask: Strip other income, provision write-backs and exchange gains from the margin. What is left?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: (paraphrase of S011, call 2025-10-24) an analyst computed that without other income the EBITDA margin was below 23 per cent, and the CEO said he did not know how the analyst got that figure and cited a higher one. No other analyst on the 23 calls framed the question this way in a quotable line.
- Straight answer: management lists what the other income holds. Pennar's call named it: "The other income includes predominantly on account of exchange fluctuations, income from mutual funds, road tap incentives and collections of owed receivables, which we have written back in the past." (S015, call 2025-11-10).
- Evasive answer: restating the headline margin without engaging with the stripped figure (paraphrase of S011).
- Should worry a retail holder: a margin that holds up only because other income or a one-time reversal rose while gross margin fell (inferred from S011, S015, S020). Delhivery's total income was Rs 2,309 crores against services revenue of Rs 2,190 crores while profit after tax was Rs 10 crores (S020, call 2024-11-14).

### Q2.3 Why did profit grow slower, or faster, than operating profit?
- Ask: Walk operating profit to net profit: other income, interest, depreciation, tax, exceptional items.
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "what is that INR140 crores adjustment whether it is into the crate already, and that is to be separated, considering it is one-off" (S010, call 2025-11-05). "if you see, Rs. 90 crores of EBITDA at quarter level but the PAT which is being there that is just around Rs. 15 crores." (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Straight answer: a tax rate and a direction. The Sun Pharma CFO said the effective rate was 24.5% and "we expect it to be hovering around 25%" (S010) on 2025-11-05, but the 140 crore item was not explained on that call.
- Evasive answer: reframing losses as investment. On 2024-08-13 the founder said of subsidiary losses "we don't consider this as losses." (S021) and did not explain the gap between EBITDA and pre-tax profit.
- Should worry a retail holder: an exceptional item that is most of the headline profit and that no analyst asked about. Adani Enterprises' half-year pre-tax profit was INR 5,864 crore, of which INR 3,583 crore was exceptional (S022, call 2025-11-04, the note-taker's share is about 61%).

### Q2.4 Why is the item booked in that line?
- Ask: Which line of the profit and loss holds this gain or reversal, and why was it not shown as exceptional?
- For: CFO, and the auditor where the answer is unclear.
- Analyst examples: "why have you taken the reversal into the other expenses and not as an exceptional item?" (S020, call 2024-11-14). "where is the corresponding gain booked, which line item in the P&L, 2.35% of equity will probably reflect somewhere in the P&L?" (S004, call 2025-03-10).
- Straight answer: the booking rule. Delhivery's CFO: "Because it was charged to the other expenses when we made the provision." (S020). IndusInd's CFO said in most cases the net interest income line (S004).
- Evasive answer: a promised report rather than a line item.
- Should worry a retail holder: a reversal that lifts operating profit with no current business activity behind it. Delhivery's Rs 21 crore recovery on a COVID-era dispute is the example (S020, call 2024-11-14).

### Q2.5 Is the first number final, and when are restated numbers coming?
- Ask: Who validated the figure? Could it be larger? When do we get restated quarters?
- For: CEO, CFO, auditor.
- Analyst examples: "just so that we can understand what was accrued on the NII line." (S004, call 2025-03-10). "Or could this possibly be also higher if the scope were to be expanded?" (S004, call 2025-03-10). "Can the number be significantly different from this or it will be like 10-20 basis points here and there." (S004, call 2025-03-10).
- Straight answer: an outside review date. The CEO said the full information would come with Q4 results "or maybe prior to results itself" (S004).
- Evasive answer: "it's still not validated completely right now." (S004) and "very difficult to answer that question accurately right now" (S004).
- Should worry a retail holder: a first disclosed loss given only as a percentage of net worth, which management itself called "an approximate number, but not an exact number." (S004). SEBI's later interim order (allegation stage, 2025-05-28) worked the 2.35% of December 2024 net worth into about INR 1,529.88 crore (S054). Treat the first number as provisional.

### Q2.6 Why has disclosure shrunk?
- Ask: What did you stop reporting, why, and will you restore it? Why is segment profit not shown for a segment that is a large share of revenue?
- For: CEO, CFO, investor relations.
- Analyst examples: "And we have never seen less disclosures from Dabur." (S013, call 2025-07-31). "So, I am not clear as to why we don't reveal the same." (S015, call 2025-11-10). "we are not able to see the margins at the segment level. So, I would like to understand what are the margins at the segment level?" (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Straight answer: a number and a date for the restored disclosure. Pennar's MD said "I do get what you are saying that 45% merits separating it out. We will discuss this internally and get back to you." (S015) which is a promise, not a disclosure.
- Evasive answer: a competitive-sensitivity reason. Dabur's investor relations head said the decks were streamlined to "avoid excessive details that would pose any kind of competitive sensitivity" (S013) and the request to revert was declined.
- Should worry a retail holder: disclosure shrinking in the same quarter that headline growth weakens. Dabur reported 1.7% sales growth against about 7% without the seasonal portfolio (S013, call 2025-07-31).

### Q2.7 What is inside that headline number?
- Ask: Is the count cumulative? Does the ratio exclude a large item? Does the user number include merchants?
- For: CFO, investor relations.
- Analyst examples: "So, 20 million is the life-to-date customers that we have, right?" (S002, call 2025-07-15). "does it include the merchants as well as the users?" (S019, call 2024-07-19).
- Straight answer: the definition, once asked. The HDB CFO confirmed 20 million is a life-to-date count, not current borrowers (S002).
- Evasive answer: circular replies on whether the 7.8 crore monthly transaction users include merchants (inferred from S019).
- Should worry a retail holder: a growth story that rests on a count including closed accounts, or a return ratio that reaches peers only after an adjustment. HDB's return on assets was 1.94% and 2.02% after removing about 9,000 crore rupees of assets (S002, call 2025-07-15).

### Q2.8 Is the adjusted margin adjusted both ways?
- Ask: If you remove the restructuring cost, do you also remove the matching currency or other gains?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "On the restructuring charges, would we expect a similar sort of quantum of hit over the next couple of quarters as well?" (S009, call 2025-10-13).
- Straight answer: the CFO's own bridge: "Forex gain from INR depreciation gave us positive 56 basis points and restructuring expenses had an impact of negative 55 basis points." (S009).
- Evasive answer: the CFO gave no new figure for the next quarters and said only "We expect this to be slightly on the higher side for the full year based on visibility we have right now." (S009).
- Should worry a retail holder: a margin said to be back in range after adding back only the cost. Net of both items the two nearly cancel (S009, call 2025-10-13).

## 3. Cash, receivables and working capital

### Q3.1 Why is cash from operations below profit?
- Ask: Show cash from operations after working capital, not only operating profit before working capital changes.
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "cash flow from operations. I see it was down fairly significantly vis-a-vis last year's first half." (S022, call 2025-11-04). "So just -- I can see it is working capital. But if you could explain why our lower cash generated, please?" (S010, call 2025-11-05).
- Straight answer: the driver and a number. The Adani Enterprises CFO said a copper plant moving into operations changed inventory and working capital, but gave operating profit before working capital changes of about INR7,250 crore, not the operating cash flow (S022).
- Evasive answer: pointing to the measure that looks better. The CFO said "it will be better to look at operating profit before working capital changes." (S022).
- Should worry a retail holder: profit not turning into cash for more than a year. HCLTech's Q2 FY26 call showed the healthy version: "Operating Cash Flow to Net Income conversion is at 133%, and Free Cash Flow to Net Income is at 125%." (S009, call 2025-10-13).

### Q3.2 How many days of receivables, including unbilled?
- Ask: What are receivable days with unbilled work included, versus last year and versus peers?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "the working capital reduction that we have seen in this quarter, is that something you believe will continue, the 22 days odd which we are seeing now?" (S020, call 2024-11-14).
- Straight answer: a day count and a direction. Delhivery's CFO expected one to two days of improvement a year. HCLTech: "Our total DSO, including unbilled, is currently at 78 days" (S009, call 2025-10-13).
- Evasive answer: "We are hard at work to get some of this old stuff collected at the earliest." (S015, call 2025-11-10) with debtor days "higher by two days than usual" (S015) and no ageing.
- Should worry a retail holder: days rising while revenue grows, or a quarter-end build for one customer (inferred from S009, S015, S020).

### Q3.3 Is cash sitting in escrow or restricted?
- Ask: Which current assets are restricted, and why did other current assets jump?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "it's almost doubled since , March '25 from INR2,500 crores to almost INR5,000 crores" (S010, call 2025-11-05).
- Straight answer: the cause, in one line. The CFO: "This is mainly on account of the GxMDL settlement, which has gone into an escrow account." (S010).
- Evasive answer: a general reference to working capital.
- Should worry a retail holder: an asset line that grows without a cause the call gives (inferred from S010).

### Q3.4 Which old receivables or provisions could still reverse?
- Ask: What provisions sit against disputed or inactive receivables, and how much of profit comes from reversing them?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: no analyst on the 23 calls asked this directly; the evidence is in management's own statements. Delhivery's CFO described a reversal on a disputed COVID-era purchase: "Because it was charged to the other expenses when we made the provision." (S020, call 2024-11-14). Paytm's CFO: "we have taken a very conservative provisioning policy on any trade receivables, particularly from device merchants" (S019, call 2024-07-19).
- Straight answer: the amount, the history and the cash actually received.
- Evasive answer: counting a reversal inside operating results without saying so (S020).
- Should worry a retail holder: "any time you tighten provisions, you usually have a one off expense" (S019) followed by a quarter where the same line reverses. Check whether the one-off recurs.

### Q3.5 Is channel stock flattering this year's base?
- Ask: Did you destock last year, and does primary sales growth now track secondary sales?
- For: CEO, sales head.
- Analyst examples: "Now, is that largely exercise done and in FY '26, whatever secondary growth we get, will that largely be reflected in terms of primary growth?" (S013, call 2025-07-31).
- Straight answer: a yes with a level. The CEO: "We don't intend to do any more inventory correction going forward." (S013) with distributor stock at about 21 to 22 days.
- Evasive answer: attributing weak growth to season and weather only. "season played a spoilsport for us" (S013).
- Should worry a retail holder: a base distorted by last year's destocking and a seasonal excuse used again (S013, call 2025-07-31).

### Q3.6 What does the cash pile earn, and is profit mostly interest?
- Ask: How much of profit is treasury income, and does the cash earn the rate it should?
- For: CFO, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls frames it this way. Delhivery reported profit after tax of Rs 10 crores beside Rs 5,488 crores of cash, and the call did not say how much was treasury income (S020, call 2024-11-14). ABB India: "So we have, at this point of time, INR6,042 crores, but this is without the cash which we got from the sale of Robotics. If I include that, it is roughly round about INR7,600 crores." (S014, call 2026-05-08).
- Straight answer: cash with and without one-off disposal proceeds, as ABB's CFO gave it.
- Evasive answer: quoting total cash without the interest line.
- Should worry a retail holder: the Satyam confession recorded "recorded non-existent bank balances and accrued interest, understated liabilities and overstated debtors position" (S041, SEBI order 2023-11-30). Cash that earns less than it should is the check that point suggests (inferred from S041).

## 4. Debt and funding

### Q4.1 Why is debt not coming down?
- Ask: How much of EBITDA goes to interest, what is the debt-reduction plan, and is a raise coming?
- For: CFO, founder.
- Analyst examples: "why are we not speaking on the lines of reducing our debt as significant portion of our EBIDTA is basically going in servicing our debt obligation?" (S021, call 2024-08-13). "Are we doing something to bring down the debt immediately? And are we considering a QIP or a rights issue?" (S015, call 2025-11-10).
- Straight answer: a figure and a date. Gensol's founder said gross debt fell from Rs 1,397 crores at March 2024 to Rs 1,363 crores, about 2% (S021, call 2024-08-13). Pennar's MD said "Our debt equity is right now 0.8, and we intend to bring that down." (S015).
- Evasive answer: "I would not be able to share anything on corporate actions as of right now." (S015).
- Should worry a retail holder: a small fall in debt beside a large raise, with no interest figure given. Read borrowings and finance cost in the filing (inferred from S015, S021).

### Q4.2 Is the leverage target a ceiling?
- Ask: Is the stated net debt to EBITDA figure a hard cap, and what could break it?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "is the 2.5x target, is it a ceiling? Or is it something that you aspire to be to like optimally to gear your balance sheet?" (S017, call 2026-04-30).
- Straight answer: the second reply, which turned a guidance number into a flexible ceiling: "2.5x is the ceiling but even if I go 3" (S017).
- Evasive answer: the first reply, "2.5x is our guidance" (S017).
- Should worry a retail holder: a cap that management describes as breakable for days or weeks. The CFO said an acquisition could take leverage to 3.2 or 3.3 times briefly (S017, call 2026-04-30).

### Q4.3 Which covenants are breached and by how much?
- Ask: List each covenant breach, the lender, the amount, and whether a waiver was obtained.
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "Would it possible to quantify, just even if a rough estimate." (S006, call 2024-11-05).
- Straight answer: a count and an amount per facility.
- Evasive answer: "We will share it separately." (S006) for the bank term loan, while for the debenture breach the CFO said "there is only one covenant breach on NCD, which is a very small amount." (S006).
- Should worry a retail holder: a breach tied to gross NPA at a lender whose regulator had just halted a subsidiary's lending (S006, call 2024-11-05).

### Q4.4 Where is the new debt used and at what rate?
- Ask: Which loans, for what use, at what interest rate, in which currency?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "just want to understand where exactly are we using this debt? And at what, let's say, interest rates have we raised this sort of loans?" (S015, call 2025-11-10).
- Straight answer: a use and a rate per loan. The CFO said "in India, we are at around 10% in terms of the finance cost" (S015).
- Evasive answer: two speakers giving different US rates, "5-ish" (S015) then the MD's correction "7, I believe one should include." (S015).
- Should worry a retail holder: long-term debt defined differently on one call, about Rs 120 to Rs 130 crores by the MD against near Rs 247 crores quoted by an analyst, not reconciled (S015, call 2025-11-10).

### Q4.5 What is the size, price and timing of the raise, and who subscribes?
- Ask: For a rights issue, QIP, warrants or convertible bond: size, price, staging, use, promoter share.
- For: CFO, board.
- Analyst examples: "And then how should we think about leverage and rights issue, timing, use of proceeds? If you can just help us understand that, please?" (S022, call 2025-11-04). "We raised money through FCCB last year. I think we raised only 10% of what was approved by the Board." (S023, call 2026-01-22). "we are allocating roughly Rs. 1 crore warrants, but so far we have only allocated 60 lakh warrants" (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Straight answer: "We have deferred the next drawdowns" (S023) until there is "full visibility on the deployment of those proceeds" (S023).
- Evasive answer: asked twice, the Adani Enterprises CFO explained that about INR20,000 crore of promoter-family loans would convert to equity and never answered timing or staging (S022).
- Should worry a retail holder: a raise that closes below plan with a vague reason ("given the market conditions" (S021)), or a rights issue whose price and terms are not published (S022, call 2025-11-04).

### Q4.6 Has a lender or rating agency acted, and when was it disclosed?
- Ask: Any default beyond 30 days, rating cut, or agency doubt about papers, and the date each was filed.
- For: CFO, board, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. The question rests on the rule and an order. Rule, master circular dated 2023-07-11: "Such disclosure shall be made promptly, but not later than 24 hours from the 30th day of such default." (S053). Order, confirmatory, 2025-07-30, prima facie stage: both rating agencies cut a company to D in March 2025, and one said "certain documents shared by GEL with ICRA, on its debt servicing track record, were apparently falsified" (S038).
- Straight answer: dated filings in the exchange record that match the lender's dates.
- Evasive answer: a no-default statement that outlasts the first default. In the Gensol order the first loan default was on 2024-12-31 while no-default statements continued (S038, allegation stage).
- Should worry a retail holder: lender and rating actions leading the stock story. The order records that promoter denials without documents did not hold up at that stage; the forensic audit and final findings were pending (S038).

## 5. Related parties and group companies

### Q5.1 Which small private firms received large sums, and who stands behind them?
- Ask: Name every counterparty that received loans, advances or deposits above a threshold, its net worth, its owners and the end use of the money.
- For: board, audit committee, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. Source: SEBI final order, 2025-09-18, on Hindenburg allegations about two small firms (S039). The show cause notice alleged conduit routing; the final order found the allegations "not established" (S039) because the firms were not related parties under the rules then in force and all loans were repaid with interest before the probe.
- Straight answer: a related-party note with the counterparty's ownership and end use. The order described the intermediaries as having "very insignificant net worth and most of their profits have been generated out of" (S039).
- Evasive answer: "Adicorp is not a related party, and transactions with Adicorp are not" (S055) as the group's public line, in a case where two CFOs also confirmed in recorded statements that the loans were not disclosed in the related-party notes (S055).
- Should worry a retail holder: the order rests on the wording of the rules at the time, not on a clean bill for the structure. The 2021 amendment, effective 2022-04-01 with a glide path to 2023-04-01, would now reach such routes (S039, S055). Allegation versus finding: the allegations were not established.

### Q5.2 Does the annual report's related-party list match what the audit committee approved?
- Ask: Compare the names in the committee's omnibus approvals with the annual report list.
- For: audit committee chair, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI order dated 2026-05-12 (first-instance, appeal status unchecked): entities were "disclosed as related parties in Audit Committee Meetings while taking omnibus approval in FYs 2019-20 and 2020-21, however, they were not disclosed as related parties in the related party list of FRL" (S043).
- Straight answer: one reconciled list, with the approval date beside each name.
- Evasive answer: describing an entity as unrelated because no shares are held, when control sits elsewhere. The order found seven entities related in substance through the chairman (S043, finding).
- Should worry a retail holder: counterparties whose "revenue from operations of these companies (except FMNL) was linked to the operations of FRL." (S043).

### Q5.3 What was the approval ceiling, and what actually moved?
- Ask: What ceiling did the audit committee approve, was it reviewed each quarter, and how much moved?
- For: audit committee chair, CFO.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI order dated 2026-05-12: the audit committee "had granted omnibus approval to FRL to the extent of Rs. 300 Crore towards a transaction which makes it obliged to make payment to FCRPL" (S043) while Rs 3,148.58 crore went to one related party, more than 10% of consolidated turnover (finding on disclosure and approval; fraud and diversion allegations were mostly not established).
- Straight answer: the quarterly review minutes and the running balance against the ceiling.
- Evasive answer: relying on an ordinary-course carve-out. The order read the rules as having no such exception: "None of these requirements create any exception regarding transactions in ordinary course of business." (S043).
- Should worry a retail holder: flows many times the approved ceiling. Rule as applied to 2019 to 2021 events: omnibus approvals must be reviewed at least quarterly (S043). Rules change; read the current text.

### Q5.4 Will the product be sold or leased to a related entity?
- Ask: Which customers or lessees are group or promoter-linked, and what share of sales do they take?
- For: founder, CEO, board.
- Analyst examples: "do you want to lease them as well in your BluSmart or other ventures or others, let us say barring BluSmart, there are other companies also who are into leasing, so what is your plan around that?" (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Straight answer: a yes or no on the named party.
- Evasive answer: the founder spoke about OEM financing arms in general and did not mention the named party, so the reply neither confirmed nor denied any dealing (S021).
- Should worry a retail holder: a small counterparty that lives on the company. In SEBI's confirmatory order of 2025-07-30, supplier sales to the company were "55% and 91% of total sales of Wellray during the said FYs, respectively" (S038), while the same supplier held company stock. This is a prima facie finding; the forensic audit was pending.

### Q5.5 Why is the subsidiary's margin so far above the parent's?

- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "is this is a sustainable margin profile of the specialty molecules here, or there is something more to this surge in the margin?" (S016, call 2026-02-09). "there is some investment into subsidiaries, which is showing up in the cash flows amounting to almost like INR3,400 crores. Can you explain what is it all about?" (S010, call 2025-11-05).
- Straight answer: a stated basis. Navin's CFO: "there is a proper transfer pricing arms-length association between these two companies to transfer the costs between the two sort of entities" (S016) and "I do not think the EBITDA is 65% north." (S016).
- Evasive answer: an investment line explained only as the Checkpoint deal, without bridging consideration to the cash-flow line (S010).
- Should worry a retail holder: a large share of group profit in an unlisted or lower-overhead entity. Check the segment and subsidiary notes (inferred from S010, S016).

### Q5.6 Why was a gain booked on a sale to your own subsidiary, and when was cash received?
- Ask: Compare the gain with book value and with cash actually received, year by year.
- For: CFO, audit committee, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI order dated 2026-05-29, a review that set aside a lenient adjudication (appeal status unchecked): "The record indicates that the OMS business, having a net book value of about Rs.77.08 crore, was transferred for a consideration of Rs.2,000 crore." (S044).
- Straight answer: a schedule of cash received against the receivable.
- Evasive answer: a waived due date. "SGSL had failed to make payment of the OMS consideration by the completion date, whereupon SEL waived such default" (S044).
- Should worry a retail holder: the order found only Rs 700 crore was paid over FY2015 to FY2017 and Rs 1,300 crore was cleared by same-day group money moves in March 2017 (finding on review, S044). Net worth was shown as Rs 2,663.96 crore against Rs 741.04 crore without the deal.

### Q5.7 How much brand fee or royalty goes to the parent?

- For: board, audit committee.

(S045). IiAS (2023-24 edition) benchmark: "median royalty pay-outs for 30 companies in the Indian market amounted to 1.8% of sales and 10.5% of pre-tax pre-royalty profits in FY21" (S046).

- Evasive answer: an unlimited or open-ended approval.
- Should worry a retail holder: royalty above 5% of consolidated turnover without a shareholder vote. S046 reports the 5% test as in force in 2023; check the current text.

## 6. Promoter: pledge, selling, succession, control

### Q6.1 How much of the promoter stake is pledged, and why?
- Ask: What percentage of the promoter holding is encumbered, which lender, what is the loan for, and what is the plan to reduce it?
- For: promoter, board.
- Analyst examples: "what the promoters are doing to reduce the pledge of shares because as I can see, as the company is growing the percentage of pledge is also increasing." (S021, call 2024-08-13).
- Straight answer: a percentage, a purpose and a dated step. The founder said the pledge secures long-term lender debt and "we expect that will taper down over the 3-4 year time period" (S021), with no percentage and no dated step by the promoters.
- Evasive answer: a purpose without a number.
- Should worry a retail holder: a rising pledge in a growing company. Rule, SEBI FAQ updated to 2022-03-30: reasons must be given if encumbrance reaches "either 50% of their shareholding in the company or 20% of the total share capital of the company" (S034). In the March 2025 shareholding, promoters had pledged 75.74 lakh shares and held 35.125% (SEBI order 2025-07-30, S038). Pledges were invoked in April 2025 (S038).

### Q6.2 What happens if the lender invokes the pledge?
- Ask: At what price does the lender act, what share would change hands, and who then holds control?
- For: promoter, board.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI's insider-trading FAQ (dated 2024-12-31): "Hence, trading would include creation/invocation/revocation of pledge." (S029). A lender's invocation is shown as "the transaction can be represented as invocation in Form C." (S029).
- Straight answer: the trigger price, the quantity and the cover.
- Evasive answer: silence on invocation terms.
- Should worry a retail holder: pledges invoked while the stock is falling (SEBI order 2025-07-30, S038). Also note that a pledge placed through a family holding vehicle may not show in the listed company's own pledge column, as in the Satyam orders (S040, order 2018-11-02).

### Q6.3 Why have insiders been selling, and was it under a public plan?
- Ask: Who sold in the quarters before bad news, under what trading plan, and did anyone buy?
- For: promoter, directors, senior officers, compliance officer.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. Rule, SEBI FAQ: "a cooling off period of 120 calendar days is mandated from the date of public disclosure of the trading plan till the commencement of trading by the insider" (S029). Interim order of 2025-05-28 (allegation stage, ex parte, no finding): "no new shares have been bought during the UPSI period" (S054).
- Straight answer: a filed trading plan, dates inside the allowed windows, and a stated reason.
- Evasive answer: no plan on record. The interim order noted none was filed with the exchanges for those years (S054).
- Should worry a retail holder: steady family selling while the company reports growth. The SAT finding recorded that "None of the appellants have placed any material on record to suggest there were compelling reasons" (S040, order 2018-11-02).

### Q6.4 Who funds the promoter's side of a preferential issue or warrants?
- Ask: Where did the promoter's money come from, and did the company or a counterparty supply it?
- For: promoter, audit committee, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. IiAS 2023-24: "In a warrants issue, 25% of the conversion price is paid up front, with an option to convert the warrants into equity shares anytime during the next 18 months." (S046). SEBI interim and confirmatory orders (2025-07-30, prima facie): the company supplied money, through layers, to a promoter entity that then subscribed in a 2022 preferential issue (S038).
- Straight answer: the funding source named and documented.
- Evasive answer: no answer on source of funds.
- Should worry a retail holder: a round trip. In the Future Retail order (2026-05-12) a Rs 412.35 crore trail was accepted as starting from the promoter group's own debenture money, so diversion was not established, and the transfers were still undisclosed and unapproved (S043, finding).

### Q6.5 Who has left the key seats, and what reason was given?
- Ask: Which CFO, chair, auditor or independent director left in the past two years, and what do the filed reasons say?
- For: board, CEO.
- Analyst examples: "the CFO had resigned a couple of months back, is there any link to the fact that there has been a transaction around this where he was aware and hence he was not comfortable?" (S004, call 2025-03-10). "The Board has approved for 3 years but you've got only 1 year?" (S004, call 2025-03-10).
- Straight answer: the CEO said the resignation was public and "I don't think it is linked to only this transaction" (S004) and confirmed the CFO was aware of the transaction.
- Evasive answer: attributing the shorter term to the regulator's view of leadership.
- Should worry a retail holder: an exit that predates a bad-news disclosure, or an interim senior finance officer with no date for a permanent one. AU Small Finance Bank's call on 2026-01-20 was led by an interim CFO (S008). On the HDFC Bank call of 2026-04-18 the chair's exit and a legal review were raised only in opening remarks and no analyst asked about them (S001).

### Q6.6 In an open offer, is the price just the formula minimum?
- Ask: Did the outgoing promoter receive anything beyond the offer price, and what did the independent directors say in writing?
- For: acquirer, target board, independent directors.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI takeover FAQ (updated to 2022-03-30): "no Promoter or shareholder can be paid any extra price, by whatever name it may be called." (S034). The target's independent directors must give "reasoned recommendations on such open offer" (S034).
- Straight answer: the formula inputs, the price and a reasoned recommendation.
- Evasive answer: a recommendation with no reasons.
- Should worry a retail holder: an offer at the bare minimum for a business the independent directors do not value on paper (inferred from S034). The FAQ also says the primary purpose of an open offer is an exit option for shareholders.

### Q6.7 What does the stock exchange record show about disclosures that did not come?
- Ask: Were promoter and insider filings made on time, and what do the gaps in them say?
- For: compliance officer, board.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI insider-trading FAQ (dated 2024-12-31): "the disclosure of the incremental transactions after any disclosure under this sub-regulation shall be made when the transactions effected after the prior disclosure cross the threshold" (S029).
- Straight answer: dated filings matching the trades.
- Evasive answer: a missing or late filing, which is itself a flag (inferred from S029).
- Should worry a retail holder: sales just before results that sit inside a closed trading window (S029) or a gift of shares not disclosed. SEBI treats a gift as a trade (S029).

## 7. Capital allocation: capex payback, acquisitions, dilution, dividends, buybacks

### Q7.1 What is the payback on this capex?

- Evasive answer: "this is a license to grow capacity or license to dream capacity" (S016), and no ballpark when asked again.

### Q7.3 What did you pay for the acquisition, and what does it add?
- Ask: Price, enterprise value, revenue and profit acquired, and how it reconciles to the cash-flow statement.
- For: CEO, CFO.
- Analyst examples: "And what was the acquisition price, the consideration that you paid for this?" (S015, call 2025-11-10). "there is some investment into subsidiaries, which is showing up in the cash flows amounting to almost like INR3,400 crores. Can you explain what is it all about?" (S010, call 2025-11-05).
- Straight answer: a price that ties to the statements. Sun Pharma gave only a partial version: its IR head corrected the analyst: "the Checkpoint consideration is not USD500 million" (S010). It was less, USD355 million plus as disclosed. The call never bridged that to the USD471 million intangible or the cash-flow line.
- Evasive answer: Pennar's MD said "It was about $14 million including at an enterprise value." (S015) beside earlier "our total capital was about Rs. 200 crores" (S015), while the cash-flow note showed Rs 212.22 crores. The call did not reconcile them.
- Should worry a retail holder: a purchase price that is a large multiple of the sales acquired. In (paraphrase of S011, call 2025-10-24) an analyst said a brand with about Rs 100 crore of sales cost $50 million, and the CFO said it added little to growth.

### Q7.4 Is the acquired business only part-year in the base?

- For: CFO, investor relations.

- Evasive answer: leaving the part-year effect unsaid. The call said "But in the annual announcements, we counted only the quarter 4 of the Australia." (S017).
- Should worry a retail holder: international EBITDA growth of 180% that includes a one-quarter acquisition, read as organic (S017, call 2026-04-30).

### Q7.5 How much dilution is in the plan?
- Ask: Add ESOPs, QIP, preferential issue, warrants and rights. What is the total new share count and who gets it?
- For: board, CFO.
- Analyst examples: "Are we doing something to bring down the debt immediately? And are we considering a QIP or a rights issue?" (S015, call 2025-11-10).
- Straight answer: a total and a split by allottee. The IiAS expectation (2023-24 edition) is "IiAS expects the dilution to be restricted to less than 5%." (S046) for ESOPs.
- Evasive answer: "I would not be able to share anything on corporate actions as of right now." (S015).
- Should worry a retail holder: frequent equity dilution to fund operations that do not generate cash (inferred from S045, S046). IiAS reported recommending against 66.0% of ESOP resolutions in calendar 2022 (S046, the note-taker's arithmetic, 192 of 291).

### Q7.6 Is the dividend or buyback paid from free cash flow or from debt?

(S009, call 2025-10-13). The note-taker adds that a payout near 96% of net income is safe only while free cash flow stays above net income.

- Evasive answer: payout without the free cash flow figure.
- Should worry a retail holder: IiAS names the flag as payout while "The company has defaulted on any of its debt obligations" (S046). Its buyback test (2023-24 edition) is "Ensure that the buy-back amount is 25% or less of the aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves of the company" (S046).

### Q7.7 Is research or marketing being cut to hold margin?

"how do you see our R&D expenses go up considering we will be competing head-on with a lot of the rich world OEMs in their own backyard?" (S009, call 2025-10-13).

## 8. Lenders: asset quality and provisioning

### Q8.1 How much of the deposit surge is temporary?
- Ask: How much is volatile, callable or bulk, and what is the gap between period-end and average deposits?
- For: CFO, treasurer.
- Analyst examples: "on the deposits, how much of this is transitory in nature? And how much of this it can sustain?" (S001, call 2026-04-18). "So, this time on a large part of your deposit growth has come from non-callable bulk deposits." (S008, call 2026-01-20).
- Straight answer: a share. The HDFC Bank CEO said about 15% of total deposits is volatile (S001, call 2026-04-18).
- Evasive answer: AU's reply used a different word from the analyst's. "I don't think there is any increased reliance on bulk callable deposits" (S008) answered callable, when the question was non-callable.
- Should worry a retail holder: a year-end surge with no named driver. The HDFC Bank CFO named liquidity, more customers and corporate loans up 13%, but no single driver for the last two weeks (S001).

### Q8.2 Where does credit cost peak?
- Ask: What is steady-state credit cost, and what would make you wrong?
- For: CFO, chief risk officer.
- Analyst examples: "at what levels do you think it's going to peak out?" (S002, call 2025-07-15). "is it fair to say that the credit cost may touch double digit in the next couple of quarters for Asirvad?" (S006, call 2024-11-05). "And just any comments on credit cost for next year?" (S008, call 2026-01-20).
- Straight answer: a range with a reason. Bajaj Finance's CFO tied improvement to bounce rates in June and July and about two quarters to normalise (S005, call 2024-07-23).
- Evasive answer: "I wouldn't want to double guess something" (S002); in the AU call the moderator moved to the next caller and the credit cost question was not answered (S008).
- Should worry a retail holder: credit cost rising five quarters in a row with no peak given (S002, call 2025-07-15), or a group guide of 2% kept while one segment ran near 7% (S006).

### Q8.3 Is the better credit cost from recoveries, write-backs or guarantee cover?
- Ask: Separate recoveries, write-backs, utilised provisions and guarantee cover from fresh slippages.
- For: CFO, chief risk officer.
- Analyst examples: "was there any benefit of recoveries, and it should be managed at this level of 1.3% odd, or the steady state MFI credit cost should be relatively higher?" (S008, call 2026-01-20). "is this against any specific exposure? Is this in the run-up to ECL or any more color on that?" (S003, call 2026-04-18).
- Straight answer: AU's credit officer said the fall came from lower slippages, higher guarantee cover and "a marginal amount of the previous provisioning" (S008). A guarantee scheme covered 83% of the microfinance book.
- Evasive answer: YES Bank's CFO called a INR 340 crore one-time provision prudent and "in no way reflect an underlying credit issue or an impairment" (S003) in the same quarter as a INR 288 crore write-back and INR 450 crore of recoveries.
- Should worry a retail holder: ROA that depends on write-backs from asset reconstruction securities. YES Bank's recoveries were above INR 1,550 crore in FY26 and guided to INR 800 to 1,000 crore in FY27 (S003, call 2026-04-18).

### Q8.4 Do stage 2, stage 3 and coverage move together?
- Ask: Show stage 1, 2 and 3 coverage year on year, the stage 2 movement and the collection efficiency.
- For: CFO, chief risk officer.
- Analyst examples: "what is kind of going into the ECL model for it to churn out lower provision covers on your Stage 1 and Stage 2 loans?" (S002, call 2025-07-15). "Are there markers that you can identify for these stress apart from this one quarter? Is it multiple loans for the customer who are more prone to these lower recoveries?" (S005, call 2024-07-23). "So are LGD at 50%+ or are we carrying excess provision?" (S002, call 2025-07-15).
- Straight answer: Bajaj Finance gave numbers on multiple-loan customers (no unsecured loan fell from 63% to 58% since March 2020), though the CEO and CFO gave different gap figures in one answer (S005).
- Evasive answer: "we've calibrated it based on what we believe are the right levels to carry." (S002) to the excess-provision question.
- Should worry a retail holder: default rates look fine while customers keep moving to stage 2 (S005), or coverage falls while the macro view is weak.

### Q8.5 Is the fast-growing line tiny, and what does it do to the mix?
- Ask: What share of the book and of incremental growth is the new segment, and how seasoned is it?
- For: CEO, CFO.
- Analyst examples: "Yes, but if you look at the incremental growth, just 2%-2.5% of your open book is contributing north of 12% of the incremental growth. So, I was just asking on that." (S008, call 2026-01-20).
- Straight answer: a mix number. The CFO said a book up 50% in a year will contribute oversized growth (S008).
- Evasive answer: "Yes, maybe." (S008) from investor relations.
- Should worry a retail holder: a segment that grew 52% from a base of about Rs 3,000 crores in a Rs 130,000 crore book and drives a disproportionate share of new lending (S008, call 2026-01-20, the figures are the note-taker's reading).

### Q8.6 What is the loan to value, and what if the collateral price falls?
- Ask: Give average and fresh-disbursal loan to value, auction volumes, and the effect of a price fall on non-performing loans.
- For: CEO, CFO.
- Analyst examples: "is it a conscious decision from our end, given the heated prices right now?" (S007, call 2025-11-13). "we are still comfortable going up to 75% LTV on the current price?" (S006, call 2024-11-05).
- Straight answer: three bases named. Muthoot's CFO gave "the 43% you see is the average LTV." (S007) and about 70% to 71% on daily disbursal; the analyst had said 57%.
- Evasive answer: the CEO said the industry association was taking the matter to the regulator (S006) and did not say what limit the lender was using.
- Should worry a retail holder: low auctions because the price is high. Muthoot's CFO: "Rs. 10 crores, very miniscule because the gold price is there." (S007). A price fall reverses that (inferred from S007).

### Q8.7 Is interest income inflated by clearing old bad loans?
- Ask: How much of the yield is interest booked on cash recovery from non-performing accounts?
- For: CFO.
- Analyst examples: "when I look at the Stage 3 reduction, last quarter, it was Rs. 700 crore, this year, it was around Rs. 100 crore. Despite that, our interest income recovery this time seemed very strong." (S007, call 2025-11-13).
- Straight answer: the CFO's rule: "As you know, on NPA accounts, we do not recognize the interest." (S007). Core yield after removing the one-off was 18% to 18.5%.
- Evasive answer: "that is because we give more time to the customers to take the loan." (S007) as the reason accounts stay non-performing.
- Should worry a retail holder: a classification that looks like a choice, plus an absolute claim such as no non-performing loan ever causing a loss, beside a sale of non-performing gold loans to an asset reconstruction company the same year (S007).

### Q8.8 Did the regulator's own assessment differ from the bank's?
- Ask: Was there any divergence in asset classification or provisioning found by the regulator, and was it disclosed?
- For: CFO, board, auditor.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. ## 11. Retail-specific traps: IPO and grey market premium, F&O, tips and finfluencers

### Q11.1 How much of the IPO is fresh money, and why are the sellers selling?
- Ask: Split the issue into fresh issue and offer for sale, and say what the fresh money will do.
- For: promoter, merchant banker, board.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI investor booklet (undated): "In this kind of an issue, the funds of the investors will go to such sellers of the shares and not to the company." (S024). One 2024 listing: "Every rupee went to the sellers through an offer for sale (OFS) , in which existing holders sell their shares rather than the company issuing new ones." (S051, October 2026).
- Straight answer: a use of funds in the offer document.
- Evasive answer: a vague use of money or thin promoter detail (inferred from S024).
- Should worry a retail holder: offers for sale were 61.2% of about Rs 2.37 lakh crore raised from January 2025 to August 2026 (S051, October 2026, an editorial; figures are perishable).

### Q11.2 What did each seller pay, compared with the issue price?
- Ask: Read the weighted average acquisition cost of each selling holder in the offer document.
- For: promoter, selling investors, merchant banker.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. The red herring prospectus put cost at "₹23.52 for AceVector, the promoter formerly known as Snapdeal, and ₹30.87 for SoftBank's SB Investment Holdings (UK)" (S051), against an issue price of Rs 108 in that example.
- Straight answer: cost and issue price side by side.
- Evasive answer: leaving the cost to the fine print.
- Should worry a retail holder: sellers exiting at a multiple of their cost with no fresh issue. The source says "None of this proves manipulation." (S051). It is a pointer to ask, not a finding.

### Q11.3 Is the grey market premium a signal?
- Ask: What does an unofficial quote tell me that the offer document does not?
- For: the retail holder; no company can answer it.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. "A GMP is an unregulated, unaudited quote from an informal market, not a forecast" (S051). A vendor analysis of 294 listings in 2026 found "A GMP above 25% never led to a listing below the issue price in 2026 (0 of 55 IPOs)." (S052, post dated 2026-10-28).
- Straight answer: GMP as sentiment, in the same breath as its error. "The typical miss is 8.6 percentage points." (S052).
- Evasive answer: quoting GMP as the expected listing gain.
- Should worry a retail holder: "so a thin premium is closer to a coin toss than a signal" (S052). The author sells the data used, so the numbers are unaudited, and the study forecasts only listing price, not what the stock does later.

### Q11.4 What happened to buyers on listing day and a week later?
- Ask: Compare allotment price, opening price and a later price for the same issue.
- For: the retail holder.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. One 2024 IPO: "A retail applicant fortunate enough to receive one lot of 138 shares paid ₹14,904 and could have sold at the opening for about ₹32,430." (S051). SEBI's 2024 study of 144 IPOs found "individual investors sold 50% of their allotted shares by value within a week of listing, and 67.6% when listing returns exceeded 20%." (S051).
- Straight answer: who got what price at what time.
- Evasive answer: citing oversubscription as proof of value.
- Should worry a retail holder: "oversubscription measures how many people wanted in at a given price, not whether that price was sound or whether the demand was genuine" (S051). That issue was 168.35 times subscribed, opened at Rs 235 and closed at Rs 82.52 on 2026-10-19 (S051, one case, not a sample).

### Q11.5 Why trade options on this stock?
- Ask: What odds do individual traders have, and how does my plan beat them?
- For: the retail holder; also any seat suggesting a derivatives play.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI study, August 2026, sample of 15 brokers: "Around 91% of individual traders incurred net losses during FY25-FY26" (S030). "Longer trading experience was not associated with improved outcomes" (S031).
- Straight answer: the base rate stated before any strategy.
- Evasive answer: a story about one winning trade.
- Should worry a retail holder: "Individual traders incurred net losses in almost every quarter, regardless of market direction." (S030). For FY26 alone a magazine report of the studies gave 87.7% of individual traders losing, with a net loss of about Rs 91,685 crore (S048, 2026-08-20). The studies are descriptive and do not establish cause.

### Q11.6 Is the position size out of line with what you own?
- Ask: Compare derivatives turnover with the equity portfolio, and check whether there is any underlying stock at all.
- For: the retail holder.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI August 2026: "about 43 lakh traders (35%) had no underlying equity portfolio at the end of FY26, indicating participation in derivatives without any cash equity holdings" (S030). "Traders below 30 years generated derivatives turnover of about 93 times their equity portfolio, compared with around 20 times among traders above 50 years." (S031).
- Straight answer: turnover as a multiple of holdings, and loss as a share of capital.
- Evasive answer: leverage described as hedging.
- Should worry a retail holder: "The incidence of losses declined consistently with portfolio size, suggesting that traders with larger equity portfolios experienced comparatively lower loss rates." (S030). These are FY25 to FY26 figures and perishable.

### Q11.7 Does the strategy that wins often also wipe out capital?
- Ask: What is the average loss on the losing days, and how big is the tail?
- For: the retail holder; any seat promoting option selling.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI (August 2026): "Overall, the data indicates a risk asymmetry in options selling, where relatively high win rates may mask a tail-risk profile that can result in significant financial losses." (S031).
- Straight answer: win rate and average loss together.
- Evasive answer: win rate alone.
- Should worry a retail holder: the study's group record for FY26 had sellers losing less often (44%) but losing about Rs 51.7 lakh each time, against about Rs 1.3 lakh for buyers (S031, a random sample of about 5,050 traders).

### Q11.8 Who pays the person giving the tip, and are they registered?
- Ask: Is the adviser registered, what does the post carry (registration number, grievance line), and is the praise paid for by the product?
- For: the tipster, the broker or fund that works with them.
- Analyst examples: none on the 23 calls. SEBI board memo, July 2024, a proposal not an enacted rule: "many of them are effectively enticing their followers/investors/prospective investors to purchase financial products, services, or securities in return for undisclosed compensation from producers or platforms." (S033). SEBI investor booklet (undated): "Do not rely on making your investment decisions on hot tips as a person who wants to offload securities which may not be marketable may be indulging in it." (S024).
- Straight answer: a SEBI registration number the holder can look up. SEBI's 2025-26 annual report describes a tool that checks the bank account, UPI ID and QR code of registered intermediaries (S037, August 2026).
- Evasive answer: assured or steady returns regardless of market conditions (paraphrase of S025).
- Should worry a retail holder: the memo says some influencers are genuine educators, so the test is whether they advise, recommend securities or claim returns (S033). Check what the final SEBI notification said; the memo does not.

- A direct answer is not a true answer. The labels record whether the question was answered, not whether the answer was right.
