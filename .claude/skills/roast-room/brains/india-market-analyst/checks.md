# Checks: India Market Analyst

The red flags, checks and bars a retail investor can run on a listed Indian company, merged across 55 public sources (23 earnings calls dated 2024 to 2026, SEBI and NFRA documents and orders, the LODR master circular, the Kotak committee report, IiAS voting guidelines, five recent articles). Entries are most-used first inside each section.

## How to read an entry

- **Class.** REGULATION means a rule in a regulator or statute document, as dated. GUIDANCE means a recommendation, FAQ, proposal, company target or an adjudicator's reading. EXAMPLE means a case, a worked figure or one company's snapshot.
- **Date** is the date of the document or call. Rules change. Every rule here must be re-read in its current text before use.
- **Sources** counts the distinct source ids that state or apply the check.
- A check that fires is a prompt to read the filing. It is not a finding about the company, and none of this is a buy, sell or wait call.
- Regulator orders keep allegation and finding apart. An interim order is an allegation backed by early evidence. A final order is a finding after a hearing. Appeal status is unchecked for every order here.
- S011 and S025 forbid quoting. They are paraphrased and carry no quotation marks.

## A. Disclosure and LODR rules

### A1 Earnings call transcripts must be filed with the exchanges
- **Check:** Look for the company's filed transcript and read it as a filed document, with its cover letter and its date.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** letters from 2024-07-26 to 2026-05-15. **Number:** letters came 5 to 8 days after the call in the cases where both dates are given (S007, S019, S020, S021, S023). **Sources:** 14 (S001, S003, S005, S007, S008, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S019, S020, S021, S023).
- **Quote:** "pursuant to Regulation 30 of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, the transcript of the Earnings Call with analysts and investors held on" (S001)
- **Red flag:** The letters cite different rules (Regulation 30, 46 or 46(2)(oa)). The sources give no deadline. A late or missing transcript is a question. Check the current regulation for the time limit.

### A2 A filed transcript is edited, not a verbatim record
- **Check:** Treat a transcript as the company's edited version of the call.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-10-13 (S009), 2026-01-22 (S023). **Sources:** 3 (S009, S014, S023).
- **Quote:** "The transcript has been edited wherever required for clarity, correctness of data or transcription error." (S023)
- **Quote:** "This document has been edited for readability and is not a verbatim record" (S009)
- **Red flag:** Wording on the page may differ from the audio. Take no exact figure from a transcript without the filed results.

### A3 Figures in a call are often garbled or contradicted inside the same call
- **Check:** Match every figure that matters to the filed results or deck. Look for two speakers giving two numbers for one thing.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** calls from 2024-07 to 2026-05. **Number:** HDFC Bank mobilisation printed as "INR 3.9 2.7 lakh crores" (S001); a bank's personal-loan slippages given as 180, 186 and about 160 crore (S003); revenue growth 22.04% and 21.26% on one call (S015). **Sources:** 9 (S001, S003, S005, S007, S008, S010, S015, S016, S018).
- **Quote:** "This is for the quarter ended September 30th, 2026." (S015)
- **Red flag:** Units mixed between INR million, crore and USD (S010, S023). Dates slipped (S016). A holder who quotes the call instead of the filing repeats the slip.

### A4 Time limits for results: 45 days quarterly, 60 days annual
- **Check:** Note whether results came on time and what reason a late filing gave.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** master circular 2023-07-11. **Number:** 45 days quarterly, 60 days annual; reasons for delay within one working day; fine Rs 5,000 a day; suspension after two consecutive quarters of failure. **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "have to be submitted within a period of 45 days and 60 days, respectively" (S053)
- **Quote:** "freeze the entire shareholding of the promoter(s) in such entity as well as all other securities" (S053)
- **Red flag:** Results late two quarters in a row. A vague reason for delay.

### A5 A loan default must be disclosed within 24 hours of the 30th day
- **Check:** Read exchange announcements for a default on a bank or financial-institution loan, and for the date it was disclosed against the 30th day.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** master circular 2023-07-11; the Gensol order cites the 2024-11-11 circular for the same rule (S038, 2025-07-30). **Number:** 24 hours from the 30th day. **Sources:** 2 (S053, S038).
- **Quote:** "Such disclosure shall be made promptly, but not later than 24 hours from the 30th day of such default." (S053)
- **Red flag:** A rating cut or lender statement that appears before the company's own disclosure. In the Gensol case the first default was on 2024-12-31 while no-default statements kept going to rating agencies (allegation in the interim order, sustained prima facie in the confirmatory order).

### A6 Default on unlisted debt and the quarterly default summary
- **Check:** Look for a disclosure of default on NCDs or similar debt, and the quarterly summary.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Number:** within 24 hours of the default; summary within 7 days of quarter end. **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "The above disclosure shall be made within 7 days from the end of each quarter." (S053)
- **Red flag:** A quiet quarter followed by a rating cut.

### A7 An officer is deemed to know what he ought to have known
- **Check:** Ask when the company first held the information, not when it announced it.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Number:** board meeting outcomes to the exchange within 30 minutes of the close. **Sources:** 1 (S053); the same gap appears in S054.
- **Quote:** "or as soon as, an officer of the entity has, or ought to have reasonably come into possession of the information" (S053)
- **Quote:** "within 30 minutes of the closure of the meeting" (S053)
- **Red flag:** A long gap between internal knowledge and public disclosure (see D11).

### A8 A modified audit opinion must carry a statement of impact
- **Check:** Read the audit report for a qualification, then the statement that sizes it.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Sources:** 2 (S053, S045; S045 is a 2017 recommendation that qualifications be quantified except for going concern and sub judice matters).
- **Quote:** "audit report with modified opinion shall be accompanied by a Statement on Impact of Audit qualifications" (S053)
- **Red flag:** A qualified opinion with an explanation that gives no numbers.

### A9 A headline number's definition and what it leaves out
- **Check:** Ask what a new or favourite metric excludes, and whether the narrow scope survives retelling.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07 to 2025-11. **Sources:** 7 (S002, S009, S013, S018, S019, S020, S014).
- **Quote:** "new bookings we disclose do not include rate card deals or renewals" (S009)
- **Quote:** "So, 20 million is the life-to-date customers that we have, right?" (S002)
- **Red flag:** A customer count that includes closed accounts (S002). A bookings number that excludes renewals, where deflation shows up (S009). A profit promise that excludes share-based pay or incentives (S019). A breakeven on total EBITDA, not on the main business (S018).

### A10 Disclosure that shrinks, or is withheld as confidential
- **Check:** Ask for old-format comparatives when a company changes its disclosure, and for the segment table when a large segment is not reported.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07 to 2026-02. **Sources:** 6 (S013, S015, S016, S019, S022, S020).
- **Quote:** "But we don't have the balance sheet." (S019)
- **Quote:** "we are not yet reporting it as a significant independent segment." (S022)
- **Quote:** "we are under a confidential agreement to not talk about the quantities or the revenue associated with that" (S016)
- **Red flag:** Analysts say the deck carries fewer disclosures than in 15 to 17 years of coverage (S013). A segment with about 45% of revenue and no segment profit (S015). A growth story that rests only on undisclosed contracts.

### A11 Guidance that moves between questioners or calls
- **Check:** Write down each range a company gives on one call and across calls.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-08 to 2026-04. **Number:** one bank gave 14% to 15% and later 13% to 15% in the same call (S003); one lender gave gold loan growth as 12% to 15%, 10% to 18% and 10% to 15% on one call (S006). **Sources:** 5 (S003, S006, S007, S021, S005).
- **Quote:** "that ball broadly anchors around the 14% to 15% range." (S003)
- **Quote:** "our guidance on the consolidated EBITDA will be north of Rs. 400 crores" (S021)
- **Red flag:** A range that shifts with each questioner. Two guides that do not agree: Rs 2,000 crore revenue at 15% to 18% margin gives Rs 300 to 360 crore of EBITDA, not "north of Rs. 400 crores" (S021, arithmetic by the note-taker).

### A12 Guidance withheld, dropped or soft
- **Check:** Note which numbers the company refuses and which earlier guides it no longer repeats.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-11 to 2026-05. **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S013, S014, S020, S006, S008).
- **Quote:** "we have basically a policy of not providing guidance as such" (S002)
- **Quote:** "So normally, we don't give any guidance about what could be the future." (S014)
- **Red flag:** No guidance together with no answer on where credit cost peaks (S002). A growth range asked about and not reaffirmed (S020). A margin promise with no number: "we want our operating margin to only inch up significantly as compared to last year to this year" (S013).

### A13 Calls limit questions, and many topics never come up
- **Check:** List what no analyst asked. A call is not an audit.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-11 to 2026-04. **Sources:** 8 (S003, S005, S008, S010, S017, S018, S022, S023).
- **Quote:** "keep the number of questions restricted to two per participant" (S008)
- **Quote:** "You are requested to ask 2 questions in the initial round." (S010)
- **Red flag:** Related parties, promoter pledges, auditor changes and the exceptional item were not raised on several calls (S017, S018, S022, S023). Many opening questions are praise. This is inferred from S017, S018, S022, S023.

## B. Related-party transactions

### B1 Every related-party transaction needs audit committee approval, or an omnibus approval
- **Check:** Ask whether each material deal with a related party went to the audit committee.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** as applied to FY2016 to FY2021 events in an order of 2026-05-12. **Sources:** 3 (S043, S053, S049).
- **Quote:** "Regulation 23(2) of the LODR Regulations as applicable at the relevant period provided that all RPTs shall require approval of the Audit Committee unless omnibus approval has been obtained under Regulation 23(3)" (S043)
- **Red flag:** A deal outside any approval. In the Future Retail order the finding was a disclosure and approval default, not fraud.

### B2 Omnibus approvals: one year at most, reviewed each quarter
- **Check:** Compare the omnibus ceiling with the amount that moved.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11 (S053), applied to 2019 to 2020 events (S043). **Number:** valid for no more than one year; quarterly review. **Sources:** 2 (S053, S043).
- **Quote:** "omnibus approval granted by the audit committee shall be valid for a period not exceeding one year" (S053)
- **Red flag:** In the Future Retail order the approval was Rs 300 crore while Rs 3,148.58 crore went to one related party, above 10% of consolidated turnover (finding of a disclosure default). Quote: "had granted omnibus approval to FRL to the extent of Rs. 300 Crore towards a transaction which makes it obliged to make payment to FCRPL" (S043)

### B3 The annual report's related-party list against the audit committee's own papers
- **Check:** Compare the names the audit committee approved as related with the annual report list.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (finding in a first-instance order, 2026-05-12). **Date:** order of 2026-05-12. **Number:** seven entities controlled in substance through the chairman were left out of the list; ten related parties received deposits or advances with no approval. **Sources:** 2 (S043, S045).
- **Quote:** "disclosed as related parties in Audit Committee Meetings while taking omnibus approval in FYs 2019-20 and 2020-21, however, they were not disclosed as related parties in the related party list of FRL" (S043)
- **Red flag:** A counterparty named in the minutes but missing from the annual report.

### B4 Related-party relationships with control are disclosed even if no money moved
- **Check:** Look for control relationships in the related-party note even with a zero balance.
- **Class:** REGULATION (Ind AS 24 as quoted in the 2026-05-12 order). **Date:** order of 2026-05-12. **Sources:** 1 (S043).
- **Quote:** "Para 14 of Ind AS 24 provides that a related party relationship between two entities should be disclosed even if there are no transactions between the parties if there is existence of control relationship between parties." (S043)
- **Red flag:** A group company that runs the business of the listed one and appears nowhere.

### B5 The 2021 amendment widened the definition, from 1 April 2022
- **Check:** Ask whether a past loan route that was legal then would be reportable now.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** orders of 2025-09-18 (S039, S055). **Number:** effective 1 April 2022, glide path to 1 April 2023. **Sources:** 2 (S039, S055).
- **Quote:** "This amendment was made effective from a prospective date of April 1, 2022 and also provided a glide path till April 1, 2023." (S039)
- **Red flag:** Loans through small private intermediaries. The final orders held the older routes were not related-party transactions under the rules then in force. Allegation (show cause notices of 2024-01-15) versus finding (not established, 2025-09-18). The final orders say a legal win on definitions is not a statement that the structure was fair to minority holders.

### B6 Conduit firms: tiny net worth, large flows, same-day movement
- **Check:** Look at the half-yearly related-party disclosure for loans to small private firms, then ask who sits behind them and what the firm earns.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** final orders 2025-09-18. **Number:** about Rs 1,282 crore passed through one intermediary and was repaid with interest (S055); peak outstanding not above Rs 2,900 crore though yearly totals were many times that (S039). **Sources:** 3 (S039, S055, S044).
- **Quote:** "on the same day or the next day" (S055)
- **Red flag:** Same-day in-and-out flows, a margin near 20 basis points, a counterparty with most of its debits and credits inside one group. Treat a short-seller's arithmetic as a prompt to check, not a finding. Allegations were not established in these two orders.

### B7 Yearly loan totals against the peak balance, and whether money came back
- **Check:** Read the yearly aggregate against the maximum outstanding at any time. Check interest paid.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-09-18. **Sources:** 1 (S039).
- **Quote:** "the maximum outstanding amount, at a given point of time, was not more than INR" (S039)
- **Red flag:** Rotating money makes totals look huge. The same notes show whether loans were repaid with interest before any probe began, which SEBI weighed in the final order.

### B8 Where a listed company books a large gain on selling a business to its own subsidiary
- **Check:** Compare the gain with book value and with cash actually received.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (finding on review, order of 2026-05-29). **Date:** order of 2026-05-29. **Number:** book value about Rs 77.08 crore, price Rs 2,000 crore, gain Rs 1,922.92 crore; only Rs 700 crore paid over three years and Rs 1,300 crore cleared by ten same-day entries in two days. **Sources:** 1 (S044).
- **Quote:** "The record indicates that the OMS business, having a net book value of about Rs.77.08 crore, was transferred for a consideration of Rs.2,000 crore." (S044)
- **Red flag:** A receivable from a group company that misses its due date and is waived. Net worth shown as Rs 2,663.96 crore against Rs 741.04 crore without the deal. The adjudicating officer first took a lenient view on the same facts, and the whole time member reversed it. Appeal status unchecked.

### B9 Loans converted to equity through circular entries
- **Check:** Look for loans to a subsidiary that become equity with no net cash, then equity sold at a fraction of its booked value.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (finding on review, 2026-05-29). **Date:** order of 2026-05-29. **Number:** Rs 1,200 crore shown as equity in sixteen same-day entries of Rs 75 crore, sold for Rs 191.60 crore. **Sources:** 1 (S044).
- **Quote:** "I note that the said Rs.1,200 crore did not represent a genuine fresh equity infusion but was carried out through sixteen circular entries of Rs.75 crore each" (S044)
- **Red flag:** Impairment charges years later undo reported net worth.

### B10 Guarantees to related entities are related-party transactions
- **Check:** Ask which lease or bank guarantees the company gives for group entities, and where each was approved.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (adjudicator's reading, 2026-05-12). **Date:** order of 2026-05-12. **Sources:** 1 (S043).
- **Quote:** "In my view a guarantee given for lease rentals to a related party is a RPT under LODR Regulations as it constitutes a transfer of obligations or resources" (S043)
- **Red flag:** Tripartite lease guarantees for group entities that borrowed from banks and passed money on.

### B11 Deposits and advances to thinly run counterparties
- **Check:** Look for deposits or advances to a few entities whose revenue comes mostly from the company, and ask who their directors are.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-05-12 (S043), 2025-07-30 (S038). **Number:** deposits rose from Rs 1,935.45 crore (2019-03-31) to Rs 4,390.38 crore (2020-03-31), about 53% with ten entities (S043); one supplier's sales to the company were 55% and 91% of its total in two years (S038). **Sources:** 2 (S043, S038).
- **Quote:** "Thus, majority of the revenue from operations of these companies (except FMNL) was linked to the operations of FRL." (S043)
- **Red flag:** Namesake directors with no business role. Allegations in the Gensol interim order were sustained only prima facie.

### B12 Approved and disclosed does not mean fair
- **Check:** Check price, purpose and who gains, not only whether a deal was approved.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (secondary article, 2026-10-27). **Date:** article of 2026-10-27. **Sources:** 3 (S049, S046, S045).
- **Quote:** "A transaction may be disclosed and formally approved without necessarily being fair to the company." (S049)
- **Quote:** "A transaction below a prescribed threshold may still involve a conflict of interest or an improper transfer of value." (S049)
- **Red flag:** Loans on favourable terms, inflated purchases, asset transfers to connected entities. Use S049 for orientation only, its own text tells readers to verify against official rules.

### B13 Shareholder notice must let a holder judge whether terms are fair
- **Check:** Read the notice for audit committee source of funds, cost of funds, terms and end use, and for an arm's-length test.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "are not unfavourable to the listed entity, compared to the terms and conditions, had similar transaction been entered into between two unrelated parties" (S053)
- **Quote:** "details of the source of funds in connection with the proposed transaction" (S053)
- **Red flag:** A loan to a small related firm with the final use left blank.

### B14 Half-yearly related-party disclosure and its penalty
- **Check:** Read the half-yearly disclosure of related-party loans, deposits, advances and investments, and of loans to promoters, directors and their entities.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Number:** half-yearly; fine of Rs 5,000 per day for non-compliance with Regulation 23(9). **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "Regulation 23(9) of the LODR Regulations inter-alia requires listed entities to disclose Related Party Transactions (RPTs), on a half-yearly basis" (S053)
- **Red flag:** Any balance outstanding to promoter-linked entities.

### B15 Materiality bar for shareholder approval (see L2 for the conflict)
- **Check:** Ask whether a related-party deal crossed the bar that forces a shareholder vote.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023 (S046), applied to older events in 2026-05-12 (S043). **Number:** the older test was 10% of annual consolidated turnover; from 1 April 2022 the lower of Rs 1,000 crore or 10%. **Sources:** 2 (S043, S046).
- **Quote:** "transfer of resources exceeding Rs. 1000 crores or 10% of the annual consolidated turnover" (S046)
- **Red flag:** A large deal split into small ones. Related parties may abstain or vote against.

### B16 Open-ended related-party approvals, and absolute limits on inter-corporate deals
- **Check:** Read the resolution for an amount and a period.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS, 2023). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24. **Number:** IiAS prefers validity of not more than five years. **Sources:** 1 (S046).
- **Quote:** "the approval sought is for an indefinite amount for an undefined time period." (S046)
- **Quote:** "Companies must have an absolute limit on inter-corporate transactions." (S046)
- **Red flag:** Rolling limits tied to net worth, thin detail on the recipient.

### B17 Royalty or brand fees paid to a parent
- **Check:** Compare royalty with sales and with profit growth.
- **Class:** REGULATION (shareholder approval above 5% of consolidated turnover, 2023-24 guidelines); the 2017 committee proposed the same bar (S045). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24; committee report 2017-10. **Number:** IiAS: median royalty of 1.8% of sales and 10.5% of pre-tax pre-royalty profit for 30 companies in FY21. **Sources:** 2 (S045, S046).
- **Quote:** "shareholders’ approval will be required for royalty/brand payments to related parties exceeding 5% of consolidated turnover of the company as per the last audited financials" (S046)
- **Red flag:** Royalty above 5% of consolidated revenue, or rising faster than profit.

### B18 Promoter group entities as related parties
- **Check:** Look for promoter group entities that escaped the related-party definition.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (committee recommendation, 2017-10). **Date:** committee report 2017-10. **Number:** holding of 20% or more. **Sources:** 2 (S045, S046).
- **Quote:** "certain promoters/promoter group entities were not getting categorised as related parties under SEBI LODR Regulations" (S045)
- **Red flag:** Counterparties linked to the promoter by family or control but not by shareholding. The text of the current rule must be read, the 2017 report cannot say what was adopted as written.

## C. Promoter holding, pledge and encumbrance (SAST)

### C1 Promoters must report each pledge, invocation and release
- **Check:** Read the promoter's encumbrance figure and the history of pledges, invocations and releases in the filings.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** FAQ updated through 2022-03-30. **Sources:** 5 (S034, S029, S053, S038, S028).
- **Quote:** "The promoter (along with PACs) of the target company shall disclose details of shares encumbered by them or any invocation or release of encumbrance of shares held by them" (S034)
- **Red flag:** A rising pledge is a question for management: why does the promoter need loans against the stake? A pledge invoked by a lender must be disclosed too.

### C2 Reasons are required at 50% of the promoter's holding or 20% of share capital
- **Check:** Read the stated reason for each pledge above the line. A reason that keeps changing, or repeats, is a flag.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-03-30; effective 2019-10-01. **Number:** 50% of promoter holding or 20% of total share capital; disclosure within 7 working days, reasons within 2 working days. **Sources:** 1 (S034).
- **Quote:** "specifically disclose detailed reasons for encumbrance if the combined encumbrance by the promoter along with PACs with him equals or exceeds either 50% of their shareholding in the company or 20% of the total share capital of the company" (S034)
- **Red flag:** A pledge with no reason or a vague one.

### C3 Encumbrance is wide, and a yearly declaration must match the filings
- **Check:** Compare the promoter's yearly declaration with the pledge filings. Count lien and non-disposal undertakings too.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-03-30. **Sources:** 1 (S034).
- **Quote:** "pledge, lien, negative lien, non-disposal undertaking" (S034)
- **Red flag:** A declaration that omits an encumbrance shown elsewhere.

### C4 The quarterly shareholding pattern asks whether promoter shares are pledged
- **Check:** If the answer is Yes, open the pledge columns and compare with the last quarter.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "Whether any shares held by promoters are pledged or otherwise encumbered?" (S053)
- **Red flag:** Pledge rising quarter on quarter.

### C5 Pledge rising while the business grows
- **Check:** Track pledge against borrowings and the stated purpose, especially if the loans fund group firms.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2024-08-13 (S021); interim order 2025-04-15 and confirmatory order 2025-07-30 (S038). **Number:** promoters had pledged 75.74 lakh shares and held 35.125% by 2025-03-31; more pledges invoked in April 2025 (S038). **Sources:** 2 (S021, S038).
- **Quote:** "the percentage of pledge is also increasing" (S021)
- **Quote:** "promoters had created pledges for 75.74 Lakh shares of Gensol" (S038)
- **Red flag:** Pledges invoked while the stock falls. Allegations in the interim order were sustained prima facie; the company went into insolvency on 2025-06-13 and SEBI said its observations are tentative.

### C6 Pledge through a family holding vehicle, and steady promoter-family selling
- **Check:** Do not rely on the listed company's own pledge column. Look for a family holding company that took the shares and pledged them for group loans.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** orders of 2018-11-02 and 2023-11-30. **Number:** the vehicle took the shares in September 2006 and pledged them between October 2007 and September 2008 for loans to ten group entities. **Sources:** 2 (S040, S041).
- **Quote:** "None of the appellants have placed any material on record to suggest there were compelling reasons" (S040)
- **Red flag:** Relatives virtually liquidating holdings from 2001 to 2008 while the company reported growth. The books fraud came out in January 2009 by the chairman's own admission. The order's later figures changed with method (see L8).

### C7 Minimum public shareholding of 25%
- **Check:** Look for the public float and any non-compliance.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-03-30 (S034), 2023-07-11 (S053). **Number:** at least 25% public, so promoters at most 75%; fine Rs 5,000 a day, rising to Rs 10,000 a day after a year, with promoter holding frozen. **Sources:** 2 (S034, S053).
- **Quote:** "Rule 19A of SCRR requires all listed companies (other than public sector companies) to maintain public shareholding of at least 25% of share capital of the company." (S034)
- **Red flag:** A company below the line.

### C8 Open offer thresholds and disclosure at 5%
- **Check:** Know when a buyer must make an open offer and when holders must disclose.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-03-30. **Number:** 25% or more of shares or voting rights triggers an open offer; more than 5% a year for holders at 25% or more; disclosure when crossing 5% within two working days; normal offer for at least 26% of capital. **Sources:** 2 (S034, S026).
- **Quote:** "Acquisition of 25% or more shares or voting rights" (S034)
- **Quote:** "must be made for a minimum of 26% of the target company’s share capital" (S034)
- **Red flag:** A stake just under a trigger, repeated.

### C9 Read the open offer price against the formula and the deal price
- **Check:** Compare the offer price with the 60-day weighted average market price and the highest negotiated price in the share purchase agreement.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-03-30. **Number:** for frequently traded shares the offer price is the highest of four measures; frequently traded means at least 10% of shares traded over 12 months. **Sources:** 1 (S034).
- **Quote:** "Volume weighted average market price for sixty trading days preceding the PA." (S034)
- **Quote:** "no Promoter or shareholder can be paid any extra price, by whatever name it may be called." (S034)
- **Red flag:** An offer at the bare minimum. Extra payments to the outgoing promoter. Late payment of an offer carries interest of 10% a year and tendered shares cannot be withdrawn.

### C10 The independent directors' recommendation on an open offer
- **Check:** Read the reasoned recommendation before tendering.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-03-30. **Sources:** 1 (S034).
- **Quote:** "the board of directors of the target company shall constitute a committee of independent directors to provide reasoned recommendations on such open offer" (S034)
- **Red flag:** No reasons given. After an offer is announced the target board may not sell material assets, borrow materially or issue securities without a special resolution by postal ballot.

### C11 A SEBI filing is not a clean bill
- **Check:** Remember the regulator does not vouch for what is filed.
- **Class:** REGULATION (stated in the FAQ, 2022-03-30). **Date:** FAQ updated through 2022-03-30. **Sources:** 2 (S034, S053).
- **Quote:** "SEBI does not take any responsibility either for the truthfulness or correctness of any statement, financial soundness of acquirer, or of PACs, or of the Target Company" (S034)
- **Red flag:** Reading "filed" as "checked".

### C12 Promoter warrants and promoter reclassification
- **Check:** Read the warrant terms and the reclassification request.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS, 2023) for warrants; REGULATION for reclassification. **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24. **Number:** 25% paid up front, conversion any time in the next 18 months; reclassification needs 10% or less of voting rights. **Sources:** 1 (S046).
- **Quote:** "In a warrants issue, 25% of the conversion price is paid up front, with an option to convert the warrants into equity shares anytime during the next 18 months." (S046)
- **Red flag:** Promoters ride the stock for 18 months before paying the rest. A promoter holding warrant money that first came from the company itself (see H5).

### C13 Promoter-family loans to the company, and their conversion
- **Check:** Ask for the conversion terms and price of loans from promoter families.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-11-04. **Number:** about Rs 20,000 crore of loans, meant to convert through a partly paid rights issue of up to Rs 25,000 crore. **Sources:** 1 (S022).
- **Quote:** "those loans have been provided by the families holding -- and families' company to Adani Enterprises for growth." (S022)
- **Red flag:** Conversion price, dates and promoter participation not published. The call gave none, so dilution cannot be judged from it.

## D. Insider trading

### D1 No trading while holding unpublished price-sensitive information
- **Check:** Ask whether a sale or purchase by an insider sits close to a price-sensitive event.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** FAQ dated 2024-12-31 (S029); interim order 2025-05-28 (S054). **Sources:** 4 (S029, S054, S026, S041).
- **Quote:** "Trading in securities while in possession of UPSI is prohibited as per the regulations." (S029)
- **Red flag:** Selling before a loss is disclosed. Trades by a person with such information are presumed to be motivated by it.

### D2 The presumption and the definition
- **Check:** Read what counts as price-sensitive.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2025-05-28. **Sources:** 2 (S054, S029).
- **Quote:** "his trades would be presumed to have been motivated by the knowledge and awareness of such information in his possession" (S054)
- **Quote:** "is likely to materially affect the price of the securities" (S054)
- **Red flag:** A defence that rests only on routine selling.

### D3 Trading plans: 120-day cooling off and a price band
- **Check:** For any insider sale, ask whether a trading plan was public first.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-12-31 (revised after the 2024-09-24 amendment). **Number:** 120 calendar days; price band up to 20% from the previous close; each trade states value or number, buy or sell, and a date or a period of up to five trading days. **Sources:** 1 (S029).
- **Quote:** "a cooling off period of 120 calendar days is mandated from the date of public disclosure of the trading plan till the commencement of trading by the insider" (S029)
- **Red flag:** A sale with no plan on record, or inside the 120 days. In the IndusInd order the officers' sales were not under plans filed with the exchanges (allegation in an ex parte interim order).

### D4 Contra trades within six months
- **Check:** Look for a buy and a sell, or the reverse, by a designated person or relative inside six months.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-12-31. **Number:** six months, counted date wise; not allowed inside a trading plan. **Sources:** 1 (S029).
- **Quote:** "Any buy/sell trade, undertaken by a Designated Person (DP) and their immediate relatives, within 6 months of an earlier sell/buy trade, respectively" (S029)
- **Red flag:** Quick reversals by promoters or directors.

### D5 Disclosure threshold for insider trades
- **Check:** Look for the trade filings on the exchange and for a missing or late one.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-12-31. **Number:** Rs 10 lakh within a quarter; the next report is due when the next Rs 10 lakh is crossed. **Sources:** 1 (S029).
- **Quote:** "the disclosure of the incremental transactions after any disclosure under this sub-regulation shall be made when the transactions effected after the prior disclosure cross the threshold" (S029)
- **Red flag:** A missing or late filing is itself a flag. Index derivatives trades need not be reported.

### D6 Pledges, gifts and ESOP sales count as trades
- **Check:** Look for pledge creation, invocation and revocation, gifts and ESOP sales in the trade filings.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-12-31. **Sources:** 1 (S029).
- **Quote:** "Hence, trading would include creation/invocation/revocation of pledge." (S029)
- **Quote:** "Thus, gift is a trade and the promoter shall be required to comply with requirement of disclosure, pre-clearance and contra trade restrictions." (S029)
- **Red flag:** A gift or an invocation with no Form C. Cashless ESOP exercise and the sale are simultaneous, so pre-clearance is required.

### D7 The closed trading window
- **Check:** Ask whether insiders traded near results.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-12-31. **Sources:** 1 (S029).
- **Quote:** "The trading window shall be closed when the compliance officer determines that a designated person or class of designated persons can reasonably be expected to have possession of unpublished price sensitive information." (S029)
- **Red flag:** Trades in the weeks around results. The company's list of price-sensitive information is not public, so a holder sees only the filed trades.

### D8 Insiders beyond the company's list, and relatives
- **Check:** Remember that access to secrets makes a person an insider.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-12-31. **Sources:** 1 (S029).
- **Quote:** "even if a person is not classified as a designated person, having access to UPSI would make such a person an ‘insider’" (S029)
- **Quote:** "A spouse is presumed to be an ‘immediate relative’, unless rebutted so." (S029)
- **Red flag:** Trades by advisers, relatives and others near an event.

### D9 Selling before a bad-news disclosure
- **Check:** Compare the dates of internal knowledge, insider sales and public disclosure.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (ex parte interim order, 2025-05-28, prima facie only). **Date:** interim order 2025-05-28; call 2025-03-10. **Number:** SEBI says the information existed on or before 2023-12-04 and was disclosed 2025-03-10, about 15 months later; five officers sold shares and none bought; the stock fell 27.165% the next trading day; avoided loss worked out at Rs 19.78 crore in total. **Sources:** 3 (S054, S004, S041).
- **Quote:** "no new shares have been bought during the UPSI period" (S054)
- **Red flag:** A steady pattern of sales by senior officers, including days before the announcement. Allegation versus finding: the five officers had not been heard and had 21 days to reply. SEBI said it gave no one a clean chit. Appeal and later outcome unchecked.

### D10 Compare the date the company classified information as price-sensitive
- **Check:** Look at when the company's own database listed the information, against the date SEBI says it was price-sensitive.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (allegation in the same order). **Date:** interim order 2025-05-28. **Number:** the company's database listed it from 2025-03-04. **Sources:** 1 (S054).
- **Quote:** "likely disgorgement amount is calculated by multiplying this impact of 27.165% on each sale of shares" (S054)
- **Red flag:** Late classification. The disgorgement figure is an estimate, SEBI notes other price factors exist.

### D11 The insider-trading test changed in 2002
- **Check:** Read the date of the rule when reading an old case.
- **Class:** REGULATION as applied in an order of 2023-11-30. **Date:** order of 2023-11-30. **Number:** before 2002-02-20 the test was dealing on the basis of price-sensitive information; after it, dealing while in possession. **Sources:** 1 (S041).
- **Quote:** "any sale consideration arising out of shares sold by the Noticees prior to February 20, 2002 shall not be considered" (S041)
- **Red flag:** Quoting an old case under today's test.

### D12 A one-day fall far larger than the sector index
- **Check:** Compare a stock's one-day fall with its sector index and its own past correlation.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (order of 2023-11-30). **Date:** order of 2023-11-30. **Number:** the stock fell 36% in one day on rumours in December 2008; its correlation with the IT index was about 93.5% over ten years and 36.0% after the news. **Sources:** 1 (S041).
- **Quote:** "I do not represent that this 23.25% is perfectly accurate and precise" (S041)
- **Red flag:** Company-specific collapse with no filed explanation. The quote shows the member calls his own intrinsic-value method an estimate.

## E. Auditors, audit committee and board

### E1 Auditor rotation and IiAS tenure flags
- **Check:** Read the auditor's tenure and the audit partner's.
- **Class:** REGULATION for the Act's rotation; GUIDANCE for IiAS flags. **Date:** 2023-24 guidelines. **Number:** individual auditor every five years; audit firm after ten years (two terms of five); IiAS flags tenure over 10 years for companies and 3 years for banks. **Sources:** 3 (S046, S045, S047).
- **Quote:** "The Act mandates rotation of individual auditors every five years and of the audit firm after a maximum period of ten years (i.e., after two terms of five years each) for listed companies." (S046)
- **Red flag:** Long tenure, no peer review, ties to the promoter group, audit fee not disclosed.

### E2 An auditor who resigns early
- **Check:** Read the reasons filed within 24 hours.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11 (S053); 2017-10 and 2023 (S045, S046). **Number:** reasons within 24 hours; if the auditor resigns within 45 days of a quarter end, a limited review or audit report for that quarter must be issued first. **Sources:** 4 (S053, S045, S046, S004).
- **Quote:** "not later than twenty-four hours of receipt of such reasons from the auditor" (S053)
- **Quote:** "The resignation of an auditor before the expiry of the term may be a cause for concern." (S045)
- **Red flag:** Resignation citing non-cooperation or information not provided, or close to results (S046).

### E3 Auditor facing non-cooperation goes to the audit committee chair
- **Check:** Ask whether the audit committee chair was told.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "the auditor shall approach the Chairman of the Audit Committee" (S053)
- **Red flag:** A resignation letter that mentions non-cooperation.

### E4 Fees to the auditor and its network, on a consolidated basis
- **Check:** Read total fees for all services paid to the auditor and its network.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (committee, 2017-10). **Date:** committee report 2017-10. **Sources:** 2 (S045, S046).
- **Quote:** "total fees for all services paid by the listed entity and its subsidiaries (i.e. on a consolidated basis) to the statutory auditor and all entities in the network firm/network entity of which the auditor is a part" (S045)
- **Red flag:** Non-audit fees large against the audit fee.

### E5 Audit files, branch auditors and oral explanations
- **Check:** For a lender or any company with many branches, ask how many branch auditors there are and whether shareholders appointed them.
- **Class:** REGULATION (NFRA order of 2023-04-13). **Date:** NFRA order of 2023-04-13. **Number:** 33 branch auditors for nearly 250 branches; none approved at the AGM; audit file must be assembled within 60 days; penalty Rs 1 lakh and a one-year bar for the engagement partner. **Sources:** 1 (S047).
- **Quote:** "NFRA's investigations revealed that the appointment of none of the 33 branch auditors was approved at the Annual General Meeting (AGM) of DHFL, as required by the Act." (S047)
- **Quote:** "Oral explanations by the auditor, on their own, do not represent adequate support for the work auditor performed or conclusions the auditor reached" (S047)
- **Red flag:** Papers added to the file after an inquiry begins. The order is about one branch auditor's conduct, not whether the loan book was misstated.

### E6 Audit missed it: a problem that ran for years
- **Check:** Ask how a long-running problem passed several layers of audit.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-03-10. **Number:** the discrepancy ran about five to seven years; first estimate 2.35% of net worth at December 2024. **Sources:** 2 (S004, S054).
- **Quote:** "Why was it picked up in the last 3 months since it's gone through multiple layers of audit and the auditors have signed the balance sheets for these transactions" (S004)
- **Red flag:** The CFO said he could not answer. The final amount, the external report and any later action are not in the source.

### E7 Independent directors: term, tenure and early resignation
- **Check:** Read the term and the filed reasons for any early exit.
- **Class:** REGULATION for the term; GUIDANCE for IiAS and the committee. **Date:** 2023-24 (S046), 2017-10 (S045). **Number:** two terms of up to five years each; IiAS treats a director as non-independent after 10 years. **Sources:** 2 (S045, S046).
- **Quote:** "an independent director is permitted to be appointed for two consecutive terms of up to five years each" (S046)
- **Quote:** "listed entities should be required to disclose detailed reasons for resignation of IDs (as provided by such IDs) along with the notification of their resignation to the stock exchanges" (S045)
- **Red flag:** No reasons, or no confirmation of no other material reasons (committee recommendation, proposed from 2018-04-01).

### E8 Board independence, seat caps and attendance
- **Check:** Count independents against the chair type, count each director's seats, check attendance.
- **Class:** REGULATION (S046, 2023-24); S045 proposed more (see L3). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24; committee report 2017-10. **Number:** half the board independent if the chair is executive or a promoter-group member; no more than 10 public companies (7 listed); IiAS accepts at least 75% attendance for a director up for re-appointment; at least one independent woman director for the top 1,000. **Sources:** 2 (S045, S046).
- **Quote:** "An individual can be a director in a maximum of 10 public companies (maximum of 7 listed companies)" (S046)
- **Quote:** "IiAS expects 100% attendance, but for directors coming up for re-appointment, accepts a minimum attendance level of 75% in the just concluded year." (S046)
- **Red flag:** Overboarded or absent directors.

### E9 Independence tested against business ties
- **Check:** Look for independent directors whose firm serves the company or its group.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS, 2023). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023. **Sources:** 1 (S046).
- **Quote:** "Directors who may have business ties with the company, with their firms providing services to the company or other companies in the group, irrespective of the extent of the pecuniary relationship." (S046)
- **Red flag:** Any such tie, however small. Non-rotational promoter seats: IiAS says they give no chance to vote periodically on reappointment.

### E10 Executive promoter pay
- **Check:** Compare promoter pay with net profit.
- **Class:** REGULATION (S046, 2023-24); GUIDANCE proposal in 2017-10 (S045). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24; committee report 2017-10. **Number:** shareholder approval if all executive promoter directors together exceed 5% of net profit, or one exceeds Rs 50 million or 2.5% of net profit, whichever is higher. **Sources:** 2 (S045, S046).
- **Quote:** "shareholder approval is required if overall pay of all the executive promoter directors exceeds 5% of the net profit or if remuneration of a single promoter executive director exceeds Rs. 50 mn or 2.5% of the net profit, whichever is higher" (S046)
- **Red flag:** Pay rising faster than profit, median employee pay or dividends.

### E11 Audit committee review of money sent into subsidiaries
- **Check:** Ask what the committee reviewed on loans or advances into unlisted subsidiaries.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (committee, 2017-10). **Date:** committee report 2017-10. **Number:** above Rs 100 crore or 10% of the subsidiary's assets, whichever is lower. **Sources:** 1 (S045).
- **Quote:** "reviewing the utilization of loans and/ or advances from/investment by the holding company in the subsidiary exceeding rupees 100 crore or 10% of the asset size of the subsidiary, whichever is lower." (S045)
- **Red flag:** Large advances with no stated review.

### E12 CEO and CFO certification, and who is liable
- **Check:** Remember that the signing officers certify the statements.
- **Class:** REGULATION (as quoted in an order of 2026-05-29); the penalty bands are in K9. **Date:** orders of 2026-05-29 and 2026-05-12. **Sources:** 2 (S044, S043).
- **Quote:** "Para V of Clause 49 of the erstwhile Listing Agreement concerns certification by the CEO/ Managing Director/Manager and CFO/whole-time Finance Director regarding the review of financial statements and absence of materially untrue statements or omissions." (S044)
- **Quote:** "committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, any director" (S043)
- **Red flag:** An order that clears the auditors and audit committee but fines the officers is still a signal. In S043 the joint managing director who sat on the audit committee was held to have breached the audit committee clause.

### E13 Dividends, donations and ESOPs
- **Check:** Compare dividend with profit and cash flow; donations with profit; ESOP dilution with the cap.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS, 2023-24). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24. **Number:** donations above 5% of average net profit draw an against vote; ESOP dilution under 5%. **Sources:** 1 (S046).
- **Quote:** "IiAS will generally recommend voting AGAINST charitable donations beyond 5% of average net profits." (S046)
- **Quote:** "IiAS expects the dilution to be restricted to less than 5%." (S046)
- **Red flag:** High payout while profit is poor, debt is in default or operating cash flow is weak.

## F. Earnings quality and cash

### F1 Strip one-offs, reversals and non-operating gains out of margin
- **Check:** Ask the size of every one-off behind a beat, and whether it reverses.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07 to 2026-02. **Sources:** 14 (S003, S005, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014, S015, S020, S022, S023).
- **Quote:** "It has to reverse. there is no way." (S012)
- **Quote:** "adjusted for that, the core yield was somewhere around 18%-18.5%." (S007)
- **Red flag:** About Rs 100 crore of reversible gains in a margin beat (S012). Rs 300 crore of old-account recoveries inside a higher yield (S007). A pharma call paraphrased from S011 where other income rose sharply while gross margin fell (paraphrase of S011).

### F2 An "adjusted" margin that removes the cost but keeps the gain
- **Check:** See whether the same bridge that strips a restructuring cost also strips the matching gain.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-10-13. **Number:** a 55 basis point restructuring cost against a 56 basis point forex gain. **Sources:** 1 (S009).
- **Quote:** "Forex gain from INR depreciation gave us positive 56 basis points and restructuring expenses had an impact of negative 55 basis points." (S009)
- **Red flag:** Net of both, the one-offs roughly cancel.

### F3 Provision write-backs, recoveries and reversals that lift profit
- **Check:** Find where a reversal is booked and whether it is called exceptional.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07 to 2025-11. **Sources:** 5 (S003, S005, S007, S015, S020).
- **Quote:** "Because it was charged to the other expenses when we made the provision." (S020)
- **Quote:** "The other income includes predominantly on account of exchange fluctuations, income from mutual funds, road tap incentives and collections of owed receivables, which we have written back in the past." (S015)
- **Red flag:** A reversal booked in other expenses lifts operating profit without an exceptional label (a Rs 28 crore receivable, Rs 21 crore recovered, S020).

### F4 Exceptional items as a share of profit
- **Check:** Split profit before tax into continuing and exceptional.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-11-04. **Number:** half-year profit before tax Rs 5,864 crore, of which Rs 3,583 crore exceptional (about 61%, note-taker's division). **Sources:** 2 (S022, S016).
- **Quote:** "On a continuing basis, profit before tax of INR2,281 crores, INR3,583 crores is one of exceptional items." (S022)
- **Red flag:** Exceptional gains funding headline profit. No analyst asked on that call.

### F5 EBITDA to PAT: where did the rest go
- **Check:** Compare EBITDA with PAT, then interest, depreciation and subsidiary losses.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** calls of 2024-08-13 and 2024-11-14. **Number:** Rs 89 to 90 crore of quarterly EBITDA became about Rs 15 crore of PAT (S021); PAT of Rs 10 crore on total income of Rs 2,309 crore beside Rs 5,488 crore of cash (S020). **Sources:** 4 (S021, S020, S019, S017).
- **Quote:** "if you see, Rs. 90 crores of EBITDA at quarter level but the PAT which is being there that is just around Rs. 15 crores." (S021)
- **Red flag:** A gap that widens as debt rises. Subsidiary losses relabelled as investment: "we don't consider this as losses." (S021)

### F6 PBT growth against PAT growth, and the tax rate
- **Check:** If profit grows faster than pre-tax profit, find the tax credit or reset.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** calls 2024-07 to 2025-11. **Number:** PBT up about 13.6% against PAT up 20.1% (S015); effective tax rate 24.7% against 15.8% (S010). **Sources:** 3 (S005, S010, S015).
- **Quote:** "Tax is lower due to the credit that we have received on closing some of the assessments in the earlier years." (S015)
- **Red flag:** Earlier growth that leaned on tax benefits now ending.

### F7 Cash conversion and payout
- **Check:** Compare operating cash flow to net income or EBITDA over several periods.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-10 to 2026-04. **Number:** operating cash flow to net income 133%, free cash flow to net income 125% (S009); operating cash flow to EBITDA 85% to 90% (S017). The sources state no bar. **Sources:** 5 (S009, S010, S017, S022, S015).
- **Quote:** "Operating Cash Flow to Net Income conversion is at 133%, and Free Cash Flow to Net Income is at 125%." (S009)
- **Red flag:** Cash from operations falling while profit holds, blamed on working capital with a promise to normalise (S010). Asking the CFO for operating profit before working capital changes instead of cash after it (S022).

### F8 Receivable days and working capital
- **Check:** Track days including unbilled work, and the ageing of old dues.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-08 to 2025-11. **Number:** total DSO 78 days (S009); net working capital 22 days against 27 (S020); debtor days 60 to 70 (S021). **Sources:** 4 (S009, S015, S020, S021).
- **Quote:** "Our total DSO, including unbilled, is currently at 78 days" (S009)
- **Red flag:** Days rise while revenue grows, or old dues are "hard at work to get some of this old stuff collected" (S015).

### F9 Growth against a distorted base
- **Check:** Name the base effect: a duty cut, last year's destocking, a part-year acquisition.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-07 to 2026-04. **Sources:** 5 (S012, S013, S014, S017, S023).
- **Quote:** "has a higher base effect going forward because of last year's custom duty reduction" (S012)
- **Quote:** "Last year we did some sort of inventory correction in the same quarter last year." (S013)
- **Red flag:** Reported growth 1.7% against about 7% excluding the seasonal portfolio (S013). One acquisition only part-year in the base makes next year's growth look high without improvement (S017).

### F10 Price against volume
- **Check:** Split revenue growth into price and volume.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-07-31. **Number:** price up about 3%, volume near minus 1%, net realisation minus 1.8%. **Sources:** 2 (S013, S019).
- **Quote:** "Because of the competitive intensity, there was a higher netting of schemes." (S013)
- **Red flag:** Revenue down 80% on volume down 20% means the lost lines carried most of the revenue (S019).

### F11 Order growth is not margin
- **Check:** Separate base orders from large orders and compare price rises with cost moves.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2026-05-08. **Number:** orders up 25%, base orders 9%; segment margin 21.4% to 15.2%. **Sources:** 3 (S014, S018, S021).
- **Quote:** "The price hike which we take today is not valid next quarter because of the commodity and the forex rates move faster than the price hikes what we have." (S014)
- **Red flag:** Growth mostly from a few big awards while margins fall. Pre-orders resting on MOUs with no price or delivery (S038, see G13).

### F12 Subsidy and incentive share of margin
- **Check:** Ask the margin without the incentive and whether the certification is complete.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07-19 (S019), 2025-11-06 (S018). **Number:** PLI added about 7 to 8 points of gross margin once all product revenue qualifies; two points sat in the reported 30.7% (S018). **Sources:** 2 (S018, S019).
- **Quote:** "PLI in the 30.7% gross margin was only two points." (S018)
- **Quote:** "there will be a lump sum UPI incentive, hopefully sizable again this year" (S019)
- **Red flag:** A margin walk that depends on incentive money not yet received. Profit that comes with falling market share.

### F13 Subsidiary margins and transfer pricing
- **Check:** Ask how much group profit sits in a lower-overhead subsidiary and on what basis costs move.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2026-02-09. **Sources:** 1 (S016).
- **Quote:** "there is a proper transfer pricing arms-length association between these two companies to transfer the costs between the two sort of entities" (S016)
- **Red flag:** One strong quarter, one customer or one campaign annualised: "So, we have a campaign-driven mechanism where some products yield better margins, some product yields slightly lesser margins." (S016)

### F14 Back-ended and campaign revenue
- **Check:** Look at the share of the year's revenue booked in the last quarter.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2024-08-13. **Number:** close to 40% of last year's revenue in the fourth quarter. **Sources:** 2 (S021, S016).
- **Quote:** "we did close to 40% of our revenue in quarter 4" (S021)
- **Red flag:** A first-quarter run rate used to support a full-year guide.

### F15 Capex against guidance, funding and utilisation
- **Check:** Compare capex spent with the guided range, and utilisation before more capex.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-11 to 2026-04. **Number:** capacity planned at 80% utilisation (S017); a plant at 70% to 80% (S016); a rule of thumb to expand near 75% (S015). **Sources:** 4 (S015, S016, S017, S022).
- **Quote:** "Always capacity planning is done at 80%." (S017)
- **Quote:** "Utilization for MPP-2 is about 80%, 70% around in that range, but we expect that to pick up in the coming year." (S016)
- **Red flag:** Capex above guidance with debt rising faster than EBITDA. Reported utilisation above 100% can reflect sales timing, not production (S022).

### F16 A leverage ceiling that bends
- **Check:** Ask whether a stated cap is hard or elastic.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (company policy, April 2026). **Date:** call 2026-04-30. **Number:** net debt to EBITDA guided at 2.5x, reported at 1.9x for FY26, management would hold the rating at 3x; organic capex 60% to 70% of annual operating cash. **Sources:** 1 (S017).
- **Quote:** "2.5x is the ceiling but even if I go 3" (S017)
- **Red flag:** A cap described as breakable is not a cap.

### F17 Market-size and market-share claims made by the company
- **Check:** Treat company-built market sizes and shares as soft unless an outside series backs them.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-07 to 2026-02. **Sources:** 5 (S012, S013, S016, S018, S019).
- **Quote:** "studded market, our best estimate, and this could vary based on whom you ask the question to, is around INR 75,000 crores, give or take here and there" (S012)
- **Quote:** "Talking from memory, but that is what it is." (S016)
- **Red flag:** The main inputs are the company's own tracks. A global potential presented as addressable.

### F18 Cash balance: disposal proceeds and escrow
- **Check:** Look for one-off proceeds and restricted cash inside a cash figure.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-11-05 and 2026-05-08. **Number:** Rs 6,042 crore, or roughly Rs 7,600 crore with the proceeds of a business sale (S014). **Sources:** 2 (S010, S014).
- **Quote:** "So we have, at this point of time, INR6,042 crores, but this is without the cash which we got from the sale of Robotics. If I include that, it is roughly round about INR7,600 crores." (S014)
- **Red flag:** Cash in escrow or restricted accounts inflating current assets (S010).

### F19 Contingent liabilities and net worth from year to year
- **Check:** Compare the contingent liabilities note across years. A big guarantee that vanishes needs a reason.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (finding on review, order of 2026-05-29). **Date:** order of 2026-05-29. **Number:** an exposure of USD 569.40 million, about Rs 4,050 crore, was shown as contingent in FY2017 and dropped in FY2018 after being called an insurance contract. **Sources:** 2 (S044, S043).
- **Quote:** "It is noted that there was no change in the underlying liability structure between FY 2016-17 and FY 2017-18." (S044)
- **Red flag:** A note somewhere is not a fix: "Paragraph 18 of Ind AS 1 itself states that inappropriate accounting policies cannot be rectified merely by disclosure of the accounting policies used or by notes / explanatory material." (S044)

### F20 Test a liquidity claim yourself
- **Check:** Compute current assets against current liabilities from the balance sheet.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (order of 2026-05-12). **Date:** order of 2026-05-12. **Sources:** 1 (S043).
- **Quote:** "The Company does not face a significant liquidity risk with regards to its lease Liabilities as the current assets are sufficient to meet obligations to lease liabilities as and when they fall due." (S043)
- **Red flag:** Store terminations came about seven months after this note, though the order found no contradiction. The order compares total assets with total current liabilities, so run your own ratio.

### F21 Ratios that moved by 25% or more should be explained
- **Check:** In the management discussion, look for debtor turnover, inventory turnover, interest cover, current ratio, debt-equity or margins that swing without a reason.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (committee, 2017-10). **Date:** committee report 2017-10. **Number:** 25% or more in a year. **Sources:** 1 (S045).
- **Quote:** "wherever there is a change of 25% or more in a particular financial year, along with detailed explanations thereof" (S045)
- **Red flag:** A big swing with no explanation. Cash flow statements half-yearly were also recommended.

### F22 Debt defined two ways on one call
- **Check:** Ask which debt a speaker means.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-11-10. **Number:** long-term debt "about Rs. 120 crores, Rs. 130 crores" against an analyst's near Rs 247 crore. **Sources:** 1 (S015).
- **Quote:** "A long-term debt is actually quite small. It is about Rs. 120 crores, Rs. 130 crores, which is probably a quarter of our EBITDA." (S015)
- **Red flag:** Definitions not reconciled on the call.

### F23 A written commitment is a test for the next result
- **Check:** Record the promise and check it next quarter.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-11-10. **Number:** a 20% PAT growth floor and a 7.5% PBT margin target. **Sources:** 2 (S015, S010).
- **Quote:** "My team and I will commit to both the value of PAT and the margin growing in Q3." (S015)
- **Red flag:** A fix announced before it is proven: management had said the labour problem was solved, then it "got caught up" late in the quarter. Also test "will normalise" promises (S010, S022).

### F24 Customer and permission concentration
- **Check:** Ask which customer, concession or permission would stop revenue if withdrawn.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07 to 2026-04. **Sources:** 4 (S017, S019, S020, S038).
- **Quote:** "Similarly, I mean, our largest port as well, concession is due at that time." (S017)
- **Red flag:** A vendor's volume depends on how much the biggest marketplaces outsource (S020). An unsigned renewal on the largest asset (S017). A company that depends on a few permissions or one bank partner (S019).

## G. Lenders

### G1 Read stage 3, credit cost and margin together
- **Check:** Never read margin or credit cost alone.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-07 to 2026-01. **Number:** gross stage 3 rose from 2.26% to 2.56% in a quarter, credit cost Rs 670 crore against Rs 634 crore (S002). **Sources:** 5 (S002, S005, S006, S008, S003).
- **Quote:** "our NIM has expanded because our yields have improved, not because of benefit of cost of funds" (S002)
- **Red flag:** Margin up on yield while bad loans and credit cost rise.

### G2 A fall in credit cost can come from cover, not better loans
- **Check:** Ask how much of the fall is guarantee cover, recoveries or earlier provisions used.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2026-01-20. **Number:** a guarantee scheme covered 83% of the microfinance book; 9-month credit cost 1.1% against about 1% guided for the full year. **Sources:** 3 (S008, S003, S007).
- **Quote:** "the large reduction of credit cost happened on account of lower slippages and higher coverage of guarantee." (S008)
- **Red flag:** A good quarter built on cover, seasonality and a smaller unsecured book needs a year of data before it is called a trend.

### G3 Recoveries, write-backs and a prudent provision in the same quarter
- **Check:** Look at all provisions, recoveries and write-backs, not only net credit cost.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2026-04-18. **Number:** a Rs 341 crore extra provision in a quarter with a Rs 288 crore write-back; recoveries from security receipts above Rs 1,550 crore in FY26 and guided to Rs 800 to 1,000 crore in FY27. **Sources:** 1 (S003).
- **Quote:** "we had a write-back of about INR 288 crores during the quarter." (S003)
- **Quote:** "we expect recoveries to the tune of INR 800 crores to INR 1,000 crores from SRs in FY27." (S003)
- **Red flag:** A tailwind that is guided lower next year.

### G4 Provision coverage across stages, and the unit of comparison
- **Check:** Compare coverage by stage year on year, not only against the last quarter.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-07-15. **Number:** total provision 3.3% of the book; coverage 56% on a mostly secured book. **Sources:** 2 (S002, S008).
- **Quote:** "March to June would be a better comparison." (S002)
- **Red flag:** Management steering to the shorter comparison. Microfinance rule at one lender: "for MFI at 90 DPD, we provide 50% and then every month, we provide another 15% so that by 180 day, we are 100% provided." (S008, an example, not a rule)

### G5 Core yield against a one-off recovery
- **Check:** Strip recoveries of old non-performing accounts out of yield. Interest on bad loans is booked only on cash recovery.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-11-13. **Number:** about Rs 300 crore inside the reported yield; core yield 18% to 18.5%. **Sources:** 1 (S007).
- **Quote:** "As you know, on NPA accounts, we do not recognize the interest." (S007)
- **Red flag:** Stage 3 falling only Rs 100 crore against Rs 700 crore the quarter before while interest recovery looked strong. Reported yield rises while management says the core yield is lower.

### G6 Deposit quality
- **Check:** Look at the gap between period-end and average deposits, the volatile share, and bulk or callable money.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-01-20 and 2026-04-18. **Number:** the bank said about 15% of deposits are volatile, and deposits under Rs 3 crore were 47% of net accretion against 31% (S001); a stable deposit ratio of about 80% (S008). **Sources:** 2 (S001, S008).
- **Quote:** "that aspect of the 15% of the total deposits will be volatile in nature." (S001)
- **Red flag:** A surge in the last fortnight with no single driver named. Reliance on mandated or priority deposits (S003: about 6% of assets against 9%).

### G7 The same ratio on two definitions
- **Check:** Ask which base a lender is using before comparing.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-07 to 2026-01. **Number:** credit to deposit ratio about 89% reported and 80% excluding refinance (S008); loan to value given as 57%, 43% and 70% to 71% (S007); ROA 1.94% reported, 2.02% adjusted (S002). **Sources:** 3 (S002, S007, S008).
- **Quote:** "if you see the CD ratio excluding the refinance, then we are 80%, right?" (S008)
- **Red flag:** The preferred measure is the lower or more flattering one.

### G8 Growth claims against the regulator's system figure
- **Check:** Recompute system credit growth from the central bank's period-end data.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2026-04-18. **Number:** management put system growth at 13.5% to 13.9%, the analyst at upward of 15%; the bank did 12%. **Sources:** 1 (S001).
- **Quote:** "when you compare the period-end numbers as of 31st March, which is published by the Reserve Bank of India and you make the math, it comes to somewhere around 13.5% to 13.9%." (S001)
- **Red flag:** Guidance restated vaguely, not renewed.

### G9 Gold loan rules and exposures
- **Check:** Read the loan to value limit and the regulator circular, and the share of auctions.
- **Class:** REGULATION (as described on calls, so check the circular text). **Date:** calls 2024-11-05 and 2025-11-13. **Number:** a 75% maximum loan to value; new rules for loans up to Rs 2.5 lakh at 85% considered from the next April; the gold lender's average LTV 58%. **Sources:** 2 (S006, S007).
- **Quote:** "we are lending at 75% of the gold price as per RBI norms." (S007)
- **Red flag:** Auctions tiny because gold is high: "Rs. 10 crores, very miniscule because the gold price is there." (S007) A price fall reverses that.

### G10 A regulator stop on a subsidiary, and what the group looks like
- **Check:** Find the subsidiary's share of group assets and profit, and read the regulator's order itself.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (management's account; the order text is not in the source). **Date:** call 2024-11-05. **Number:** the microfinance subsidiary held Rs 12,149 crore of Rs 45,716 crore group assets; its profit fell 25% on the quarter; ban expected to last "minimum they take 3 to 4 months". **Sources:** 1 (S006).
- **Quote:** "RBI has directed our major subsidiary, Asirvad Microfinance to desist from disbursement from 22 October '24" (S006)
- **Red flag:** An inspection finding disclosed on the call ("This was raised by the regulator in their inspection and immediately thereafter, we have stopped it."). Whether the ban was lifted is not in the source.

### G11 Covenant breaches
- **Check:** Look for breaches in the notes and decks. Ask the amount and the lender.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2024-11-05. **Number:** one debenture breach under Rs 50 crore tied to gross NPA; a bank term loan breach not quantified. **Sources:** 1 (S006).
- **Quote:** "there is only one covenant breach on NCD, which is a very small amount." (S006)
- **Red flag:** A breach tied to bad loans. A group credit cost guide of 2% held while one segment was said to be near 7%.

### G12 The first disclosed number is a provisional estimate
- **Check:** Treat a first loss number as an estimate until an outside party validates it. Track the restated results.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-03-10. **Number:** 2.35% of net worth at December 2024 (about Rs 1,529.88 crore, worked out in the SEBI order of 2025-05-28). **Sources:** 2 (S004, S054).
- **Quote:** "because this is an internal number that we've arrived at, which is an approximate number, but not an exact number." (S004)
- **Red flag:** A percentage with no rupee figure and the external review awaited. The correction goes through profit and loss: "I think the general reserves cannot be touched, and we'll have to take it through the P&L." (S004)

### G13 Exits and interim seats at the top of finance
- **Check:** Look for CFO exits before bad news, short CEO terms, and an interim CFO.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-03-10 (S004), 2026-01-20 (S008). **Number:** the board approved a three-year CEO term and the regulator gave one. **Sources:** 2 (S004, S008).
- **Quote:** "The Board has approved for 3 years but you've got only 1 year?" (S004)
- **Quote:** "the CFO had resigned a couple of months back" (S004)
- **Red flag:** An exit that predates a disclosure with a vague reason. A short extension is a signal to read, not proof of wrongdoing.

### G14 Pending litigation with no number
- **Check:** Ask the size of the exposure for any case before a court.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2026-04-18. **Sources:** 2 (S003, S007).
- **Quote:** "this matter is subjudice, as you all know." (S003)
- **Red flag:** "Reserved for judgment" gives no balance sheet impact. A retail complaint case disclosed on the exchange but not explained on the call (S007).

### G15 Divergence disclosure for listed banks
- **Check:** Look for a divergence disclosure after the central bank's risk assessment.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-07-11. **Number:** within 24 hours when extra provisioning exceeds 5% of reported profit before provisions and contingencies, or extra gross NPAs exceed 5% of reported incremental gross NPAs. **Sources:** 1 (S053).
- **Quote:** "exceeds the 5% of reported profit before provisions and contingencies for the reference period" (S053)
- **Red flag:** A divergence disclosed late or not at all.

### G16 A rating that sits above what the market pays
- **Check:** Compare the rating with the bond yield and the share price.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (SEBI order of 2023-04-20). **Date:** SEBI order of 2023-04-20. **Number:** one housing lender kept AAA while its 10 year bonds yielded 9.9245% against 9% for another AAA housing lender; the share fell 42% in a day; the rating was revalidated 2018-11-01 and the lender defaulted to D on 2019-06-05. **Sources:** 3 (S042, S038, S028).
- **Quote:** "This would indicate that markets were already pricing in a much lower credit quality for DHFL." (S042)
- **Red flag:** Downgrades that follow a press exposé instead of leading it.

### G17 Rating agency independence
- **Check:** Look at the fee concentration and who sits on the rating committee.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-04-20. **Number:** one issuer paid Rs 7.10 crore in FY2018-19; persons with business responsibility may not sit on the committee (an MD or CEO exception was removed by the circular of 2019-11-04). **Sources:** 1 (S042).
- **Quote:** "Persons who have business responsibility shall not be part of the Rating Committee." (S042)
- **Red flag:** Rating on promises, not performance. Allegation versus finding: interference by the MD in one rating was established; charges on other ratings and on the chairman were not.

### G18 A rating cut to D and an agency that doubts the papers
- **Check:** Read the stated reason for each downgrade, and note when lenders and agencies spoke before the company did.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (confirmatory order, 2025-07-30). **Date:** confirmatory order 2025-07-30. **Number:** both agencies cut to D in March 2025. **Sources:** 2 (S038, S053).
- **Quote:** "certain documents shared by GEL with ICRA, on its debt servicing track record, were apparently falsified" (S038)
- **Red flag:** Forged lender conduct letters (allegation in the interim order, sustained prima facie).

### G19 Loans for a purpose against the assets bought
- **Check:** Compare the assets the loan was meant for with what the notes show was bought.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (confirmatory order, 2025-07-30, prima facie only). **Date:** confirmatory order 2025-07-30. **Number:** loans for 6,400 vehicles, 4,704 bought, Rs 262.13 crore unaccounted; Rs 977.75 crore of term loans in all. **Sources:** 1 (S038).
- **Quote:** "either transferred back to the Company itself or routed to entities that were directly or indirectly related" (S038)
- **Red flag:** Money paid to a supplier who sent it back or on to promoter-linked entities. The order's own limits: no forensic audit was done and nothing is a final finding of fraud.

### G20 Subsidiary losses against the stake and microfinance arms
- **Check:** Size the subsidiary's loss and the group's stake in it.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2025-11-13. **Number:** a 66% owned microfinance arm lost Rs 160 crore in the half-year. **Sources:** 2 (S007, S006).
- **Quote:** "It incurred a loss of Rs. 160 crores during the half-year" (S007)
- **Red flag:** Return to profit promised in "two more quarters".

## H. IPOs and fund raises

### H1 Fresh issue or offer for sale
- **Check:** Find the split. In a fresh issue the money goes into the company. In an offer for sale it goes to the sellers.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** undated booklet (S024); article of October 2026 (S051). **Number:** offers for sale were 61.2% (Rs 1.45 lakh crore) of about Rs 2.37 lakh crore raised from January 2025 to August 2026 (S051, perishable). **Sources:** 3 (S024, S051, S026).
- **Quote:** "In this kind of an issue, the funds of the investors will go to such sellers of the shares and not to the company." (S024)
- **Red flag:** A large offer-for-sale share is a question: why are insiders selling? A pure offer for sale of Rs 276.57 crore, 168.35 times subscribed, closed 2026-10-19 down from the offer (S051; the article says this does not prove manipulation).

### H2 What the fresh money is for
- **Check:** Read the stated objects in the offer document.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** undated. **Sources:** 2 (S024, S045).
- **Quote:** "details of the promoters, business model, financial history of the company, risks in that business, purpose for which the money is being raised, terms of issue" (S024)
- **Red flag:** A vague use of funds or thin promoter detail.

### H3 Money raised against where it went
- **Check:** Compare the stated purpose with the cash trail, and read the quarterly deviation statement.
- **Class:** REGULATION for the statement (2023-07-11); EXAMPLE for the case (order of 2026-05-12). **Date:** order of 2026-05-12; circular 2023-07-11. **Number:** US$500 million of notes raised in January 2020, Rs 3,559.70 crore advanced, about Rs 1,770 crore (50%) used to repay the company's own debt (see L10 for the figures that do not add). **Sources:** 4 (S043, S053, S045, S023).
- **Quote:** "Thus, Rs. 1,770 Crore (around 50% of the total advance of Rs. 3559.7 Crore) was utilized by FRL towards repayment of its own debt." (S043)
- **Red flag:** A large part of a raise comes back from the counterparty within weeks. A deviation, or a nil report that does not match the cash.

### H4 Issue price against each seller's cost
- **Check:** Read the weighted average acquisition cost of every selling holder in the offer document.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** October 2026. **Number:** Rs 23.52 for one promoter, Rs 30.87 for a fund, against an offer price of Rs 108. **Sources:** 1 (S051).
- **Quote:** "The company's red herring prospectus put the weighted average acquisition cost at ₹23.52 for AceVector, the promoter formerly known as Snapdeal, and ₹30.87 for SoftBank's SB Investment Holdings (UK)" (S051)
- **Red flag:** Sellers at a many-fold gain with no fresh money going in.

### H5 Preferential issues and who is behind the allottee
- **Check:** Ask the allottee, dilution, urgency, debt and return on capital.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS) and EXAMPLE (allegation). **Date:** 2023-24 (S046), 2025-07-30 (S038). **Sources:** 3 (S024, S046, S038).
- **Quote:** "securities are issued to an identified set of investors like promoters, strategic investors, employees, etc." (S024)
- **Red flag:** The issuer paying for its own placement through layers. In the Gensol order SEBI prima facie found money reached a promoter entity that subscribed in the September 2022 preferential issue. A stock split announced during a credit problem was put on hold.

### H6 Rights issue and warrant terms
- **Check:** Read the size, price, partly paid structure and promoter share.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2024-08-13 (S021), 2025-11-04 (S022), 2026-01-22 (S023). **Number:** a warrant plan of about 1 crore closed at 60 lakh (S021); a convertible bond drawn to about 10% of the approved amount (S023). **Sources:** 3 (S021, S022, S023).
- **Quote:** "a partly paid rights issue of equity shares for an amount of upto INR25,000 crores" (S022)
- **Quote:** "I think we raised only 10% of what was approved by the Board." (S023)
- **Red flag:** Terms not published. A raise that did not sell in full.

### H7 Oversubscription is not value
- **Check:** Read the anchor book, late large orders and investor concentration. Ask whether the offer shrank when demand was thin.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** October 2026. **Sources:** 2 (S051, S052).
- **Quote:** "oversubscription measures how many people wanted in at a given price, not whether that price was sound or whether the demand was genuine" (S051)
- **Red flag:** Unusual subscription orders shortly before the close of the order book, or undisclosed funding to subscribers.

### H8 Grey market premium is sentiment
- **Check:** Treat GMP as an unofficial quote.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-10-28 (S052), 2026-10 (S051). **Number:** across 294 listings from 2026-01-02 to 2026-10-28, correlation 0.88 and a typical miss of 8.6 points; GMP above 25% never led to a listing below issue (0 of 55); a GMP under 5% still ended below issue price in 20 of 64 cases. **Sources:** 2 (S051, S052).
- **Quote:** "A GMP is an unregulated, unaudited quote from an informal market, not a forecast" (S051)
- **Red flag:** Leaning on a thin premium. The data vendor sells the underlying data through a paid service, so the numbers are unaudited.

### H9 Who gets the shares: the 6(2) route, anchors and SME rules
- **Check:** Know the allocation route and the lock-ins.
- **Class:** REGULATION (as reported, October 2026). **Date:** article of October 2026. **Number:** Regulation 6(2) route needs at least 75% to institutional buyers and limits the retail portion to 10%; half of each anchor allotment locked for 90 days for issues opening on or after 2022-04-01; NSE caps an SME listing-day open at 90% above issue since July 2024. **Sources:** 2 (S051, S052).
- **Quote:** "For issues opening on or after April 1, 2022, half of each anchor allotment has been subject to a 90 days lock-in" (S051)
- **Red flag:** SME issues are where after-listing diversion of proceeds is concentrated (SEBI froze one issue's proceeds in October 2024 and directed refunds on 2024-12-03).

### H10 Retail limits and what retail does after listing
- **Check:** Know the cap and the habit.
- **Class:** REGULATION (undated booklets) and EXAMPLE. **Date:** undated booklets; article of October 2026. **Number:** retail bids up to Rs 2 lakh; UPI limit Rs 2 lakh per transaction (S024, S027); SEBI's 2024 study of 144 mainboard IPOs found individuals sold 50% of allotted shares by value within a week (S051). **Sources:** 3 (S024, S027, S051).
- **Quote:** "SEBI's own 2024 study of 144 mainboard IPOs found that individual investors sold 50% of their allotted shares by value within a week of listing, and 67.6% when listing returns exceeded 20%." (S051)
- **Red flag:** Buying at the opening price: one allottee could sell at about Rs 32,430 on one lot of 138 shares costing Rs 14,904, while an opening-price buyer was down 64.9% by 2026-10-19 (S051).

### H11 Corporate actions that move cash: asset sale, buyback, delisting
- **Check:** After an asset sale, ask where the cash goes. Check buyback size.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS) and REGULATION. **Date:** 2023-24. **Number:** buyback 25% or less of paid-up capital and free reserves; delisting acted on only if public votes in favour are at least twice those against; sale of 20% or more of a material subsidiary's assets in a year needs a special resolution. **Sources:** 1 (S046).
- **Quote:** "If there is no immediate need for the cash, the sale proceeds (after transaction expenses) must be distributed to shareholders." (S046)
- **Red flag:** Cash piling up with no plan.

### H12 A borrowing limit measured by what full use would do
- **Check:** Ask what happens to debt to EBITDA if the limit is fully used.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (IiAS, 2023-24). **Date:** IiAS guidelines 2023-24. **Sources:** 1 (S046).
- **Quote:** "if the company raises debt to the full extent of the limit, its credit protection measures (Debt/EBITDA and/or Debt/Equity) will deteriorate significantly from current levels" (S046)
- **Red flag:** A rolling limit tied to net worth.

### H13 A listed subsidiary's IPO and the holding company
- **Check:** Ask what a subsidiary listing does to the minority holders of the parent.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** call 2024-07-23. **Sources:** 1 (S005).
- **Quote:** "Bajaj Housing Finance, which is a 100% subsidiary, has filed DRHP on 8th of June with SEBI and stock exchanges for potential IPO equity shares" (S005)
- **Red flag:** A parent's value moved into a separately priced listing.

## I. F&O and trading

### I1 The base rate: most individual derivatives traders lose
- **Check:** Start from the loss rate before asking whether a strategy works.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (SEBI studies of 2025-07 and 2026-08). **Date:** SEBI studies of 2025-07 and 2026-08; magazine report 2026-08-20. **Number:** around 91% of individual traders had net losses over FY25 and FY26 combined (S030); nearly 91% in FY25 (S032); 87.7% in FY26 (S048). These are different periods and samples (see L6). **Sources:** 4 (S030, S031, S032, S048).
- **Quote:** "Around 91% of individual traders incurred net losses during FY25-FY26" (S030)
- **Red flag:** A retail holder asked to "play" a stock with futures or options. The booklet states derivatives are not recommended for retail investors (S024).

### I2 Size against capital, and derivatives with no shares behind them
- **Check:** Compare position size with the portfolio, and ask whether there is any cash-equity holding.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-08. **Number:** about 43 lakh traders (35%) had no underlying equity portfolio at the end of FY26; 18.6 lakh (24%) had no cash-market turnover in FY26. **Sources:** 2 (S030, S031).
- **Quote:** "about 43 lakh traders (35%) had no underlying equity portfolio at the end of FY26, indicating participation in derivatives without any cash equity holdings" (S030)
- **Red flag:** Bigger portfolios had fewer losses, but losses were substantially larger among higher-capital traders (S031).

### I3 Costs come before the first rupee of profit
- **Check:** Count transaction costs and tax.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-08. **Number:** costs were 35% of gross losses of loss-makers and 21% of gross profits of profit-makers; about Rs 25,000 crore in FY26 (S048). **Sources:** 2 (S030, S048).
- **Quote:** "Transaction costs accounted for 35% of gross losses of loss-makers, compared with 21% of gross profits of profit-makers." (S030)
- **Red flag:** About 5.3 lakh traders who had gross profits became net loss-makers after costs in FY25.

### I4 Experience is not skill, and repeat losers repeat
- **Check:** Do not read years of trading as an edge.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-08. **Number:** among traders active through FY22 to FY26 only 0.5% were profitable in all five years. **Sources:** 2 (S031, S048).
- **Quote:** "Longer trading experience was not associated with improved outcomes" (S031)
- **Red flag:** About 90% of those who lost two years running lost again in the third.

### I5 Option selling hides tail risk
- **Check:** A high win rate can mask a tail-risk profile.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-08. **Number:** 93% of the FY26 sample were only options buyers and 90% of them lost; major sellers lost less often (44%) but about Rs 51.7 lakh each against about Rs 1.3 lakh for buyers (random sample of about 5,050 traders). **Sources:** 1 (S031).
- **Quote:** "Overall, the data indicates a risk asymmetry in options selling, where relatively high win rates may mask a tail-risk profile that can result in significant financial losses." (S031)
- **Red flag:** The strategy that wins more often can be the one that wipes out capital.

### I6 Short-dated bets and expiry day
- **Check:** Ask how much trading sits on expiry day.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2026-08. **Number:** 70% of turnover on expiry day in S030's measure; 59% of index-options turnover on expiry day and 75% within one day in S048 (see L7). **Sources:** 2 (S030, S048).
- **Quote:** "70% of turnover occurred on the expiry day itself" (S030)
- **Red flag:** Losses in almost every quarter regardless of market direction.

### I7 Rule changes, and why volume drops prove little
- **Check:** Know the dated measures, and do not read a drop in volume as proof a rule worked.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** circular 2024-10-01 (S032, dated 2025-07). **Number:** weekly contracts to one index per exchange per measure effective 2024-11-20; bigger contract sizes 2025-01-02; upfront option premium and no calendar-spread on expiry day 2025-02-10; intraday monitoring of position limits 2025-04-01; securities transaction tax on derivatives raised from 2024-10-01. **Sources:** 4 (S030, S031, S032, S048).
- **Quote:** "any causality can be difficult to establish, given that multiple variables and factors may be behind any variation in volumes." (S032)
- **Red flag:** Using a post-rule fall in volume as evidence of benefit. SEBI's own studies say they are descriptive.

### I8 Do not borrow to invest; keep broker risk small
- **Check:** Keep little cash at the broker, no signed delivery slips, and check the broker is not pledging your shares.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** undated booklets. **Sources:** 2 (S024, S027).
- **Quote:** "Don’t borrow money for investment." (S024)
- **Quote:** "Ensure that Stock Broker doesn’t pledge your securities to raise funds." (S027)
- **Red flag:** A loan against your shares puts the shares at risk if the price falls (S028, a 2010 figure of 75% of demat share value).

### I9 Who wins
- **Check:** Know the counterparty.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE (magazine report of SEBI studies, 2026-08-20). **Date:** magazine report of 2026-08-20. **Number:** algorithmic trading entities made 99% of the profits earned by proprietary traders and foreign investors. **Sources:** 2 (S048, S030).
- **Quote:** "SEBI found that algorithmic trading entities generated 99 per cent of the profits earned by proprietary traders and foreign investors." (S048)
- **Red flag:** A retail trader on the other side of a fast systematic trader.

## J. Tips, finfluencers and fraud

### J1 Advice must come from a registered adviser
- **Check:** Look up the SEBI registration number before paying.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** undated booklet (investment adviser rules last amended 2020-07-03 per the booklet). **Sources:** 4 (S024, S025, S027, S037).
- **Quote:** "It is illegal to act as Investment Adviser without SEBI registration." (S024)
- **Red flag:** Assured returns offered by an adviser. Advice in widely available media is carved out of the definition. S025 gives the same warning (paraphrase of S025).

### J2 Hot tips may come from someone who wants to exit
- **Check:** Ask who gains if you buy.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** undated. **Sources:** 4 (S024, S028, S027, S025).
- **Quote:** "Do not rely on making your investment decisions on hot tips as a person who wants to offload securities which may not be marketable may be indulging in it." (S024)
- **Red flag:** Tips and rumours. S025 says disseminating hot tips is itself illegal (paraphrase of S025).

### J3 Unusually high or steady returns
- **Check:** Treat any promise of high return with little risk as a warning.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** 2010 (S028), undated (S024, S027); S025 (2020) lists the Ponzi pattern: high return with little risk, returns too smooth, no registration, trouble cashing out (paraphrase of S025). **Sources:** 4 (S024, S025, S027, S028).
- **Quote:** "Anything that appears unnaturally high or low will have some ‘catch’ disguised" (S028)
- **Red flag:** Statement errors and difficulty withdrawing (paraphrase of S025). The Sharada and Sahara cases are named only as pointers, with no figures (paraphrase of S025).

### J4 Do not copy winners or follow adverts
- **Check:** Do not copy a win or trust a media report on financial performance.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** undated. **Sources:** 3 (S024, S027, S028).
- **Quote:** "Don't blindly imitate investment decisions of others who may have profited from their investment decisions." (S027)
- **Quote:** "Don’t invest on the basis of market sentiments, rather analyze about the company and its financials and invest smartly." (S024)
- **Red flag:** Impulsive buying or panic selling.

### J5 Undisclosed pay behind a finfluencer
- **Check:** Ask whether the person is paid by the products he praises, and whether he gives advice or claims returns.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (SEBI board memorandum, July 2024, a proposal not an enacted rule; the final notified version is unchecked). **Date:** board memorandum, July 2024. **Number:** 153 consultation responses. **Sources:** 1 (S033).
- **Quote:** "many of them are effectively enticing their followers/investors/prospective investors to purchase financial products, services, or securities in return for undisclosed compensation from producers or platforms." (S033)
- **Red flag:** Some are genuine educators. The test is advice, recommendation or claimed return. The proposal puts the duty on the regulated person, and exempts education that gives no advice.

### J6 Verify the payee before paying
- **Check:** Use SEBI's check of the bank account, UPI ID and QR code of a registered intermediary, and the Verified badge on a broker app.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE (SEBI annual report, 2026-08, data to 2026-03-31). **Date:** a 'SEBI Check' follow-up in December 2025; valid UPI handles effective 2025-10-01. **Sources:** 1 (S037).
- **Quote:** none (read from S037, Box 8.2).
- **Red flag:** Fake trading apps show invented investments while the funds are stolen. Regulated entities now show their registered name and registration number on social media, so an advice account without one is a flag. A claimed track record without independent verification (PaRRVA) is worth questioning. Regulated entities may use the 1600 number series only for service calls to existing customers.

### J7 A short seller's report or a channel check is a lead
- **Check:** Verify against filings before acting on the arithmetic.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2025-09-18 (S055), 2025-11-06 (S018). **Sources:** 2 (S055, S018).
- **Quote:** "it would take Adicorp Enterprises around 900 years to earn enough to pay back the loans even without interest" (S055)
- **Quote:** "I did some channel check on service thing." (S018)
- **Red flag:** Arithmetic that was never established as wrongdoing in the final order.

### J8 Fraud includes omission
- **Check:** Remember that concealment can be fraud even without a gain.
- **Class:** REGULATION (PFUTP, as quoted in the order of 2025-09-18). **Date:** order of 2025-09-18. **Sources:** 2 (S055, S038).
- **Quote:** "whether or not there is any wrongful gain or avoidance of any loss" (S055)
- **Red flag:** Silence on a material related-party loan.

## K. Grievance and remedies (SCORES, exchanges)

### K1 The order of complaint: company, broker or exchange first, then SCORES
- **Check:** Complain in writing to the company, exchange or broker first. Keep every letter.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** SCORES FAQ 2024-10; decks of 2022-09-30 and undated. **Sources:** 5 (S027, S035, S036, S025, S037).
- **Quote:** "Retain copies of your letter and all related correspondence done with trading member." (S036)
- **Red flag:** A trade you did not place, left unanswered. Question it at once and in writing: "Immediately question your TM about any transaction that you do not understand or you did not authorize your trading member." (S036)

### K2 SCORES time limit and reply clock
- **Check:** Use the current limits, one year to complain and 21 days for the entity to reply.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** FAQ of 2024-10. **Number:** one year (3 years in the 2022 deck, see L1); entities reply within 21 calendar days; review request within 15 calendar days of the reply; first review by the designated body within 10 days. **Sources:** 3 (S035, S036, S037).
- **Quote:** "within a period of one year from the date of occurrence of the cause of action" (S035)
- **Quote:** "Entities shall resolve the Complaint and upload the ATR on SCORES within 21 calendar days of receipt of the Complaint." (S035)
- **Red flag:** Using an old time limit. SCORES 2.0 launched 2024-04-01 (S037).

### K3 What SCORES will not take
- **Check:** Know the exclusions before filing.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-10. **Sources:** 2 (S035, S025).
- **Quote:** "Mismanagement of companies, financial performance of the company, Annual General Meeting, etc." (S035)
- **Red flag:** Mismanagement goes to the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, not SEBI. Complaints against insolvent, struck-off or dissemination-board companies are not taken. SCORES facilitates and does not adjudicate: "SEBI SCORES platform is a facilitator of redressal of complainant’s grievance from SEBI regulated entities and does not adjudicate disputes between the complainant and the entity." (S035)

### K4 Tips about violations go to the Market Intelligence route, and silence means nothing
- **Check:** Report price manipulation, insider trading or rumours as market intelligence.
- **Class:** GUIDANCE. **Date:** 2024-10 (S035); portal from May 2024 (S037). **Number:** Market Intelligence inputs rose to 860 from 440 in FY2025-26. **Sources:** 2 (S035, S037).
- **Quote:** "SEBI will neither confirm nor deny the existence of any examinations as the same may be a price sensitive information." (S035)
- **Red flag:** Reading SEBI's silence as a clean result.

### K5 Investor protection fund and broker default
- **Check:** On a broker default, claim within the deadline.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** deck with information as on 2022-09-30. **Number:** NSE up to Rs 25 lakh per investor and BSE a maximum of Rs 15,00,000 per client; claims within 3 months of the public notice; exchange funds stood at Rs 4,169.3 crore at 2026-03-31 (S037, unaudited). **Sources:** 3 (S036, S037, S026).
- **Quote:** "Register claim within 3 months from date of Public Notice." (S036)
- **Red flag:** Using 2022 limits in 2026.

### K6 Arbitration
- **Check:** File within the limit.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2022-09-30. **Number:** within three years of the dispute (NSE); a client with a claim up to Rs 20 lakh is exempt from fees; interim relief capped at Rs 10 lakh a year. **Sources:** 1 (S036).
- **Quote:** "Three years from date of dispute." (S036)
- **Red flag:** Missing the window.

### K7 Unclaimed dividends and the seven-year transfer
- **Check:** Claim before the transfer.
- **Class:** REGULATION. **Date:** 2024-10 (S035), undated (S024). **Number:** seven consecutive years; dividend payable within 30 days of declaration. **Sources:** 2 (S024, S035).
- **Quote:** "remaining unpaid or unclaimed for seven or more years are transferred to Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF)" (S035)
- **Red flag:** Dividends unpaid beyond 30 days.

### K8 What a shareholder can do
- **Check:** Use the vote, ask the board, inspect the books, requisition a meeting.
- **Class:** REGULATION (rights subject to limits). **Date:** undated deck (S027), 2023-24 (S046), 2025-12-11 (S050). **Number:** 10% together to propose removing a director at an EGM; retail voting about 20% against nearly 80% for institutions and promoters; in 2024, 24 of 4,840 resolutions in Nifty 500 companies were rejected. **Sources:** 3 (S027, S046, S050).
- **Quote:** "Right to contribute in key corporate governance decisions through postal ballot/ e-voting." (S027)
- **Red flag:** Retail holders not voting. S050 is an opinion blog and does not prove its link between low retail voting and tunnelling.

### K9 What the regulator can do, and the penalty bands
- **Check:** Know the tools and bands when reading an order.
- **Class:** REGULATION (as quoted in orders of 2026-05). **Date:** orders of 2026-05; 2018-11-02. **Number:** Section 15HA fraud penalty from Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 crore or three times profits, whichever is higher; Section 15A(a) from Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore; Section 15HB Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1 crore. **Sources:** 4 (S043, S044, S054, S040).
- **Quote:** "minimum five lakh rupees upto to twenty-five crore rupees or three times the amount of profits made out of fraudulent practices, whichever is higher" (S043)
- **Red flag:** Interim powers include impounding money and restraining dealing. Disgorgement is sale price less cost and taxes, and philanthropic use of proceeds does not reduce it (S040).

### K10 Detection lag and the stage of an order
- **Check:** Note how long after the event a finding came, and whether it is interim, ex parte, first-instance or final.
- **Class:** EXAMPLE. **Date:** 2018 to 2026. **Number:** the Future Retail order came about 8 years after some of the conduct; the Suzlon FY2014 facts were settled only in 2026. **Sources:** 5 (S043, S044, S038, S054, S051).
- **Quote:** "even the disclosures made during these years are treated as fraudulent after 8 years" (S043)
- **Red flag:** A mild penalty on a company later in insolvency tells a holder the regulator may arrive years after the loss. The fraud standard is higher than a disclosure default.

## L. Where sources disagree

### L1 SCORES: time limit and reply time
- **Sources:** S035 (2024-10) against S036 (2022-09-30) and S025 (2020-11, paraphrase).
- **Quote:** "within a period of one year from the date of occurrence of the cause of action" (S035)
- **Quote:** "Lodge a complaint on SCORES within three (03) years from the date of cause of complaint." (S036)
- **Reading:** The 2022 deck gives three years and a 30-day reply. The 2024 FAQ gives one year and 21 days. The later one is the working figure. Check the current text.

### L2 Materiality for related-party shareholder approval
- **Sources:** S043 (older events) against S046 (from 1 April 2022).
- **Quote:** "a transaction with a related party is considered material if the transactions to be entered into individually or taken together with previous transactions during a financial year, exceeds ten percent of the annual consolidated turnover" (S043)
- **Reading:** S046 gives the lower of Rs 1,000 crore or 10% of turnover from 1 April 2022. Use the current rule.

### L3 Board independence
- **Sources:** S045 (2017 proposal) against S046 (2023-24).
- **Quote:** "every listed entity, irrespective of whether the Chairperson is executive or non-executive, may be required to have at least half its total number of directors as IDs" (S045)
- **Reading:** S046 describes half only where the chair is executive or a promoter-group member. The universal rule was not what S046 reports.

### L4 Whether related parties can vote on their own deals
- **Sources:** S049 (2026-10-27, an advocate's article) against S046 (2023-24).
- **Quote:** "Related parties are generally restricted from voting on resolutions concerning related-party transactions where the relevant rules require their abstention." (S049)
- **Quote:** "Interested/related parties can abstain from voting or vote against such resolutions." (S046)
- **Reading:** The wording in S049 is loose. Trust S046 or the rule text.

### L5 Deposit insurance limit
- **Sources:** S028 (July 2010) against S025 (2020, paraphrase: Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank).
- **Quote:** "Bank deposits are safe investments as all bank deposits are insured upto a maximum of" (S028)
- **Reading:** The 2010 book says Rs 100,000. Treat every number in S028 as history. Check the current limit.

### L6 Derivatives study numbers
- **Sources:** S030, S031, S032, S048.
- **Reading:** Around 91% lost over FY25 and FY26 combined (S030); nearly 91% in FY25 (S032); 87.7% in FY26 (S048). Active traders fell from 106.2 lakh to 87.5 lakh in S030 and from 98.1 lakh to 78.6 lakh in S048, which uses a different sample. The FY25 aggregate net loss was Rs 1,05,603 crore in S032 and about Rs 1.12 lakh crore in S048 after the sample grew. S030 itself prints 87.5 lakh and 87.71 lakh for FY26 traders. Use ranges, not decimals.

### L7 Expiry-day share of trading
- **Sources:** S030 against S048.
- **Quote:** "70% of turnover occurred on the expiry day itself" (S030)
- **Quote:** "SEBI found that 59 per cent of index-options turnover occurred on the expiry day itself, while around 75 per cent took place within one day of expiry." (S048)
- **Reading:** The two measure different things (all derivatives turnover against index options). Do not merge them.

### L8 The Satyam gains order
- **Sources:** S040 (2018-11-02) against S041 (2023-11-30).
- **Reading:** The 2018 order totalled Rs 813.40 crore with 12% interest. The 2023 order, after the appellate tribunal set the earlier orders aside, totalled Rs 624.10 crore on a different intrinsic-value method. Do not quote the 2018 figures as final.

### L9 Listing gain: opening price or closing price
- **Sources:** S051 against S052.
- **Quote:** "As of December 24, 2025, only 54 of the year's 102 issues traded above their offer price." (S051)
- **Reading:** S051 measures listing-day close, S052 the opening price. Neither forecasts what the stock does afterwards.

### L10 The Future Retail order's own arithmetic
- **Sources:** S043 (2026-05-12).
- **Quote:** "Thus, Rs. 1,770 Crore (around 50% of the total advance of Rs. 3559.7 Crore) was utilized by FRL towards repayment of its own debt." (S043)
- **Reading:** The order says Rs 1,347 crore came back directly and Rs 521 crore through another entity. That is Rs 1,868 crore, not Rs 1,770 crore, and the order does not reconcile the two.

### L11 Branch auditors in the NFRA DHFL order
- **Sources:** S047 (2023-04-13), internal.
- **Quote:** "NFRA's investigations revealed that the appointment of none of the 33 branch auditors was approved at the Annual General Meeting (AGM) of DHFL, as required by the Act." (S047)
- **Reading:** The summary paragraph speaks of an engagement partner on two branches. The rest of the order says nine branches, and names a different auditor. This is a drafting inconsistency in the source.

### L12 Disagreement inside one call
- **Sources:** S003, S005, S006, S007, S015, S020.
- **Reading:** One bank gave 14% to 15%, then 13% to 15%, in a single call (S003). A lender's CEO and CFO gave 5% and 3% for the same gap (S005). Revenue growth was 22.04% from the MD and 21.26% from the CFO (S015). An analyst put a quarter at 18% to 15% while the CEO said 16.8% for the earlier year (S020). Read the filed results.

### L13 Date of a source against the date of its call
- **Sources:** S005, S006, S008, S012, S013, S015, S016.
- **Reading:** The source list dates some transcripts by the filing letter and the notes by the call: for example Titan 2025-08-11 against a call on 2025-08-07, Dabur 2025-08-06 against 2025-07-31, Pennar 2025-11-13 against 2025-11-10, Navin 2026-02-13 against 2026-02-09. ### L14 A later master circular on the same default rule
- **Sources:** S053 (2023-07-11) against S038 (cites the 2024-11-11 circular).
- **Reading:** Both give the same 24-hours-from-the-30th-day rule. The newer circular is what S038 cites. Rules may have moved again.

### L15 Allegation against finding in two May 2026 orders
- **Sources:** S043 against S044.
- **Reading:** The quasi-judicial authority (S043) declined to find fraud or diversion on the evidence before it and found disclosure defaults only. The whole time member (S044) found the group deals misleading and overturned a lenient adjudication. The facts differ, but both turn on whether the substance of a group deal is traced to cash. Appeal status is unchecked for both.
