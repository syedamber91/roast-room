# Case book: BIOCON

Facts only, from filings and the exchange feed, built 2026-10-30. It holds no management claim and no management quote; those come out only in the hearing. Money is in Rs million as filed unless a line says otherwise. A derived figure is worked out by code and says so. This is a simulation aid, not advice.

## 1. The company in plain words

- Biocon Limited is a medicines company with its head office in Bengaluru, India. (AR p51)
- It makes medicines and also sells research and manufacturing services to other companies. (AR p13)
- In FY26, the report says Biocon Biologics was integrated into Biocon Limited. (AR p13)
- The products side groups biosimilars, insulins, complex generics and peptides on one platform. (AR p13)
- The services side is built around Syngene International Limited, a listed subsidiary, which means a company Biocon controls. (AR p13)
- Syngene does research, development and manufacturing work for partner life sciences companies around the world. (AR p13)
- The annual report splits the company into three reported segments: Biosimilars, Generics and CRDMO. (AR p6)
- Biocon also works on novel biologics, which are new kinds of large-molecule medicines for cancer and immune disease. (AR p17)
- Biocon has offices, factories and research sites in India, the United States, the UK, Malaysia, Brazil and other countries. (AR p51)
- The report prints total income for FY26 of 172,695 million rupees. (AR p6)

## 2. How it makes money

- The company earns money from three reported segments: Biosimilars, Generics and CRDMO. (AR p6)
- Total income for FY26 was 172,695 million rupees, with growth of 14% on a like-to-like basis. (AR p6)
- Biosimilars is the biggest segment, at 104,312 million rupees. (AR p6)
- The report shows Biosimilars at 60% of revenue, Generics at 18% and CRDMO at 22%. (AR p6)
- The three segment figures added together are more than the total income, and the pages read here do not say why. (AR p6)
- Biosimilars sells copy-style versions of biologic medicines, including insulins, to patients in major markets. (AR p14)
- Biosimilars launched Ustekinumab, Aflibercept and Denosumab versions in FY26. (AR p14)
- Its cancer medicines include biosimilar Trastuzumab and biosimilar Pegfilgrastim. (AR p14)
- The insulin range and biosimilar Adalimumab each passed USD 250 million in global revenues. (AR p14)
- After the Denosumab launch, Biosimilars had 12 commercialized products, meaning products on sale. (AR p14)
- Generics brought in 31,681 million rupees. (AR p6)
- Generics sells complex generics and peptides, with a focus on GLP-1 medicines for diabetes and obesity. (AR p15)
- Generic Liraglutide went on sale in several European countries and received approval in the United States. (AR p15)
- A plant in Cranbury, New Jersey, began commercial supplies of tablets and capsules for the United States market. (AR p15)
- CRDMO is Syngene, and it brought in 37,387 million rupees. (AR p6, p13)
- Syngene is paid for research services and for development and manufacturing services. (AR p16)
- Syngene's customers include Bristol Myers Squibb, a drug company that is a long-standing partner. (AR p16)
- Biocon owns 10.1% of Bicara Therapeutics, which is listed on Nasdaq and develops a head and neck cancer medicine. (AR p17)

## 5. How it is funded

- **Standalone (the company), PDF pages 309 and 310, note 15 Borrowings.** The company must comply with financial covenants under its borrowing facilities, measured on three ratios: net debt to EBITDA, cash flow cover, and net debt to net worth. The terms are defined in the facility agreements, and the note gives no threshold for any ratio. The covenants are tested annually and semi-annually. The note says the company complied with all of them throughout the reporting period and that there are no indications it would have difficulty complying when they are next tested as at 2026-09-30.
- **Consolidated (the group), PDF pages 377 and 378, note on borrowings (k).** The group must comply with covenants under its major borrowing facilities, measured on six ratios: net debt to EBITDA, cash flow cover, net debt to net worth, net debt to adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA to interest cost, and a gearing ratio. Again no thresholds are given, the tests are annual and semi-annual, the group is stated to have complied throughout the reporting period, and the note says there are no indications of difficulty at the next test as at 2026-09-30.
- **Maturity of borrowings, consolidated, PDF page 404, liquidity risk (iii).** Contractual, undiscounted, excluding interest, Rs million. At 2026-03-31: less than 1 year 65,476; 1 to 2 years 13,003; 2 to 5 years 69,767; more than 5 years nil; total 148,246. At 2025-03-31 the total was 177,555 (53,498; 25,506; 98,551; nil). The same page says the group believes its working capital is sufficient and that it maintains lines of credit as stated in notes 14 and 19.

## 7. Glossary

### Plain-words terms used in every case

- Revenue from operations: the money a company earned from selling its products and services, before any cost is taken off.
- Operating profit: what is left of sales after the costs of running the business, before interest, tax and wear on equipment.
- Operating margin: operating profit divided by sales, shown as a percentage. It says how many rupees of each hundred are left after running costs.
- EBITDA: a company's own way of writing operating profit. Companies can define it differently, so it may not match the filed figure.
- Finance costs: the interest a company pays on money it borrowed.
- Profit for the period: what is left after every cost, interest and tax. It includes the share that belongs to other owners of subsidiaries.
- Profit to shareholders: the part of profit that belongs to the owners of the listed company itself.
- Consolidated: the whole group added together, the company and the companies it controls.
- Standalone: the parent company on its own, without its subsidiaries.
- Quarter: three months. A year-ago quarter is the same three months one year earlier, so the two can be compared fairly.
- Fiscal year: the company's accounting year. In India it runs from April to March and is named by the year it ends in.
- Filed: given to the stock exchange as an official document. A filed number is on the record.
- Derived: worked out by code from filed numbers. It is not printed in any filing.
- Guidance: a number or range a company says it expects to reach. Many companies give none.
- Like-to-like: a growth figure the company adjusts so two periods look comparable. The adjustment is the company's own choice and is not a filed number.
- One-off item: a gain or cost that is not expected to repeat. It can make profit look better or worse than normal.
- Segment: one of the main businesses inside a company, reported with its own numbers. Sales between segments are usually left out of the group total, so segment figures can add to more than the total.
- Working capital: the money tied up in running the business day to day, such as stock and unpaid bills from customers.
- Capital work in progress: the cost of things still being built, such as a new factory, that are not yet in use.
- Capex: money spent on buying or building lasting assets such as plants and equipment.
- Borrowings: money the company owes to lenders.
- Maturity: the date a loan has to be paid back. A maturity table shows how much falls due in each period.
- Covenant: a promise made to a lender, for example to keep debt below a limit. Breaking it can let the lender ask for money back early.
- Undrawn line: a loan facility the bank has agreed to but the company has not yet used.
- Promoter: the person or family who founded the company and controls it.
- Pledge: shares put up as security for a loan. If the loan is not repaid, the lender can take the shares.
- Dilution: the promoter's percentage falling because more shares were created, not because the promoter sold.
- Preferential issue: new shares sold to chosen buyers at an agreed price instead of to the public.
- Qualified institutional placement: new shares sold to large institutions such as funds and insurers.
- Related party: a person or company closely linked to the company, such as an owner or a company they also control.
- Related party transaction: a deal between the company and a related party. Shareholders may need to vote on large ones.
- Independent director: a board member who is not part of the company's management or owner group and is meant to speak for all shareholders.
- Statutory auditor: the outside accounting firm that checks the company's accounts and signs a report on them.
- Unmodified opinion: the auditor's report says the accounts give a true and fair view, with no reservation.
- Key audit matter: an area the auditor found most important or difficult in the audit and describes in its report.
- Gate: a serious red flag in this court's rule, such as a regulator finding. A standing gate overrides the score.
- Not in the record: the filings and sources read for this case do not say it. It is a gap, not a finding.
- Listed company: a company whose shares can be bought and sold on a stock exchange. A listed subsidiary is a company the group controls that is listed on its own.
- Integrated: joined into one company's operations. A report may use the word without saying whether the legal form changed, so check what the page says.
- Platform: in a company report, a shared base of factories, labs and sales teams that several products use.
- FDA: the United States government body that decides which medicines may be sold there. Approval means it allowed a medicine onto that market.
- Antibody: a protein the body makes to fight infection. Many biologic medicines are built to work like antibodies.
- Medicine names used in company reports, as general descriptions and not from any report: trastuzumab is used against some breast and stomach cancers; pegfilgrastim helps white blood cells recover after cancer treatment; denosumab is used for weak bones and bone damage from cancer; ustekinumab is used against psoriasis and bowel inflammation; aflibercept is used against some eye diseases; adalimumab is used against joint and other inflammatory diseases; liraglutide is used against diabetes and obesity.

### Terms for this company, from its annual report

- Biosimilar: a medicine made from living cells that is highly similar to an existing biologic medicine. (AR p14)
- Biologic: a large-molecule medicine made from living cells, such as insulin or an antibody. (AR p14)
- Complex generic: a copy of a hard-to-make medicine, such as an injection, sold by the Generics segment. (AR p15)
- Peptide: a short chain of building blocks used as a medicine, such as the GLP-1 medicines. (AR p15)
- GLP-1 therapy: a type of medicine used against diabetes and obesity. (AR p15)
- CRDMO: the Syngene business that does research, development and manufacturing work for other companies. (AR p6, p16)
- Syngene: the listed subsidiary that carries out Biocon's services business. (AR p13)
- Biocon Biologics: the Biocon business unit for biosimilars, which the report says was integrated into Biocon Limited in FY26. (AR p13, p51)
- Drug Substance and Drug Product: the active ingredient itself, and the finished medicine made from it. (AR p14)
- Oral solid dosage: medicines taken by mouth as tablets or capsules. (AR p15)
- Novel biologics: new large-molecule medicines that Biocon invents, instead of copying an existing one. (AR p17)
